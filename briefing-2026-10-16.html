🌟 Top Story: Oil Tanker With 15 Indian Crew Struck as Both Washington and Tehran Claim Control of Hormuz BRENT CRUDE AT $110

A crude oil tanker, the MT ST 3, carrying 15 Indian crew members, was struck by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday; India's ports minister confirmed all crew were safe and uninjured. The incident is part of a wave that has seen at least 10 ships struck in and around the strait since October 11 — the highest concentration of the war. The dispute over attribution reflects a larger standoff: the US and Iran are now both publicly claiming control over who can transit the strait, with Washington framing Iranian claims as illegitimate and Tehran continuing to assert a right to board or strike vessels it deems in violation of its restrictions. Markets registered the risk directly — Brent crude touched $110 a barrel for the first time since May on a weekly gain, prompting the International Energy Agency to move toward releasing the balance of its strategic stock intervention. Separately, President Trump renewed threats against Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, keeping the diplomatic and military tracks intertwined.

🌟 Top Story: Windows Shell Zero-Day KEV Deadline Passes as BeyondTrust Attribution Firms Up CVE-2026-96951 · CISA DEADLINE TODAY

Today is CISA's remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies to patch CVE-2026-96951, the actively exploited Windows Shell security-feature-bypass flaw (CVSS 8.8) that lets attackers evade Mark of the Web protections — one of 1,413 CVEs fixed in Microsoft's record October Patch Tuesday, which included 1,102 Windows fixes, 134 in Office, 61 in developer tools and 36 in SharePoint Server. Security teams working through that backlog now have independent confirmation on yesterday's other major story: SecurityWeek and CyberScoop both corroborate that the BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764, CVSS 9.3) was exploited by a prolific, suspected PRC-nexus espionage group — "Armory Castle" — for roughly seven weeks before disclosure. The combination of a hard compliance deadline landing in the same week as confirmation of a long-dwelling nation-state intrusion vector gives CISOs two distinct priorities that shouldn't be conflated: patch-by-deadline for the Windows flaw, and retroactive compromise-hunting for BeyondTrust regardless of patch status.

If your organization has not confirmed patch status for CVE-2026-96951 today, you have missed the federal remediation deadline — prioritize it immediately even outside federal scope, given confirmed active exploitation.

🌟 Top Story: Google Launches Agentic Checkout with Universal Cart; White House Starts a 120-Day AI-Risk Clock NEW UCP PROTOCOL

Google launched Universal Cart across Search and Gemini in the US, running agentic checkout on a new protocol it calls UCP — letting an AI agent complete a purchase on a user's behalf across participating merchants rather than just recommending a product. The move is one of the clearest signals yet that major platforms are racing to own the "agent completes the transaction" layer of commerce, not just the discovery layer. In a lighter but widely circulated demonstration, Google DeepMind placed four Gemini 4 Argon coding agents into a sandboxed Minecraft survival world and had them defeat the Ender Dragon from a random seed in 27 hours and 14 minutes, writing 2,783 programs and dying 605 times along the way — a concrete, if playful, illustration of long-horizon autonomous problem-solving. On the policy side, the White House's Super Intelligence Task Force formally adopted its charter, starting a 120-day window to deliver a risk report covering cybersecurity, biosecurity and model control, chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton. Separately, Reflection AI open-sourced Beam, a 501-billion-parameter (23B active) mixture-of-experts model for coding, agents, science and math, under the permissive Apache 2.0 license — a notable full-weight release at frontier-adjacent scale.
