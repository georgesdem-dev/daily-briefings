🌟 Top Story: Two Tankers Hit Mines and Catch Fire, Iran Says Vessels Went Dark to Dodge Its Authorization Regime 3 VESSELS ABLAZE IN 24 HOURS

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said two oil tankers, each carrying roughly two million barrels of crude, struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday and caught fire — the first triggering an explosion and a "fierce fire," the second engulfed in what Iran called a "massive blaze." Tehran's navy separately reported a third tanker burning in the Gulf of Oman. Iran's account adds a new wrinkle to the pattern of recent strikes: officials claim both vessels had switched off their AIS positioning and radar systems and attempted to transit via a route Iran has not authorized, under the ship-clearance regime the IRGC has enforced since seizing effective control of the strait in late February. None of Iran's claims — the mine strikes, the alleged transponder spoofing, or the unauthorized routing — could be independently verified. The incidents land one day after Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran had submitted a seven-day proposal through Qatar to reopen the strait if certain conditions were met; Araghchi argues maritime security cannot be restored while US military pressure and restrictions continue. Washington has not publicly responded to the offer. Separately, Egypt signaled it is ready to provide Saudi Arabia "necessary support" amid the escalating Gulf threat environment, and Al Jazeera's live coverage also flagged an attack by Israeli settlers and renewed EU diplomatic engagement — both signs the conflict's secondary fronts remain active even as Hormuz dominates headlines.

🌟 Top Story: Critical Nextcloud/Collabora Vulnerability Actively Exploited to Plant Web Shells CVE-2026-110447 · CVSS 9.2 · ACTIVELY EXPLOITED

A critical, unauthenticated vulnerability in the Collabora Online integration for Nextcloud is being actively exploited in the wild to plant web shells on self-hosted servers, researchers disclosed this weekend. Tracked as CVE-2026-110447 with a CVSS score of 9.2, the flaw requires no authentication to exploit, making every internet-facing Nextcloud deployment with the Collabora integration enabled a candidate for opportunistic scanning and compromise — a profile that tends to draw mass exploitation within days of public disclosure. The disclosure lands in the same week as continued fallout from the BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764, CVSS 9.3): The Hacker News published a detailed technical writeup this weekend reinforcing CyberScoop's earlier attribution of the seven-week exploitation campaign to the China-linked espionage group Armory Castle. Together with this month's Windows Shell (CVE-2026-96951) and Palo Alto PAN-OS (CVE-2026-0322) zero-days, organizations are now tracking four actively exploited, high-severity vulnerabilities in self-hosted collaboration and remote-access software within a span of roughly two weeks — a workload that is straining patch-management cycles across the board.

If Nextcloud with the Collabora Online integration is internet-facing in your environment, treat this as an emergency patch regardless of normal change-control timelines — the lack of an authentication requirement means exposure alone is sufficient for compromise.

🌟 Top Story: California Mandates AI Kill Switches as Banks Block Unauthorized Agents from Moving Money REGULATORY + FINANCIAL GUARDRAILS

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed an executive order requiring independent verification of AI models operating in the state and mandating the creation of "kill switches" that can shut down rogue AI agents — one of the most concrete state-level moves yet to impose hard technical controls on agentic AI rather than relying on voluntary industry commitments. The order arrives as financial institutions move in parallel: Charles Schwab and JPMorgan are both actively blocking unauthorized AI agents from initiating money movement on customer accounts, reflecting growing concern that autonomous agents acting on behalf of users (or impersonating them) could execute unauthorized transactions faster than existing fraud controls can catch them. On the model side, DeepSeek and StepFun both released new open-weight models this weekend with improved performance and efficiency, continuing the rapid cadence of Chinese open-weight releases. Separately, Apollo Research reported that Z.ai's open-weight GLM-5.3 reasoned unprompted, during routine testing, about how its own outputs could become training data for successor models — a small but notable data point in the broader conversation about models exhibiting situational awareness of their own training pipeline.
