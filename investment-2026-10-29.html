🏛️ Fed Holds at 3.75%–4.00% — Warsh Signals Patience

The FOMC voted 8-2 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.75%–4.00% at yesterday's October 27-28 meeting. Dissenters Cook and Hammack voted for a 25bps hike, citing services inflation still running above 3%. Chair Kevin Warsh, in his post-meeting statement, described the economy as "resilient but not impervious" — language markets read as hawkish but not imminently so. The 10Y Treasury yield edged up to 5.01% in reaction, a level that has historically compressed equity valuations at the margin.

Warsh is also floating plans to reduce annual rate-setting meetings from eight to six, replacing two with "strategy sessions" — a structural change markets are watching closely. The next rate decision is December 15, 2026. Consensus now prices zero cuts in 2026 and one 25bps cut in Q1 2027, contingent on oil normalization post-Iran ceasefire.

🪖 Iran War — Day 244: Strait of Hormuz Partially Reopened

The Strait of Hormuz is now transiting approximately 18 vessels per day — up from the April low of 8 but still well below the pre-war norm of ~40. Oil prices rose +2.3% to $102.13 today, pushed higher by reports that Iran test-fired new Shahed-class drones near Kharg Island, undermining ceasefire negotiations. The US 5th Fleet has maintained a carrier strike group in the Gulf since September, adding a structural supply premium to WTI that market consensus estimates at $15–20/bbl.

For portfolio positioning: energy stocks (XOM, CVX) continue to generate extraordinary free cash flow at $100+/bbl. The Iran risk premium makes oil-related equity a natural hedge against geopolitical escalation — but be aware that any credible ceasefire deal could remove that $15-20 premium quickly, as seen on the May 5 one-day drop of -4.2%.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital — October Memo: "Selectivity Is Everything"

"The GOOGL Miss Is a Warning Shot — AI Capex Is Not a Guarantee"

Howard Marks' latest memo, circulated to clients this week, opens with a pointed observation: "When a company with $127B in quarterly revenue can still disappoint markets, you know we are in an environment where expectations have run ahead of reality." Marks argues that 2026's AI investment frenzy has created a binary outcome distribution — companies that monetize AI efficiently (MSFT, Amazon) will be rewarded disproportionately; those that spend ahead of monetization (GOOGL, Meta) will be punished. His prescription: increase exposure to dividend-growth equities and investment-grade credit (5%+ yields on IG bonds now provide genuine competition to equities). For the moderate-risk investor: BRK-B, JNJ, and JPM embody the Marks playbook — quality at reasonable valuations with income cushion.

Kevin Warsh / Federal Reserve — Post-FOMC Remarks

"The Economy Does Not Yet Warrant Accommodation" — Rates Steady at 3.75%–4.00%

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's post-FOMC press conference was characteristically disciplined. He declined to signal any rate cut timeline, noting that "services inflation remains above our symmetric 2% target and the labor market, while moderating, continues to generate above-trend wage growth." Two dissenters voted for a hike — unusual hawkish pressure. Warsh's "fundamentalist" reputation on price stability, combined with the structural rate reform (fewer meetings planned), suggests the Fed is not going to rescue equity valuations. For portfolio construction: the 10Y at 5.01% is genuinely competitive with the S&P earnings yield of ~3.5%, meaning only the highest-quality earnings growth names deserve premium multiples — reinforcing the MSFT vs. GOOGL divergence thesis.
