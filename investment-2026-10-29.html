🏛️ Fed Holds Steady at 3.75%-4.00%, but the Vote and Tone Were Hawkish

The FOMC voted 10-2 on Wednesday to hold its benchmark rate at 3.75%-4.00%, but the split vote was notable: Fed Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. The post-meeting statement said "inflation remains elevated" and the Committee upgraded its description of economic activity to "strong," language that reads more hawkish than the market's prior assumption of a multi-meeting hold. Fed-funds futures now price roughly one more 25bp hike before year-end, with additional tightening possibly extending into 2027 — a reversal from the rate-cut hopes that were in play earlier this year.

Equities nonetheless extended their recovery for a third straight session: the S&P 500 (+0.28%), Dow (+0.19%) and Nasdaq (+0.27%) all closed higher Wednesday, and the VIX fell another 3.5% to 16.91 — its calmest reading in over a week — as investors read the hold itself as the more important signal than the hawkish dissents.

📉 Alphabet's Ugly Quarter: EPS Miss, Negative Free Cash Flow, Debt Up ~5x

Alphabet reported Q3 results after Wednesday's close that fell well short of expectations: adjusted EPS missed the Street's ~$3.01-3.02 consensus*, and revenue of $127.58B came in just under the ~$128.2B forecast. Far more alarming to investors was free cash flow of -$9.07B against an expected -$2.70B, and long-term debt that ballooned to $125.1B from $21.6B a year ago — a roughly 479% increase, almost entirely tied to AI infrastructure buildout. Google Cloud grew a blistering 103%, but that strength was overshadowed by the capex and cash-burn figures. GOOGL closed the regular session at $324.89 and then fell as much as ~10% in after-hours and premarket trading toward the high-$280s to low-$300s*, management having left 2026 capex guidance unchanged while flagging a significant further increase for 2027.

*Sources gave conflicting EPS figures ($2.12 vs. $2.67 adjusted) and conflicting magnitude for the after-hours decline (8% to 11%); the figures above reflect the more frequently-cited range. A single premarket data point showed GOOGL only modestly lower (-1.1% at $324.25), which looks stale relative to the earnings reaction and is treated as an outlier here.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

"Classic Bubble" Nearing Its Bursting Point

Dalio reiterated at the Forbes Global CEO Conference in Singapore that AI investment has the hallmarks of a classic bubble nearing a bursting point, driven by rising rates and heavy debt-funded capex. Alphabet's results — a near-5x jump in debt and a swing to deeply negative free cash flow in a single quarter — read as exactly the kind of data point Dalio has been warning about, even though it came from fundamentals rather than a market-wide panic.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Still Not Calling It a Bubble — But the Argument Gets Harder to Make

Marks told Bloomberg (Oct 18) that he doesn't see a "classic bubble" today, arguing that widespread awareness of bubble risk is itself evidence against one — true manias happen when risk is ignored, not debated. He points to the Nasdaq 100's double-digit pullback this year as proof the market isn't oblivious. Alphabet's reaction today — a sharp, fundamentals-driven repricing rather than blind momentum-chasing — arguably supports Marks' framing, even as it validates Dalio's underlying concern about capex sustainability.

Goldman Sachs & JPMorgan — Fed Reaction

Hawkish Hold Reframes the Rate Picture Into Year-End

The Fed's 10-2 split vote and "inflation remains elevated" language mark a real shift from the market's earlier assumption of a benign, multi-meeting hold. With futures now pricing a further hike before year-end, financials like JPM may see a modest NIM tailwind, while rate-sensitive growth names (including AMZN and GOOGL) face a slightly higher discount-rate headwind just as they report earnings.
