📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

📉 "Broken Market": 75% of S&P 500 Stocks Had a Lousy September

Despite the S&P 500 finishing the month down a modest 0.7%, the breadth underneath was ugly: three-quarters of S&P 500 constituents posted a losing September. Technology (+5% MTD) was the only sector in positive territory, masking declines of -5% to -7% across Staples, Utilities, Consumer Discretionary, Financials, Materials, and REITs. This is a market being carried by a narrow slice of mega-cap AI winners.

📈 Inflation Cooled, But Yields Kept Climbing Anyway

Wednesday's PCE inflation print came in better than feared — 0.3% monthly, 3.4% year-over-year versus a 3.7% forecast — yet the 10-year Treasury yield still pushed to 5.29%, a fresh 24-year high, on persistent worries about the federal debt load and a still-solid economy. That disconnect between cooling inflation and rising yields is the market's central tension heading into October.

🏛️ Shutdown Risk Removed — CR Funds Government Through Dec 11

Congress passed and President Trump signed a continuing resolution on September 2 extending federal appropriations through December 11, with broad bipartisan support (Senate 90-6, House 370-48). That removes a government-shutdown risk that briefly worried markets heading into the October 1 fiscal year start, clearing the runway for Friday's payrolls report and Q3 earnings season to take center stage.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
CNBC / Street Consensus — September Wrap

A "Broken Market": Narrow Leadership Masks Broad Weakness

Despite the S&P 500's modest 0.7% September decline, 75% of its constituents finished the month lower — a stark reminder that headline index moves are being driven by a small group of AI/tech mega-caps. This narrow-breadth dynamic raises the stakes for Q3 earnings season: any stumble from the AI leaders could expose just how thin this rally's foundation really is.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Still Waiting — Cash Pile Unmoved by September's Dip

Buffett's continued reluctance to deploy Berkshire's roughly $397B cash reserve, even after a rough September for most of the market, signals he still views current valuations — especially among the narrow group of winners — as unattractive relative to his bar for deployment.

Goldman Sachs Asset Management — Market Pulse

Watching October Earnings as the Breadth Test

With Technology the only sector positive in September, Goldman's house view is watching Q3 earnings season closely as the next catalyst to either validate the AI-led rally's staying power or trigger a broader rotation into the beaten-down cyclical and rate-sensitive sectors that lagged badly last month.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

24-Year-High Yields Are the Debt Cycle Playing Out in Real Time

Dalio's long-running thesis about debt-cycle stress looks increasingly literal as the 10Y yield touches levels not seen since 2002, even with inflation cooling. His framing: when yields rise on debt-load concerns rather than inflation fears, it's a structural warning sign, not a cyclical one.
