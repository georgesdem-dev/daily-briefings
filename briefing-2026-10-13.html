🌟 Top Story: Shipping Attacks Intensify Further as Hormuz Traffic Collapses 12 TANKERS HIT IN ONE WEEK

The surge in attacks on Strait of Hormuz shipping accelerated again this week, with at least 12 tankers carrying oil, LNG and LPG struck in the week spanning late September into early October — surpassing even the 11-tanker week reported two days ago as the new wartime high. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency confirmed six separate vessel strikes in a single day this past Sunday alone, including a drone attack near Oman's Sohar anchorage, underscoring how the campaign has widened beyond the strait itself into nearby anchorages. On Monday, UKMTO reported yet another tanker struck by an unidentified projectile while exiting Hormuz, with all crew reported safe. Iran's IRGC Navy has repeated its warning that any tanker ignoring the strait's "security and rules" will face the same fate as vessels already hit, while the US Navy-led Joint Maritime Information Centre assessed the pattern as showing Iran's "intent to assert presence along key transit lanes and maintain pressure on transiting vessels." The cumulative effect is now visible in the data: Hormuz traffic has reportedly fallen to its lowest level in two months, the clearest sign yet that the blockade-and-counter-blockade standoff is materially disrupting one of the world's most important oil chokepoints, even as the diplomatic track remains completely frozen.

🌟 Top Story: CISA Expands Alert as Citrix NetScaler Zero-Days See Global Active Exploitation CVE-2026-88771 / 88772 · CVSS v4 9.5

Threat actors are actively exploiting two critical zero-day vulnerabilities in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway appliances, tracked as CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, each carrying a CVSS v4 score of 9.5 and allowing full remote code execution. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after confirming global exploitation activity from its own telemetry and partner intelligence. The agency has since widened the alert twice — first to add CVE-2026-107406, then to stress prompt patching across the entire CVE-2026-88771 through 88779 range — making this the sixth distinct exploited NetScaler flaw disclosed in 2026 alone. The NetScaler exploitation is not happening in isolation: Ivanti disclosed its own actively exploited Connect Secure zero-day (CVE-2026-104935, CVSS 8.6, reported by Volexity, no authentication or user interaction required) affecting ICS versions 22.8R2.4 and earlier, and Fortinet's FortiMail path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-104286, CVSS 9.8) was already being exploited before a fix shipped on October 1. Taken together, this is now a half-dozen simultaneously-exploited edge-appliance zero-days across four vendors (Citrix, Ivanti, Fortinet, and last week's SonicWall/Palo Alto bugs) — the defining cybersecurity pattern of this autumn.

If you run NetScaler ADC/Gateway, Ivanti Connect Secure or FortiMail anywhere internet-facing, treat all three as emergency-patch items today — all are confirmed under active exploitation right now, not theoretical risk.
