Iranian attacks in the Strait of Hormuz have climbed to their highest level since the war began, with at least 22 attacks reported in November alone, CNN and ABC News reported over the weekend. The standoff unfolds against the backdrop of last week's Senate vote blocking a war-powers resolution 51-48 and Trump's comment to Axios that he won't decide on next steps until the new year.

🌟 Top Story: Critical ScreenConnect Flaw Lets Attackers Hijack MSP Technicians' PCs in Nine Seconds CVSS 9.9 · 688K MALICIOUS REQUESTS

Huntress and SecurityWeek detailed a monthlong campaign (Oct 20–Nov 16) exploiting CVE-2026-84869, a critical ScreenConnect client flaw (CVSS 9.9) that lets a connecting "guest" session push and execute a malicious file on a technician's host the instant they join a support session. Attackers blindly sprayed roughly 688,000 "run file" requests for files named update.bat or update_<hex>.bat from 29 source IPs against 42 MSP ScreenConnect servers, waiting for any technician to connect. On November 12, Huntress caught a technician workstation triggering one of these batch files, which phoned a Telegram bot, downloaded an MSI, and launched a rogue ScreenConnect client — fully compromising the machine in roughly nine seconds. ConnectWise has shipped a fix in host client version 26.6.5+, but because the exploit lives on the technician's side rather than the server, MSPs that only patched their servers remain exposed unless every technician's local client is force-updated too. Separately, Google's Chrome zero-day CVE-2026-125350 — an XML use-after-free flaw and the eighth Chrome zero-day patched this year — sits under a CISA KEV patch deadline of November 24; Microsoft has shipped a matching Edge fix. The three-day federal remediation deadline CISA set on November 20 for the China-linked Armory Castle VMware vCenter zero-day (CVE-2026-123890) also lapses this week.
