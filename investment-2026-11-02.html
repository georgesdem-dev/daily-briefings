📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🕊️ Trump Signals More Time for Iran Diplomacy — Oil Reverses Hard

President Trump said he'd prefer to give diplomacy with Iran more time before considering further military strikes, with the decision reportedly deferred until after Tuesday's midterm elections. Iranian and Qatari officials held a third consecutive day of talks aimed at ending the broader US-Israel-Iran conflict, and the absence of any large-scale clash over the weekend eased near-term supply-disruption fears. WTI crude, which spiked to a five-month high near $104 on Friday's Hormuz tanker attack, gave back roughly 1.5-3% to trade in the $102-103 range — a sharp reversal of the geopolitical risk premium that had been building all month.

The de-escalation headlines lifted the broader market: the S&P 500 rose about 0.5% to roughly 7,618, clawing back a slice of Friday's trillion-dollar selloff. Gold also pulled back from its elevated Friday levels as safe-haven demand eased alongside the lower war-risk pricing.

📱 Apple Reports Fiscal Q4 Tonight — Tech Still the Laggard Into the Print

Technology was the only sector group to decline today (-3.85%), still digesting Friday's Intel guidance shock and trading cautiously into Apple's fiscal Q4 results, due after the close with a 5pm ET call. Analysts expect EPS of roughly $1.98 (+7% y/y) on revenue of about $113.3B (+11% y/y), though the quarter predates the September 18 launch of the iPhone 18 Pro line, so it won't say much about current demand. Communication Services remains the single weakest sector over the past month, with GOOGL down roughly 17% and META down roughly 15% over four weeks — Alphabet's post-earnings slide is now part of a broader group-wide derate, not an isolated story.

Energy and Healthcare continue to lead both today's tape and the past month, even as oil itself pulled back — a sign the sector rotation out of mega-cap tech and into value/defensives has more to do with valuation reset than with the commodity move itself.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Treasury Slump and AI-Bubble Risk Unchanged by Today's Relief Rally

Dalio's standing warning — that expanding bond supply is pushing yields higher in a self-reinforcing spiral, and that AI capital spending looks like a bubble approaching its bursting point — isn't resolved by one good day for stocks. Today's rally was driven by geopolitical relief, not any change in the capex/cash-flow dynamics behind Friday's Intel shock or the ongoing GOOGL/Communication Services derate. Worth remembering before reading too much into a single bounce.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Cold, Hard Reality Check" Warning Still Stands

Buffett's message from earlier this month — that elevated valuations and crowded positioning leave many investors exposed to a sharp reset — looks more relevant, not less, after a month where Intel, GOOGL and META all posted double-digit drawdowns in a matter of weeks. Diversification away from the most crowded AI-capex and mega-cap tech names remains the better-tested approach into a headline-driven week.

Goldman Sachs & JPMorgan — Rotation Playbook

Nine of Eleven Sectors Advancing Validates the Rotation Thesis

Today's breadth — nine of eleven S&P sector ETFs higher even with the index up only modestly — is exactly the broadening-out pattern GS and JPM have been calling for since the AI-capex names got crowded. This portfolio's weighting toward UNH/JNJ/HII/OXY sits squarely inside that rotation, with Technology and Communication Services remaining the two areas analysts are most cautious on into year-end.
