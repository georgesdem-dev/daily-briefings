💥 The $5 Trillion AI Rout — What Happened Last Week

Intel (INTC) Worst Day Since 1974: Intel crashed -28.58% on Friday October 30 on volume of 455 million shares (vs a normal 80–150M), its single worst trading day in 52 years. The catalyst: an earnings print that confirmed "AI risks showing up in Big Tech earnings" — Intel is losing share to TSMC-fabbed chips from AMD and Nvidia while its own data center CPU business stalls. Analyst 12-month consensus target sat at $79 before Friday; at $65.76 close it is now BELOW the consensus, but the question is whether targets get revised downward en masse. Overnight Monday: INTC +1.3% pre-market.

Reddit (RDDT) -29.22%: Reddit crashed to 52-week lows at $91.73, down nearly a third in a single session on earnings-related disappointment. The stock had been a darling of the AI data-licensing thesis — expectations were too high.

Broader AI Infrastructure Carnage: SOXL (3x Semiconductor ETF) collapsed -24.75%. Kioxia (Japan, DRAM/NAND) fell -15.78% citing a cautious memory outlook, triggering a cascade: Micron (MU) -5.30%, TSMC -5.96%, SK Hynix -7.95%, NVIDIA -4.22%. Twilio fell -15.24% on "messaging deceleration and AI adoption" concerns. Roblox tumbled -18.05% on weak Q3 + holiday guidance. The message: AI excitement has run ahead of actual earnings power across many names.

Today's Bounce: Futures are recovering — S&P +0.45%, Nasdaq +0.84%, NVIDIA +1.43%, Intel +1.29% overnight. The market is attempting to separate the AI platform winners (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN) from hardware casualties. This week's earnings calendar (AppLovin Nov 4, Qualcomm, Arm) will determine whether the bounce holds.

📉 Macro Backdrop: Yields Retreat, Fed in Focus, Oil Pulls Back

10-Year Treasury Yield: The 10-year peaked at 5.329% on October 14 — a level that historically triggers equity re-pricing. As of October 30, it retreated to 4.929% (-6 bps on the day), and Monday pre-market suggests further decline. The yield range of 4.72%–5.34% over the past 2 months shows elevated uncertainty, but the trend from the October high is constructive for equities. The Fed is expected to hold rates steady at its next meeting, with one cut now priced for Q1–Q2 2027 contingent on inflation data.

WTI Crude Oil at $100/bbl: Oil fell -3.31% Monday to $100.67/barrel, continuing a reversal from recent highs. The $100 level is a key psychological threshold. At $65–70/bbl breakeven for major producers, $100 oil still represents extraordinary free cash flow for XOM, CVX, and energy majors.

Binance Hack — $6.3 Billion: The world's largest cryptocurrency exchange suffered a record $6.3 billion hack, leading to withdrawal suspensions. Bitcoin is trading at $78,070 — down 39% from its peak. Meme coins have collapsed -99%. The hack is likely to trigger a regulatory response and could dampen crypto sentiment for weeks. This is not a direct equity market risk, but it signals system-level fragility in digital assets.

Gold at $3,916: Gold has pulled back from its $4,074 October high to the $3,900 range, but remains elevated — a persistent signal of institutional inflation anxiety and uncertainty. The modest +0.43% bounce today alongside equity recovery suggests gold is being held as insurance, not panic.

Dan Niles — Independent Tech Investor

"AI Risks Are Now Showing Up in Big Tech Earnings"

Dan Niles, one of the most closely-followed independent tech investors, issued his clearest AI caution to date following Intel's collapse: "AI risks are showing up in Big Tech earnings." His thesis: the market has been pricing in AI-driven earnings acceleration across the entire tech sector, but the reality is that AI is disrupting many existing tech business models (legacy chips, enterprise software, payments, SaaS) faster than it is benefiting them. The winners are the AI platform owners (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN) and AI-native companies; the losers are incumbents whose revenues are being automated away. Niles noted that Intel, Twilio, PayPal, and others are experiencing AI-driven margin compression. His posture for Q4 2026: reduce hardware and legacy software exposure; accumulate AI platform leaders on dips.

Goldman Sachs Macro Desk

S&P 500 Year-End Target: 7,800 — "Buy the AI Platform Leaders; Reduce AI Hardware Exposure"

Goldman Sachs maintains a year-end 2026 S&P 500 target of 7,800, implying a ~2.9% recovery from current levels. Their updated sector view following the October rout: (1) Overweight Healthcare, Energy, Utilities — the three sectors that have proven their resilience; (2) Selective Tech — only the AI platform owners with clear revenue monetisation paths (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN); (3) Underweight Semiconductors — the Intel collapse is a leading indicator that AI chip demand is already being priced into earnings in ways that don't match reality. On the macro side: Goldman sees one Fed rate cut in Q2 2027, 10yr Treasury declining to 4.5% by year-end 2027, and corporate credit spreads remaining contained.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"The AI Rout Was Predictable — Markets Still Not Offering True Value"

Howard Marks' framework — that risk is highest when it feels lowest — applies directly to what happened in October. VIX had drifted below 16 in mid-October even as 10-year yields spiked to 5.33%. Marks would argue the Intel crash, Reddit collapse, and Twilio selloff are natural consequences of paying 100x+ earnings for AI-adjacent names when rates are 5%. His prescription for November: increase allocation to income-producing assets (dividend stocks, short-duration corporate bonds), maintain significant cash reserves for further dislocations, and resist the urge to chase the Monday morning bounce. For this brief's purposes, his framework validates the defensive tilt: BRK-B, JNJ, XOM, NEE, JPM — quality, income, reasonable valuations.

JPMorgan Equity Strategy

"Defensive Rotation Is Structural, Not Tactical — Add Healthcare and Utilities"

JPMorgan's equity desk argues the rotation into defensives is structural rather than temporary. With the 10-year Treasury at 4.93% and potentially heading to 4.5%–4.7% by mid-2027, rate-sensitive defensive sectors (Healthcare, Utilities) re-rate positively on each tick lower in yields. Healthcare in particular offers a rare combination: defensive earnings certainty, a pipeline of FDA approvals in oncology and GLP-1 drugs, and valuations that are still reasonable (sector forward P/E ~18x vs S&P's 21x). JPMorgan's top defensive long: JNJ, UNH (contrarian after its YTD decline), NEE. They also flag BRK-B as the ideal single-stock expression of the "quality + cash optionality" thesis ahead of what they see as a potential M&A wave in 2027.
