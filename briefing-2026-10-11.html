🌟 Top Story: Ransomware Affiliates Forge VPN Cookies to Bypass Palo Alto GlobalProtect Authentication CVE-2026-0257 · CVSS 7.8

ReliaQuest's October 11 threat intelligence update confirms that multiple ransomware groups are actively exploiting CVE-2026-0257, an authentication-bypass flaw in Palo Alto Networks' PAN-OS GlobalProtect portal and gateway, to open fully authorized VPN sessions without valid credentials and move laterally inside victim networks. The bug lets an unauthenticated attacker forge an authentication-override cookie rather than guess or steal a password — Arctic Wolf Labs first traced a series of June intrusions using this technique through to Qilin ransomware deployment, and ReliaQuest now says a newer Qilin-linked variant called "Settra" is using the same flaw. Palo Alto patched the vulnerability back on May 13, meaning this is a five-month-old fix still generating fresh ransomware intrusions — a pattern consistent with this autumn's run of edge-appliance bugs (NetScaler, FortiMail, SonicWall, Check Point) where patch availability has not stopped sustained exploitation. Responders who find evidence of this cookie-forgery technique are advised to rotate all domain administrator, service account and KRBTGT credentials immediately and terminate every active GlobalProtect session, since a forged cookie can't be distinguished from a legitimate one without that reset.

If GlobalProtect is internet-facing anywhere in your environment, confirm the May 13 patch for CVE-2026-0257 is applied and search VPN logs for anomalous session-establishment patterns since then — five months of exposure is enough window for a quiet compromise to be sitting undetected.

🌟 Top Story: Microsoft Ships Decision-1, a Qwen-Based Model That Scores Choices Instead of Generating Text 4.5x FASTER THAN RUNNER-UP

Microsoft released Microsoft-Decision-1, a decision-scoring model built by post-training Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.5-9B, now available in Microsoft Foundry and through OpenRouter. Rather than generating a text response, the model takes a fixed set of options — yes/no, multiple choice, a rating scale, or a rubric for grading another AI's output or an agent's action — and returns a calibrated probability for each option in a single pass, aimed at routing, classification, prioritization, verification and agent workflow control. Microsoft reports the highest accuracy across a 36-benchmark comparison spanning nearly 150,000 held-out questions, and says it runs 4.5 times faster than the next-best model and 35 times faster than its own GPT-6 Sol, at $0.042 per million input tokens with no charge for output tokens. Microsoft says it will rebase the approach on other models, including its own MAI models and OpenAI's, beyond the initial Qwen foundation. The release lands just two days after a16z led an $870 million round into startup TypeSafe AI's "System One" model Jev at a $7.5 billion valuation — also a decision-returning, non-chatbot architecture — making this the second major lab or startup in under a week to bet that a large share of enterprise AI workloads are better served by a fast, narrow decision model than by routing every task through a general-purpose chatbot.
