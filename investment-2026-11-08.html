📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

💥 Thursday's Crash: The AI Trade Melts Down, Nasdaq Enters Correction

Wednesday's post-midterm euphoria didn't survive 24 hours. On Thursday Nov 5, the S&P 500 shed -4.36% to 7,353.73 — its worst day since April 2025 — while the Nasdaq cratered -5.52% to 24,315.00, falling into correction territory (more than 10% below its October record). The Dow sank -1,833 points (-3.44%) to 51,523.06. Chip and AI-infrastructure names were hit hardest: Nvidia fell -9.26% to $181.60 (erasing its entire 2026 gain), AMD plunged -17.26% to $404.40, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped -12.25%. The S&P 500 lost more than $2 trillion in market value, with 419 decliners against just 84 advancers — a genuinely broad-based rout, not a narrow tech story.

The trigger was a reassessment of the AI-capex trade: investors who'd cheered the midterm result's "certainty" on Wednesday woke up Thursday to recalculate the odds of tighter AI/tech regulation under a Democratic Congress, compounding existing valuation anxiety after a year of parabolic chip-stock gains.

🩹 Friday's Partial Rebound — But the Dow Kept Bleeding

Friday Nov 6 saw a tentative stabilization: the S&P 500 recovered +0.31% to 7,376.86 and the Nasdaq bounced +1.09% to 24,579.42 as bargain hunters stepped into the most beaten-down chip names (AMD +11.04%, Micron +5.12%, Marvell +5.73%, Nvidia +2.78%). The Dow, however, extended its slide another -0.46% to 51,285.73, dragged by non-tech cyclicals and the still-struggling energy complex. VIX eased -9% off its spike to 25.64 — still well above the pre-selloff ~16 level from earlier in the week, signaling the market hasn't fully exhaled.

Net effect for the week: despite Friday's bounce, the Nasdaq is still down sharply from Wednesday's rally high, and the "AI bubble" conversation has moved from a background risk to the dominant market narrative heading into next week.

🛢️ Oil and Gold Both Soft — A Mixed Macro Backdrop

WTI crude firmed slightly Friday (+0.66% to ~$91.10) but remains near its lowest levels of the year after the post-midterm plunge. Gold pulled back -1.31% to $4,060.70 after its recent run — a sign that even the safe-haven trade cooled slightly as the immediate equity panic eased. The 10-year Treasury yield held in a 4.86-4.88% range, not moving dramatically despite the equity volatility, suggesting bond markets see this more as a valuation unwind in a narrow slice of mega-cap tech than a broader growth scare.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

"Whether or Not You Think AI Is in a Bubble, You Have to Recognize the Concentration Risk"

Dalio sharpened his warning this week, shifting focus from bubble-timing to concentration: he flagged that hyperscaler bond issuance topped $220 billion in the first half of 2026 alone — more than double all of 2025 — as companies lever up to fund AI capex. He compared today's euphoria to levels preceding the dot-com and 1929 crashes, and explained the mechanics of how bubbles typically pop: wealthy holders of paper gains eventually need to convert to cash, forcing sales that trigger the "pricking." This week's chip-stock crash is close to a live case study of his thesis.

Michael Burry

Trimmed 25% of His Chip-Stock Puts — But Still "Very Bearish"

Burry sold a quarter of his bearish options position on the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) following this week's sharp selloff, taking profits on a bet that's clearly paid off. Analysts read this as risk-trimming rather than a change of thesis — Burry has spent recent weeks comparing the AI capex cycle to the dot-com bubble and accusing hyperscalers of using accounting maneuvers to inflate reported profits. His continued large short positions against Nvidia and Palantir suggest he expects more downside even after this week's move.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Building a Casino Next to a Church" — Yet Berkshire Holds a $31B Alphabet Stake

Buffett's sharpest recent comment compared the current market to a casino built beside a church, warning some valuations have become "very ridiculous" and invoking the Nasdaq's near-80% dot-com-era plunge as a cautionary precedent. Notably, though, he's framed this as a broad market-wide speculative mood rather than an AI-specific phenomenon — and he personally championed Berkshire's ~$31B Alphabet stake, calling the AI infrastructure race "real money" being spent by companies he still expects to be relative long-term winners. The nuance matters: Buffett is cautious on valuations broadly, not making a pure anti-AI bet.
