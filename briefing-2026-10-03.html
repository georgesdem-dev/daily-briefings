🌟 Top Story: FortiMail Zero-Day Actively Exploited — No Patch Yet Available CVE-2026-104286 · CVSS 9.8

Fortinet and CISA are sounding the alarm on CVE-2026-104286, a critical path-traversal and NULL-byte-injection flaw in FortiMail that lets an unauthenticated attacker who reaches the management port write arbitrary content to any filesystem location — including mail-scanner configuration files, shared libraries, and web-accessible directories. The vulnerability affects FortiMail versions 7.2.0–7.2.9, 7.4.0–7.4.8, 7.6.0–7.6.6, and 8.0.0–8.0.1. Active exploitation is already confirmed: attackers are using the write primitive to install persistent backdoors, with forensic indicators including a modified /data/lib/liblog.so for library preload hijacking, dropped binaries named "smit" and "webconsole" in standard system directories, and outbound command-and-control traffic to 79.141.169.187 and 45.129.0.192. CISA added the flaw to its KEV catalog and gave federal agencies just three days to remediate — but neither Fortinet nor CISA has released a patch yet, leaving a workaround (restricting management-port access) as the only mitigation. This follows the same pattern as NetScaler and Cisco SD-WAN this week: critical edge/mail infrastructure exploited before a fix exists.

No patch exists — if you run FortiMail, lock down management-port access immediately and hunt for the specific IOCs (modified liblog.so, "smit"/"webconsole" binaries, traffic to the listed IPs) rather than waiting for a fix to ship.

Anthropic reported preliminary Q2 2026 revenue of $11.6 billion — up from just $787 million a year earlier — surpassing OpenAI's $6.7 billion for the same quarter and marking the first time Anthropic's earnings have overtaken its larger rival's. The revenue reversal reframes the OpenAI-vs-Anthropic competitive narrative heading into what could be the largest tech IPO in years.
