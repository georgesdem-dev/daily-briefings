🌟 Top Story: Arista Patches a Third VeloCloud Orchestrator Zero-Day in Four Months CVE-2026-114625 · CVSS 9.9 · ACTIVELY EXPLOITED

Arista has patched CVE-2026-114625, a critical SQL injection flaw in VeloCloud Orchestrator (VCO) — the central management console for VeloCloud SD-WAN deployments — that lets any authenticated user, even one holding only a low-privilege, read-only customer account, escalate to superuser "operator" status through a low-complexity injection attack. That status unlocks privileged internal functions Arista says were never meant to be reachable remotely, handing attackers effective control over the orchestrator and everything it manages. Arista discovered the bug itself, confirmed active exploitation in the wild, and published two IP addresses — 74.82.197.86 and 38.54.50.151 — observed in attacks. Crucially, this is the third VCO zero-day exploited since July, following two earlier incidents patched in the same window, which points to a sustained, targeted campaign against SD-WAN management infrastructure rather than opportunistic scanning. Fixes are available in VCO 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10 and 6.4.2.12; Arista has already patched its own cloud-hosted instances, but organizations running self-managed VCO deployments need to patch immediately given the repeat-target pattern.

If you operate a self-managed VeloCloud Orchestrator, patch to 5.2.3.18 / 6.1.3.10 / 6.4.2.12 now and hunt for connections from 74.82.197.86 and 38.54.50.151 — this is the third exploited VCO zero-day since July, so treat SD-WAN management infrastructure as an active, repeat target, not a one-off incident.

🌟 Top Story: Anthropic's Draft IPO Filing Reveals $450 Billion in Cloud Commitments Ahead of a Mid-November Listing TARGETING ~$1.5T VALUATION

Anthropic's confidential draft IPO filing is now coming into public view, and the headline number is its infrastructure commitment: roughly $450 billion in non-cancelable cloud-compute agreements over the next seven to ten years, split across $111 billion to Google Cloud, $110 billion to AWS, and $31 billion to Microsoft, with the company separately flagging as much as $750 billion in total potential compute spending once deals still under negotiation are included. The draft also discloses a nearly 15-fold revenue jump — first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion against just $1.1 billion a year earlier — while compute spending alone hit almost $9 billion in those six months, more than the company spent in all of 2025. Anthropic is targeting a mid-November listing at a valuation reported anywhere between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, racing to market ahead of OpenAI, which is simultaneously trying to close a $30 billion private round at a $1.25–1.4 trillion valuation rather than go public itself. In a related and more unusual move, OpenAI announced it will no longer train its most capable models with direct access to the live web, instead relying on a cached "offline world" of indexed web content for reinforcement-learning runs — a decision that follows Anthropic limiting its own newest models' live-web access two weeks earlier, meaning both leading labs now agree their training environments cannot safely touch the real-time internet.
