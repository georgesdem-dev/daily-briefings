That diplomatic overture comes against the backdrop of Monday's Rubio/Vance meetings with Qatar's PM, and reports that Trump has told Vance the administration doesn't need a deal with Iran right now, instead directing him toward domestic priorities after the midterms.

🌟 Top Story: Suspected AI-Driven Campaign Breaches 24 Check Point Management Servers Across 22 Organizations in 17 Countries GT13 · CVE-2026-16232 · AI-ASSISTED INTRUSION

Researchers detailed a campaign by a threat actor tracked as GT13 that compromised 24 Check Point Security Management Servers at 22 organizations across 17 countries and territories — spanning financial services, energy, government, transportation, mining, engineering, and manufacturing sectors in the UK, Ireland, France, Netherlands, Greece, Germany, Austria, Bulgaria, Poland, India, Thailand, Vietnam, South Korea, Hong Kong, China, Australia, and the US. GT13 exploited CVE-2026-16232, a critical authentication-bypass flaw in Check Point's SmartConsole login process (CVSS 9.3, patched in Check Point's July 2026 jumbo hotfix), to gain full administrator access to internet-exposed management servers without credentials. Once in, the actor copied firewall policy databases, stole credentials and internal CA material, minted rogue certificates, and exfiltrated security policies — enough to potentially rewrite firewall rules or impersonate trusted internal services later. Analysts flag the operation's speed and consistency across victims as consistent with AI-assisted tooling, though that attribution remains unproven. The confirmed intrusion window ran October 17 to November 2. Separately, a pre-authentication remote-code-execution flaw dating to 2016 (CVE-2026-129514, CVSS 10.0) in the Hex.pm package registry — the primary repository for the Elixir/Erlang ecosystem — was exploited Monday to run commands on six production servers; maintainers patched within roughly 15 minutes and found no tampered packages, but the registry's signing key remains unrotated and password hashes/2FA secrets were exposed. And VMware shipped a fix for a third critical vCenter authentication-bypass flaw (CVE-2026-123903, CVSS 9.8) in the vmdir/SSO component in as many weeks — distinct from the actively-exploited CVE-2026-123890 patched five days earlier — with public proof-of-concept code already posted to GitHub even though Broadcom has not yet confirmed in-the-wild exploitation.

🌟 Top Story: US Tightens Chip Remote-Access Rules on China — Nvidia Sellers Scramble for Buyers; GPT-6.2 Sol Goes Global EXPORT CONTROLS TIGHTEN · GLOBAL CHATGPT ROLLOUT

The Commerce Department's new rule closing the "remote access" loophole on advanced AI chips — announced November 16 and in effect since November 19 — is now visibly reshaping the GPU market. The rule extends 2022-era export controls, which only restricted direct chip sales to China, to also cover compute accessed remotely through data centers anywhere in the world serving China-headquartered companies; it additionally requires licenses for chip exports supporting data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil, with a 90-day window for existing operators to seek licenses. CNBC reports Nvidia GPU resellers are now actively hunting for new buyers as B300 chips hit the market and Chinese firms lose cloud-based remote access to advanced compute, while Asian data-center operators are cutting rental prices and pitching spare capacity to US buyers to fill the gap. On the product side, OpenAI's chat-tuned GPT-6.2 Sol — previously limited to ChatGPT's main tab — is now rolling out globally to all paid tiers (Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise), with the older GPT-5.6 family slated for retirement in December; free and Go-tier users are excluded for now. Anthropic made Claude artifacts installable as standalone desktop apps and expanded its Skills framework for MCP, continuing its push to make Claude outputs persistent, reusable software rather than one-off chat responses. Also notable: OpenAI demonstrated GPT-6 designing novel protein sequences with laboratory-confirmed binding across five biological targets, and published a preprint claiming a matrix-multiplication exponent of 9/4 — a potential efficiency gain pending independent validation.
