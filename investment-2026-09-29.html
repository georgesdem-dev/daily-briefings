📉 Yields Hit 19-Year High, Stocks Slide for a Second Day

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.24% Monday — its highest level since 2007 — as markets priced in a more hawkish Fed path following Fed Governor Barr's comments and a hot inflation reading last week. The Dow fell 347 points (-0.70%), the S&P 500 dropped 0.77% to 7,683.69, and the Nasdaq slid 0.92% to 26,820.38. Rate-sensitive sectors (utilities, materials, real estate) led the decline.

🪖 Oil Extends Gains as Hormuz Standoff Drags On

Brent crude climbed above $106 and is holding near $106.56-$106.89 into Tuesday, a second straight day of gains, after Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days. Iranian officials have privately expressed pessimism about reaching a deal before the US midterm elections in November — a sign this standoff could run much longer than markets initially hoped.

🏦 Fed Hike Odds Firm Up: 72% for October

CME FedWatch now shows a 72.3% probability of a 25bp hike at the October 27-28 meeting, up from prior weeks, after Fed Governor Barr voiced support for further tightening and inflation data ran hot. The Fed already raised rates to 3.75-4.00% on September 16 — its first hike since 2023 — and markets are pricing meaningful odds of a further move in December. Asian markets fell broadly overnight (Nikkei -1.11%, Topix -1.67%, Hang Seng -0.57%) tracking the same rate-path repricing.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
CME FedWatch / Street Consensus

October Hike Odds Firm to 72%, December Tilts Hawkish Too

Following Fed Governor Barr's comments and a hot inflation print, futures markets now price a 72.3% probability of an October hike and meaningful odds of a further December move. This is the single biggest driver of Monday's selloff and the 19-year-high 10Y yield — a more consequential story than the day's oil headlines for most of the market.

Goldman Sachs Asset Management — Market Pulse

Sturdy Growth, Hot Valuations, Elevated Volatility

Goldman's house view continues to favor equities on solid global growth and non-recessionary Fed policy, while flagging that stretched valuations mean volatility spikes — like Monday's yield-driven pullback — should be treated as a recurring feature of this market rather than a trend break.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cash Pile Still Parked

Buffett's continued reluctance to deploy Berkshire's large cash reserve signals he still views valuations as stretched, even as yields rise to levels that make cash and short-duration Treasuries increasingly competitive with equities.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

The Debt Cycle Meets a 19-Year-High Yield

Dalio's long-running concern about the collision of higher rates and consumer/government debt burdens looks increasingly pointed as the 10Y touches its highest level since 2007. A more hawkish Fed easing inflation risk could come at the cost of accelerating the debt-servicing strain he's repeatedly flagged.
