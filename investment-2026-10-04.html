📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🛢️ OPEC+ Holds November Output Steady — No Surprise, No Relief

The OPEC+ Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee met Sunday and kept November production targets unchanged, rolling over the same supply plan in place since September. Total permitted group-wide output stays at 36.206 million barrels per day. Actual production has been running below official targets all year, so the unchanged ceiling doesn't signal a meaningful change in real barrels reaching the market — a neutral outcome that leaves oil's near-term direction mostly in the hands of demand data and geopolitics rather than supply policy.

🗓️ A Quiet Calendar Before Earnings Season Proper

Next week brings relatively little hard economic data: ISM Services PMI on Monday, FOMC Minutes from the September meeting on Wednesday (markets will parse these closely for how unified the committee was on the pause decision), then PepsiCo on Thursday and Delta Air Lines on Friday as the first notable earnings prints. The real Q3 reporting wave — and the test of whether 2026's narrow, tech-led rally can broaden — doesn't begin in earnest until October 13.

📈 Friday's Rally Still the Dominant Story Into the Weekend

Friday's jobs-driven rally (S&P +0.7%, Nasdaq +1.2% to a record, NVDA to a fresh all-time high) remains the freshest read on sentiment. With the data calendar light this week, follow-through — or a reversal — in that risk-on move is likely to hinge on Wednesday's FOMC Minutes and whatever tone emerges on the rate-pause consensus.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
CNBC / Street Consensus — Week Ahead

A Calendar-Light Week Puts Oil and Yields Back in the Driver's Seat

With Q3 earnings season not really starting until October 13, and this week's data limited to ISM Services and FOMC Minutes, the Street's view is that price action will largely track the two forces that dominated September: the path of Treasury yields and oil prices. OPEC+'s steady-targets decision Sunday removes one source of surprise, shifting more weight onto Wednesday's Fed Minutes.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

No Change in Posture Over the Weekend

Buffett's reluctance to deploy Berkshire's ~$397B cash pile remains unchanged — a quiet weekend with an uneventful OPEC+ decision gives him no new reason to view current valuations as more attractive than they were Friday.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

A Quiet Week Is a Good Time to Reassess, Not Relax

Marks's standing caution about nearly four years of investor optimism applies just as much in a slow news week as a volatile one — a market that rallies on a single weak jobs print, then goes quiet into an even lighter calendar, is exactly the kind of complacency he's flagged as a risk.

Goldman Sachs Asset Management — Market Pulse

Watching the FOMC Minutes for Confirmation, Not Surprise

Goldman's house view heading into Wednesday is that the September meeting's minutes are unlikely to overturn the market's rate-pause assumption, but any hint of a split committee or lingering hawkish voices could unwind some of Friday's relief rally quickly given how thin this week's data calendar otherwise is.
