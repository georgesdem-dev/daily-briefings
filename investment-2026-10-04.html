📉 Friday's Catalyst: Soft Jobs Data Ignites Rate-Cut Rally

Friday's September nonfarm payrolls report came in below consensus, sending Treasury yields lower and fueling the broadest market rally in weeks. The cooling labor market gives the Federal Reserve clear runway to cut rates at its November 2026 meeting — futures now price a 25bp cut at ~78% probability. The 10-year Treasury yield rose modestly to 5.28% on the day as bond traders balanced rate-cut hopes against fiscal concerns, but equity investors focused squarely on the dovish signal.

The Nasdaq surged +1.20% to 27,191, touching an intraday record, with Nvidia leading megacap tech higher on the AI hyperscaler spending narrative. Every major index gained on Friday, with small caps (Russell 2000 +0.94%) finally showing signs of life — a potential sign that the rate-cut trade is broadening beyond large-cap tech.

Key observation: The VIX at 15.31 is at its lowest point since the Iran conflict began in early 2026 — the market is pricing in a benign scenario of soft landing + rate cuts + AI earnings growth. This is the complacency zone Howard Marks has been warning about.

🕊️ Iran Interim Peace Deal — Q3's Defining Macro Shift

The interim peace agreement between the US and Iran, reached during Q3 2026, has been the single biggest macro driver of the quarter. Oil has retreated from $102/bbl (May highs) to $91/bbl, removing the geopolitical premium that had boosted energy stocks. The Strait of Hormuz is gradually reopening, with tanker transits rising from 10/day to over 25/day. A permanent deal could push oil toward $80/bbl — a potential headwind for energy stocks but a powerful tailwind for consumer spending, airline stocks, and broad margin expansion across corporate America.

The peace deal paradox: while oil is lower, the conflict has permanently accelerated defense technology spending, AI-driven military systems (Palantir, Anduril), and energy independence infrastructure. JPMorgan estimates the long-term capex unlock from the "energy security" theme at $2+ trillion globally through 2030. This makes industrials and energy-tech crossover names compelling long-term holds regardless of near-term oil pricing.

📋 Q4 Earnings Season Preview — The Calendar That Matters

Q3 2026 earnings season kicks off the week of October 13, starting with the big banks. JPMorgan (Oct 14), Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, and Citigroup will set the tone for financial services. After Q2's impressive beat cycle (S&P 500 delivered +24% YoY earnings growth), the bar is high — consensus expects +19% YoY growth for Q3. The key variable: how much of the AI infrastructure capex is translating into actual revenue for the hyperscalers.

Amazon reports October 28 with Q3 EPS consensus of $2.00 and revenue of $205.6B. AWS re-accelerated to 28% growth in Q2, buoyed by $364B in cloud backlog and $238B in Trainium-specific AI commitments. Microsoft (est. Oct 22) will be the key Azure read-through. Nvidia reports in November (Nov 18), with consensus EPS of $2.09 — the hyperscaler capex guidance for 2027 will be the key catalyst, with $1.3 trillion projected vs. $800B this year.

Net view: Earnings season is the key catalyst for Q4 upside. A beat cycle driven by AI monetization could push the S&P toward Goldman's year-end target of 8,200.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway — Greg Abel Era Begins

$397B Cash Pile: "If There's a Big Decline, We Will Deploy Capital"

At the 2026 Berkshire annual meeting, Buffett sat in the audience for the first time in 60 years — Greg Abel now runs day-to-day operations. Buffett's message on markets was characteristically direct: "If there is a big decline, we will deploy" capital — with the emphasis firmly on "big." With the S&P at 7,700 and the Buffett Indicator (total US market cap / GDP) now at ~227% — a level Buffett himself once described as "playing with fire" — his $397B cash hoard is his clearest signal about valuations. He has specifically criticized the rise of one-day options and prediction market speculation as having crossed from investing into "outright gambling." BRK-B below $480 would be the accumulation zone implied by this thesis; at $494 it's reasonable but not cheap.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital — "Investors Are Overly Optimistic"

Stocks Above Intrinsic Value — Below-Average Returns Likely Ahead

Oaktree's Howard Marks issued a stark warning this quarter: stocks are "being bought at prices higher than their actual value," which means "returns on such investments may turn out to be below average over the next few years." With the VIX at 15, S&P at 7,700, and the prevailing mood decidedly optimistic, Marks advises investors to constantly ask: "How can we prepare for less favorable times?" His prescription for sophisticated investors mirrors this brief's philosophy: increase allocation to income-generating assets (corporate bonds, dividend growth stocks), reduce high-beta names trading at 100x+ earnings, and maintain 15–20% in cash or alternatives for optionality. This is not a bear call — it's a quality call. At these valuations, the difference between good picks and mediocre picks widens dramatically.
