🌟 Top Story: IRGC Says Mines Halted Two More Tankers as CENTCOM Publicly Calls Iran's Claims False BRENT $111.36 · 5-MONTH HIGH

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy said Tuesday that mines it laid in the southern transit zone of the Strait of Hormuz exploded and halted two more tankers it accused of violating its transit regulations, continuing a run of claimed mine strikes that has stretched across multiple consecutive days. For the first time in this cycle, a US CENTCOM spokesman went on record to say Iran's mine claims are false, directly contradicting Tehran's account rather than simply declining to confirm it — a notable escalation in the information war running alongside the physical one. Independently, ship-tracking firm Kpler has recorded at least 18 tanker attacks over the past two weeks, and IMF PortWatch data shows daily transits through the strait have collapsed to roughly 4 vessels on October 18, against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. The squeeze is now fully priced into energy markets: December Brent crude futures climbed 1.65% to $111.36 a barrel Tuesday, their highest level in five months, as the daily flow of oil out of the Persian Gulf has shrunk to about 8.5 million barrels from more than 20 million barrels two weeks ago.

🌟 Top Story: Microsoft's Record Patch Tuesday Lands as Ransomware Pivots From VPN Gateways to the Virtualization Layer 1,413+ CVEs · ACTIVELY EXPLOITED WIN ZERO-DAY

Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday shipped a record haul — over 1,413 CVEs by most counts, with some trackers putting the fully-ranked total at 1,771 — including at least one actively exploited Windows zero-day, continuing a steady climb in monthly patch volume. Separately, Unit 42 and multiple incident-response firms report that ransomware affiliates tracked as Qilin (Spikey Scorpius) and Inc/Settra (Tarnished Scorpius) are still exploiting CVE-2026-0257, the Palo Alto Networks GlobalProtect authentication bypass, with over 167,000 exposed instances still unpatched five months after the fix shipped. The more alarming shift is tactical: ReliaQuest and Unit 42 now describe attackers pivoting directly from the compromised VPN gateway to the vCenter management appliance, bypassing in-guest EDR and endpoint controls entirely by attacking the virtualization layer itself rather than the servers running on top of it. That KEV pileup sits alongside last week's BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764), which Mandiant traced to zero-day exploitation by a China-linked actor as early as late August, and which passed its CISA federal remediation deadline yesterday, October 19.

Prioritize this month's Windows zero-day patch immediately, and if GlobalProtect/PAN-OS is in your environment, verify vCenter and other virtualization-layer management interfaces are segmented from VPN-accessible network zones — EDR on the guest OS will not catch an attacker who pivots straight to the hypervisor layer.
