📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

📈 Nasdaq Sets a Fresh Record as Tech and M&A Chatter Drive Monday's Gains

All four major US indices closed higher Monday, with the Nasdaq's +1.1% move to a new record close leading the way on mega-cap tech strength and M&A speculation. The Russell 2000 also advanced (+0.50%), showing the rally broadening beyond just large caps. Overnight, Asia extended the risk-on tone — the Nikkei jumped 2.4% — while Europe opened more mixed (FTSE +0.34%, DAX +0.09%, CAC -0.80%).

🛢️ Oil Eases as Hormuz Crude Flows Recover, But Diesel Stays Scarce

WTI fell 1.8% Monday to $89.43 and Brent slipped to $100.32, as reports indicated crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to roughly 76% of pre-war levels. Refined-product flows remain far more constrained (~11% of normal), keeping US diesel prices at record highs ($6.53/gal nationally, $8.44/gal in California). Oil ticked modestly higher again in early Tuesday Asian trade, still capped near these levels. Iran's Ghalibaf reiterated the strait won't fully reopen until Washington meets Iran's seven conditions, and reports surfaced of tankers hit by unidentified projectiles near Hormuz over the weekend — keeping the risk premium alive even as headline barrels recover.

🏦 FOMC Minutes Wednesday — Parsing a Hawkish September Hike

Wednesday's minutes cover the September 15-16 meeting, where the Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75-4.00% in a 12-0 vote, with then-Chair commentary stressing that "inflation is too high, and has been for too long." Vice Chair Jefferson has since signaled inflation likely stays elevated near-term before resuming its decline toward 2%. With the 10Y yield sitting near a two-decade high (5.28%) and market pricing on the next move mixed across outlets, the minutes are this week's most important single data point.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs — Macro Research

Above-Consensus Global Growth, Fading Inflation Pass-Through

Goldman's 2026 global growth outlook sits at ~2.8% versus ~2.5% consensus, with US growth seen at 2.6% on tax cuts, easier financial conditions, and reduced tariff drag. Core inflation in the US and UK is expected to keep easing toward 2% as tariff pass-through effects fade — a slower, steadier disinflation path than the market's more volatile rate-path pricing this week might suggest.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

A Bigger Footprint in Housing

Weeks after Buffett stepped down as chairman (Howard Buffett now holds the role, Greg Abel remains CEO), Berkshire's housing push continues: the acquisition of Taylor Morrison makes it America's 4th-largest homebuilder, and the firm added another $192.6M to its Lennar stake Oct 1-2. Buyback pace suggests Berkshire could top $10B in repurchases this year.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Watching the Government's Debt Position as an "Inflection Point"

Dalio has been warning that the US government's financial condition is reaching an inflection point, pointing to Treasury Secretary Bessent's debt-purchase plans and Japan trimming its US bond holdings. He continues to recommend gold and bitcoin as hedges, and separately has flagged that his "Changing World Order" framework is entering what he calls "the war stage" — a lens worth keeping in mind given the Hormuz standoff.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

No Fresh Commentary This Week

No new October remarks surfaced in this round of research. The most recent located reference has Marks warming further to bitcoin alongside Dalio's bond/dollar warnings — treat as background context rather than this week's news until a dated primary source turns up.
