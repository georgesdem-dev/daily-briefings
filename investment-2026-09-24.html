📉 Treasury Yields Hit 19-Year High — The Day's Dominant Story

The 10Y yield spiked above 5.11%, the highest since 2007, and the 5-year Treasury topped 5% for the first time since 2007 as well. The move followed a weak Treasury auction, hotter-than-expected S&P business-activity data, and Fed Governor Michael Barr's comment that additional rate hikes may be needed to bring down sticky inflation. Real estate and long-duration growth stocks bore the brunt of the selloff; Real Estate's YTD gain compressed sharply as a result.

🛢️ Oil Reverses Higher on Energy-Driven Inflation Concerns

After two straight days of declines on Iran-diplomacy hopes, Brent crude climbed back toward $100 as energy costs fed into Wednesday's inflation worries — a reminder that the oil-price de-escalation trade of the past few sessions is not a one-way bet. XOM and energy broadly benefit near-term, but sustained higher oil also reinforces the inflation/rate-hike narrative pressuring the rest of the market.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Tough to Find Values When Everybody Is Preferring Gambling"

Buffett remains pointedly cautious on equity valuations, particularly around AI-related capex by mega-cap tech, and has criticized markets increasingly driven by speculative trading rather than long-term investing. Berkshire's cash pile near $400B under CEO Greg Abel reflects that stance — Wednesday's yield spike and growth-stock selloff is the kind of volatility that cash hoard is built to weather.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"Risk-Taking Is [Elevated]... We Should Be Careful"

Marks warned this week that prudence "is not in the ascendancy at the moment," flagging that risk-taking, while not extreme, is elevated — with particular concern about stretched valuations outside the Magnificent Seven. Wednesday's rate-driven selloff is a live example of the kind of correction his caution anticipates; his prescription remains quality and income over speculative breadth.

Goldman Sachs Research — September 2026

S&P Target Still 8,000 Despite the Yield Shock

Goldman's year-end S&P target of 8,000 hasn't shifted, but Wednesday's move to a 19-year-high 10Y yield tests that call directly — higher discount rates compress equity multiples, especially for long-duration growth names. Goldman's high-yield dividend Conviction List (UPS, TSN, TPG) becomes more relevant as a hedge if the rate story persists.

JPMorgan — Bank Sector View

Higher-for-Longer Rates: A Net Positive for Bank NIMs

JPMorgan's own 2026 EPS forecast of $21.25 is underpinned partly by resilient net interest margins — a dynamic that higher-for-longer rates actually reinforces, even as it pressures growth-stock valuations elsewhere. This is one reason JPM sits in the income/defensive sleeve of this portfolio rather than the growth sleeve.
