💥 Intel Crashes ~29% on Guidance Miss, Dragging Chips and Tech Lower

Intel's stock suffered one of the worst days in its history Friday, plunging roughly 29% to $65.76 after guiding Q4 revenue to $15.0-16.0B (below the Street's $17B estimate) and adjusted EPS to just $0.25 (versus $0.43 expected) — a guide implying earnings fall by more than half quarter-over-quarter. Management flagged an accelerating PC market slump, projecting a 12% decline in 2026 and a further 10% drop in 2027. The selloff spilled into the broader chip complex (AMD -10%, Nvidia -3%, Dell -13%) and left Technology as the S&P's worst-performing sector Friday at -4.89%, pulling the Nasdaq down 2.86% on the day and capping its worst month since March 2025.

Alphabet extended its post-earnings slide for a third consecutive session, closing Friday at $279.84 — down roughly 14% from Wednesday's $324.89 close. Amazon held up better, closing at $240.35 (-1.41%), with AWS's 39% growth continuing to get less credit from the market than its AI-capex and cash-burn concerns.

🚢 Strait of Hormuz Tanker Attack Revives Iran-War Escalation Risk

A tanker was struck by explosive devices in the Strait of Hormuz Friday — UK maritime security authorities confirmed the attack, with responsibility not yet established. WTI crude settled up 2.08% to $104.12, its highest level in roughly five months, as traders priced in a higher probability of escalation around a key global oil chokepoint. Oil posted its fourth consecutive monthly gain, with WTI up 15.2% and Brent up 13.6% in October alone. Energy (+1.91%) was Friday's best-performing sector, alongside Consumer Staples (+1.41%) and Utilities (+0.67%), as investors rotated into havens on both the tech selloff and the geopolitical flare-up.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Treasury Slump Has "a Long Way to Go," and AI Spending Makes It Worse

Dalio has been warning through October that expanding bond supply is pushing yields higher in a self-reinforcing way, raising borrowing costs and debt levels further — a spiral he says won't end until higher rates weaken the economy enough to rebalance supply and demand. He's paired this with his continued view that AI capital spending is a classic bubble approaching its bursting point; this week's back-to-back GOOGL/AMZN cash-burn reactions and Intel's guidance collapse all fit squarely inside that thesis.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Bracing Investors for a "Cold, Hard Reality Check"

Buffett has issued a blunt message this month warning that elevated valuations and crowded positioning leave many investors exposed to a sharp reset. Friday's Intel-driven tech rout and GOOGL's three-day, 14% slide are the kind of concentrated, sector-specific drawdowns Buffett's framing anticipates — a reminder that diversification away from the most crowded AI-capex names remains the better-tested approach.
