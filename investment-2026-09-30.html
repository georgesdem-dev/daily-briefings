🛢️ Oil Drops Sharply as Saudi Pipeline Bypasses Hormuz

Crude posted its biggest single-day drop in more than a week Tuesday, with WTI slipping toward $89 (-3.5%) and Brent settling near $103, down from Monday's $106.89. The catalyst: Saudi Arabia resumed flows through a pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz entirely, and JPMorgan data now shows Middle East crude exports have rebounded to 17.5 million barrels a day — about 98% of pre-war levels. This is the clearest de-escalation signal in weeks, even without a formal Iran-US deal.

🥇 Gold Sinks to 7-Week Low as Rate-Hike Bets Dominate

Gold fell 3.71% to $4,127 an ounce, its sharpest drop in months, as the 10-year Treasury yield's hold near 5.244% (a 19-year high) keeps drawing capital away from non-yielding safe havens. Even with geopolitical risk still elevated, rate expectations are now the dominant force in precious metals.

⚖️ A Tale of Two Sectors: Tech +4.4% MTD, Financials -6.3% MTD

With September closing out, Technology is the only S&P 500 sector in positive territory for the month (+4.4%), while Financials are down 6.3% — on pace for their worst month since March 2023 — as surging bond yields raise the cost of capital and threaten loan demand. Tuesday alone: Financials -0.3%, Utilities +1.2%, Industrials +1.0%, Technology roughly flat.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Nothing" — Buffett Says Today's Wobbles Aren't a Real Dip Yet

Buffett has called the current market "a casino," noting "we've never had people in a more gambling mood than now," with the S&P 500 forward P/E near 21x versus a 16x long-run average. He says Berkshire's $397.4B cash pile stays on the sidelines until markets fall further — the wobbles so far in 2026 are "nothing" compared to what he's waiting for.

Street Consensus — September Wrap

A Tale of Two Sectors Closes Out the Month

Technology finishes September as the only positive S&P sector (+4.4% MTD), carried by AI-driven strength in names like MSFT and NVDA. Financials close their worst month since March 2023 (-6.3% MTD) as elevated yields squeeze the cost of capital — a split that's likely to persist into October unless the 10Y yield rolls over.

Howard Marks / Oaktree

"Good News Is Incorporated and Causes Price to Go Higher"

Marks' framing of market psychology is a useful lens on Tuesday's oil-driven relief: as Hormuz de-escalation signals emerged, energy-sensitive fear eased and risk sentiment broadly improved, even with the index barely moving. His broader message remains one of caution about excess optimism at today's valuations.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Gold Allocation Still Reflects Long-Cycle Caution

Dalio's family office has reportedly held more than 75% of its disclosed US equity exposure in gold-based ETFs — a stance that looks tested by Tuesday's 3.71% gold selloff, but consistent with his long-standing view that debt-cycle and currency risks warrant a structural hedge regardless of near-term price swings.
