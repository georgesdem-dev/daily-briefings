🔀 A Tale of Two Earnings Nights: AAPL Soars, HII Jumps, GOOGL and AMZN Slide

Thursday's after-the-bell earnings produced a genuine split verdict on the AI-capex question. Alphabet extended Wednesday's selloff into its worst day ever, closing down roughly 12.6% at $283.82, as investors kept punishing the combination of an EPS miss and negative free cash flow. Amazon delivered a standout AWS quarter (+39% growth, record 13.9% operating margin, $578B backlog) but still fell after hours — first -4.25% in the regular session to $243.79, then another leg down of roughly 7-9% after hours toward the low-$220s* — as the market again focused on heavy AI infrastructure capex and weak free cash flow rather than the top-line beat.

On the other side of the ledger, Apple beat estimates ($2.02 EPS vs. $1.99 expected) and closed at a fresh all-time high of $348.91, extending to a new peak after hours — a reminder that the market is discriminating between AI spenders with clean balance sheets and those burning cash. Huntington Ingalls was the day's biggest single-stock winner in the portfolio, gapping up 13% to roughly $316 on a record quarter and raised 2026 shipbuilding revenue guidance.

🔄 Defensive Rotation Cushions the Broader Market

Despite the Nasdaq's -1.38% drag from Alphabet's crash, most stocks in the S&P 500 actually rose Thursday. Health Care (+2.4%) and Real Estate (+2.2%) led, followed by Consumer Staples (+1.5%), as capital rotated out of mega-cap growth/communication-services names and into defensives — exactly the kind of broadening this portfolio's healthcare and dividend sleeve is designed to capture. The Russell 2000 small-cap index also outperformed, up 1.0% to 2,859.22, a sign that risk appetite outside of AI-capex-exposed mega-caps remains intact.

*AMZN's exact after-hours/premarket print varied across sources ($221.87 to ~$226); the figure above reflects the more frequently-cited range and should be confirmed against Friday's opening print.

Marks has argued that broad awareness of bubble risk is itself evidence against a classic bubble.
