🔥 Big Tech Earnings Week — The Good, The Bad & The Ugly

AMZN in Focus — Guidance Disappoints: Amazon is at the center of today's Nasdaq selloff (-1.38%) after its Q3 earnings revealed guidance that fell short of elevated expectations. While AWS cloud revenue remained strong, e-commerce margin recovery is proceeding slower than anticipated. This follows a pattern of "beat on earnings, miss on guidance" that has become common in Q3 2026 big-tech reporting.

Bright Spots: Consumer Discretionary (+2.22% today) and Capital Goods (+1.79%) show resilience as the market rotates away from high-multiple tech names toward value and cyclicals heading into year-end. XOM (+1.17%) benefits as energy remains the safe harbor for the week.

Context: Today is Halloween Eve — historically one of the strongest seasonal trading days of the year. The selloff may be exaggerated by low liquidity and end-of-month rebalancing. The S&P 500 at 7,720 remains +6.1% above its September 30 close of 7,275, signalling the broader bull market thesis is intact despite the Nasdaq stumble.

🪖 Iran War — Day 175: Oil Slides, Ceasefire Talks Grind Forward

WTI crude fell to $92.28/bbl (-1.96%) today as diplomatic channels remain open despite a fragile ceasefire framework. Brent crude continues to trade at an elevated premium (~$96–98/bbl) reflecting the ongoing Hormuz passage constraints — now down to under 15 vessels/day from the normal ~40. The OECD has warned of recession risk if the conflict persists past Q1 2027, estimating the war has already added 0.5 percentage points to US interest rates.

The geopolitical risk premium in oil has narrowed materially from the $114+ Brent highs seen in late September following Houthi strike escalations. The key question for energy investors: does WTI find a floor at $90 (structural demand) or does a ceasefire resolution compress prices toward $75–80? Goldman Sachs maintains that diesel prices stay elevated through 2027 regardless of a ceasefire, supporting energy sector free cash flows.

📉 Macro Backdrop: 10Y at 4.92%, Fed on Hold, Q3 GDP 3.4%

The 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly to 4.92% (-0.02 bps), still near its highest sustained levels of 2026 after spiking above 5.6% in late September. The Federal Reserve is expected to hold rates steady through year-end — markets are pricing one cut in Q1 2027 contingent on inflation normalization. JPMorgan notes that higher policy rates are not constraining the AI capital expenditure cycle, which is largely rate-insensitive given the long-term ROI horizons of hyperscaler investment.

Goldman Sachs reports US Q3 2026 GDP is tracking at an impressive +3.4%, driven by a trillion-dollar AI capex cycle and resilient consumer spending. With core inflation near but still above target — kept elevated by war-related goods inflation and "tech-flation" in services — the Fed's path remains cautious. Kevin Warsh, widely seen as the next Fed Chair, is described as a "fundamentalist" on price stability by Gary Cohn, suggesting higher-for-longer is the base case through mid-2027.

November Midterms (Tuesday, Nov 3): Markets are beginning to price in mid-term election outcomes. Historically, the period immediately before midterms introduces volatility that resolves sharply after results are known. Positioning for a post-election rally in the first two weeks of November is a common seasonal trade.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"We've Never Had People in a More Gambling Mood" — Record $373B Cash Pile

Buffett is issuing stark, repeated warnings about the stock market this October — an unusual frequency of public caution from the Oracle of Omaha. With Berkshire sitting on $373 billion in cash and Treasury bills (one-third of its market cap), the message is clear: the market is not cheap. "So we've never had people in a more gambling mood than now," Buffett reportedly stated, echoing concerns that bond yields at multi-decade highs are competing with overpriced equities. However, his prescription is measured: "If there is a big decline, we will deploy capital." BRK-B at current levels remains a moderate-risk holding — Greg Abel's succession is well-managed, and the cash pile is an option on a 10–20% market correction. BRK-B below $480 is the accumulation zone implied by this thesis.

Goldman Sachs — October 2026 Market Pulse

Overweight Stocks 3-Month, Overweight Bonds 12-Month — Q3 GDP at 3.4%

Goldman Sachs has upgraded both stocks and bonds in their October Market Pulse, citing "little recession risk and strong earnings growth." Their tactical call: overweight equities over the next three months as Q3 earnings season broadly beats expectations, then rotate toward bonds over 12 months as rates eventually normalise. The US Q3 GDP is tracking at 3.4% — an impressive number — driven by $1T+ in cumulative AI capex creating a cyclical growth impulse. Goldman's key message: the AI capital expenditure cycle is creating durable earnings power that the market has consistently underestimated. Their strategists flag the $10T AI + defense + climate supercycle as the defining investment theme for the rest of the decade. Top GS overweights: Technology, Energy, and select Financials (well-capitalised banks).

JPMorgan Global Research — Q4 2026 Strategy Note

US Is Core Overweight — AI Capex Cycle Insensitive to Interest Rates

JPMorgan's chief portfolio manager stated this week that higher policy rates will not constrain US economic growth because the AI capital expenditure cycle is "largely insensitive to interest rates" — the hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta) are investing based on multi-decade competitive positioning, not short-term borrowing costs. Financial conditions remain supportive with credit spreads near historic lows and corporate fundamentals solid. JPMorgan maintains the US as their core overweight market for Q4 2026 and into 2027. Specific tactical call: add MSFT and META on any earnings-driven dips (like today). Reduce AMZN until guidance clarity improves. The firm also notes that Financials (JPM, GS) benefit from a "higher for longer" rate environment through net interest margin expansion — a contrarian positive for the lagging sector.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"The S&P Is Not Cheap — But Cheaper Than It Was, And That's Encouraging"

Howard Marks' measured October commentary reflects the pragmatic balance this brief tries to embody: the S&P 500 is not cheap by historical standards, but it has derated meaningfully from its spring 2026 highs, making it "cheaper than it used to be." Marks' practical framework for moderate-risk investors: increase allocation to income-generating assets (corporate bonds, dividend stocks — hence PEP, XOM in this brief), reduce high-beta names trading at 100x+ earnings, and maintain 10–15% cash for the inevitable dislocation. His bottom line: "The market gets a head of steam based on optimism." With Goldman overweight equities and Marks urging selectivity, the synthesis for Georges is clear — own quality names with defined upside (NVDA, MSFT, META), cushion with income (PEP, XOM), and keep VOO as the broad index anchor that captures the upside if both Buffett and Marks are being too cautious.
