🌟 Top Story: AI Attack Framework ARTEX Breaches WorkOS, Exposing Customer Slack Conversations THIRD ARTEX-LINKED INCIDENT IN TWO WEEKS

WorkOS, an identity-infrastructure provider used by thousands of SaaS companies, disclosed that it detected anomalous traffic to an internal business application on October 30 at 16:39 UTC and published a security bulletin on November 2. Its investigation found evidence the intrusion was likely driven by ARTEX, an AI-assisted autonomous penetration-testing framework, operating from IP addresses based in Hong Kong and mainland China. The exposed system held customer contact details, account histories, sales and support communications, and — notably — excerpts from customers' own Slack conversations with WorkOS's sales and support teams. WorkOS says it has no evidence that production services or application data were touched; the exposure was confined to CRM and support tooling. This is the third ARTEX-linked campaign reported inside two weeks, following a China-linked operation against 200+ organizations and Microsoft's finding that North Korean groups Jade Sleet, Coral Sleet and Jasper Sleet have also used the framework operationally — reinforcing that agentic penetration-testing tools are now shared, commoditized infrastructure across unrelated threat actors rather than a one-off bespoke capability.

Three unrelated actor sets — a China-linked campaign, North Korean state groups, and now whoever hit WorkOS — have all used the same ARTEX framework within a two-week span. Treat any AI-assisted-pentest tooling as presumptively available to any sufficiently resourced adversary, not just one nation-state's toolkit.

🌟 Top Story: China to Roll Out Full-Lifecycle AI Safety Rules After Global "Security Lapses" COVERS DEEPSEEK, ALIBABA, MOONSHOT

China's state economic planner, the NDRC, is fast-tracking comprehensive legislation to govern AI from development through deployment — among the most sweeping safety frameworks any government has proposed, covering everything from training data to compute access. The rules will apply directly to China's leading model developers, including DeepSeek, Alibaba and Moonshot, who are racing to build models competitive with the best from OpenAI and Anthropic. Officials framed the push explicitly as a response to a string of AI-related security incidents emerging globally this year, stating that "AI safety is not a problem one company, industry or country can tackle alone." The move lands the same week DeepSeek confirmed plans to deploy at least 160,000 Huawei Ascend 950DT chips at a new Ulanqab data center, beginning pre-training workloads on domestic silicon as soon as late 2026 — tying China's regulatory tightening to a simultaneous push for compute self-sufficiency. Separately, with today's US midterm elections underway, OpenAI and Anthropic both stood up dedicated election-monitoring teams to watch for misuse of their models, and the UK's top cyber official warned the country will likely face AI-enabled attacks on critical infrastructure by 2028, as the technology lets attackers discover and exploit vulnerabilities at unprecedented scale.
