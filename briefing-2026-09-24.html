🌟 Top Story: First Documented Autonomous AI-Orchestrated Malware — CLOSEDQUORUM AI-NATIVE MALWARE

Cisco Talos disclosed (Sep 22) what it says is the first publicly documented Windows implant that delegates tactical command-and-control decisions to a panel of commercial LLMs — DeepSeek, Qwen, Mistral, and Google Gemini vote on the malware's next post-compromise action. The implant, written in Go (~16.4MB), targets Windows credentials, saved browser passwords, and cryptocurrency wallet data. Once deployed, the AI panel runs the attack without the operator needing to be online — a "credentials-as-a-service" model where the differentiator is the autonomous orchestration layer itself. Talos has no confirmation of in-the-wild deployment yet but traced binary artifacts to a developer active on carding forums since 2025. Talos simultaneously open-sourced CAIRN, a free toolkit for hunting and classifying this emerging class of AI-integrated malware.

This is a structural escalation, not an isolated sample: malware that no longer needs a human operator online to make tactical decisions changes response-time assumptions for detection and containment across the board.

🎣 Microsoft Disrupts EvilTokens Phishing Service — 12,000 Inboxes Affected

Microsoft announced Sep 23 it disrupted the EvilTokens phishing-as-a-service operation, which had given criminals access to roughly 12,000 victim inboxes through token-theft techniques that bypass MFA. The takedown adds to a steady cadence of PhaaS disruptions this year but underscores how session-token theft continues to outpace password-only defenses.
