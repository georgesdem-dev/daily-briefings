🚀 SpaceX Blowout Quarter Reignites the AI Trade — and Sparks Tesla Merger Talk

Wednesday's session is running hot on a single catalyst: SpaceX posted Q3 revenue of $14.6B (+161% YoY, vs. $13B estimates), net income of $4.4B (vs. a $1.05B loss a year ago) and adjusted EBITDA of $8.8B (+306%), sending the newly-public stock up as much as 10% to a high of $166.18. The bigger surprise came alongside the print: Elon Musk, who leads both companies, said SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees to discuss a potential merger — a headline still being digested by markets ahead of Tesla's own earnings tonight.

Super Micro Computer piled onto the AI-rally narrative, jumping over 11% pre-market after issuing preliminary Q1 FY2027 revenue guidance of $18.5-19.5B, well above its prior $14.5-15.5B outlook, citing surging AI-platform demand. The S&P 500 is up 0.51% to 7,751.78, the Nasdaq leads at +1.12% to 26,719.56, and the VIX has eased further to 16.51 — but all eyes are on tonight's after-the-close reports from Meta and Tesla, with options markets pricing roughly 7% moves for each.

📉 Netflix Beats Estimates but Slides on Weak Guidance — UnitedHealth Jumps on Raised Outlook

Netflix kicked off mega-cap earnings with a mixed result: Q3 revenue of $12.87B and EPS of $0.87 topped profit estimates and matched revenue expectations, but the stock fell as much as 6.65% premarket to around $64-65 — a 26-month low — as investors focused on soft Q4 revenue guidance, a lower 2026 ad-revenue outlook, and a fresh round of cost cuts and leadership changes. Needham downgraded the stock, flagging a "content problem" that "seems clear"; technicians are watching $65.52 as the next support level.

On the healthcare side, UnitedHealth posted a Q3 beat and raised its full-year guidance, sending the stock up roughly 3% to around $417 and helping lift the Dow alongside 3M. It's a reminder that this week's earnings tape is a story of dispersion, not a uniform re-rating — streaming/ad-driven names are getting punished for soft guidance while managed care and industrials are being rewarded for raised outlooks.

🏭 Sector Performance — Energy and Tech Still Running Away From the Pack

Energy remains the runaway YTD leader at +54.6%, extending its lead as Brent pushes back above $110 on persistent Hormuz risk, with Technology a distant second at +29.6% — both far ahead of the broader index. Healthcare (+14.9%) continues to climb as a defensive standout, helped today by UnitedHealth's earnings beat. Consumer staples (+10.8%), materials (+9.5%), industrials (+7.7%) and real estate (+2.8%) round out the positive column. Four sectors remain in the red for the year: financials (-0.5%), utilities (-2.3%), communication services (-5.9%) and consumer discretionary (-6.6%) — though Netflix's slide today is a reminder that communication services' underperformance is being driven by idiosyncratic, not macro, stories.

🪖 Hormuz Premium Still Live as Brent Climbs Back Above $110

Brent rose 1.23% to $110.13 Wednesday, with forecasters citing Iran-talks rumors as a swing factor alongside the persistent tanker-attack risk in the Strait of Hormuz. A bull case near $113 and bear case near $105 are both in play depending on how any diplomatic track develops — a reminder this remains the single biggest unresolved swing factor for energy, inflation expectations and the Fed's rate path into year-end, even as equity markets largely shrug it off for now.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Paul Tudor Jones / Tudor Investment Corp

"I'll Be Short the Whole Year" — Warns 2027 Could Be the Toughest Stretch in 25 Years

Jones told CNBC's Squawk Box that "2027 is going to be really, really challenging for the stock market... there's almost no value in the market," citing the most negative equity supply-demand picture since 2002. His thesis isn't valuation-based but flow-based: corporate buybacks and M&A have been shrinking the pool of available shares for years, but that tide is turning as major AI-sector offerings pile up — Anthropic and OpenAI have offerings ahead, and SpaceX just completed one. He also flags the ballooning fiscal deficit, putting odds at "60-40" that markets are already on a dangerous path. This is the most explicitly bearish call from a major macro investor in this briefing's recent history and is now added to the watchlist as a standing risk for 2027 planning, even though it doesn't change today's tactical picks.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cautious on Valuations — Still Building the Alphabet Stake Despite the Buffett Indicator at 227%

Buffett's playbook remains unchanged: Berkshire's ~$373B cash pile signals he's waiting for real distress, not just a dip, while the Buffett Indicator (market cap to GDP) sits near 227% — well above the 200% level he once called "playing with fire." Yet Berkshire's ~$37.4B build in Alphabet shows selective conviction persists even in an expensive market. The lesson for this portfolio: stay selective (UNH, AMZN, JNJ) rather than broadly aggressive, especially with Jones now warning about equity supply dynamics into 2027.

Bubble Warnings Persist Alongside Tariff and Geopolitical Risk

Dalio remains among the hedge-fund titans flagging bubble-like conditions in the AI trade. Tonight's Meta and Tesla earnings — both heavy AI-capex reporters, with Tesla also now entangled in merger speculation with SpaceX — are the next test of whether markets keep rewarding AI spending or start demanding profitability proof. Dalio's framework (alongside Jones's supply-side warning) argues for continued selectivity rather than chasing the SpaceX/Super Micro-style momentum names.
