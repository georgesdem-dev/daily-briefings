🪖 Iran War — Oil Sinks on Diplomacy Hopes, But No Ceasefire Yet

WTI crude fell $4.52 (-4.5%) Monday, its sharpest one-day drop in weeks, on renewed hopes that Washington and Tehran could return to the ceasefire framework negotiated back in June. President Pezeshkian has signaled Iran is willing to "reciprocate" if the US restores those terms, but Washington has so far kept up economic pressure and struck fresh Iranian targets — as of early September, no active ceasefire is in force and Trump has warned of harsher retaliation if Iran strikes again. Markets are trading the *hope* of de-escalation more than any confirmed breakthrough, which keeps oil a two-way risk this week.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cash Pile Pushes Past $400B — "We Aren't in It to Make 5-6%"

Berkshire's cash and T-bill hoard, already a record $397.4B at Q1, is now believed to have crossed $400B under CEO Greg Abel, who has extended Buffett's multi-year selling pattern (Apple trimmed to ~22% of the equity book; Bank of America cut by more than half). Buffett's own words: minor market drops of 5-6% aren't enough to deploy it. Buybacks resumed in March but only at a token $234M, signaling the market still looks fully valued to Omaha.

Goldman Sachs Research — September 2026 Outlook

S&P 500 Target 8,000 — And a New High-Yield Conviction Pick in UPS

Goldman's year-end S&P target stands at 8,000, anchored on a raised 2026 EPS forecast of $340 (+24% YoY). Separately, Goldman's Conviction List highlights UPS as a top high-yield dividend idea — 6.6% yield, $132 target — alongside its broader overweight-equities stance (2026 EPS growth seen at 31% in the US). September's seasonal weakness remains a live caution even after Monday's rally.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Debt "Capital War" Warning — Yet Still Buying US Tech

Dalio continues to warn that US government borrowing needs (~$2T 2026 deficit, ~$10T in maturing debt to refinance) are outpacing investor appetite, and recently called the market "very likely" in a bubble by technical definition. Yet Bridgewater's latest 13F filings show large additions to Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon — a barbell of macro-tail hedges (dollar, gold) paired with continued AI-compounder exposure that this brief's picks largely mirror.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

AI Is Structural, Not a Fad — But Selectivity Still Matters

Marks argues AI has moved from abstraction to economic force "at a speed that approaches instantaneous," a structural development investors must grapple with rather than dismiss as hype. He pairs this with continued caution on the US fiscal position — America's "golden credit card" increasingly strained by debt issuance colliding with buyer capacity limits. His prescription: favor income-generating, reasonably-valued quality over high-beta names at extreme multiples.
