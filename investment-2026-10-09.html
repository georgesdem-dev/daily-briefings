📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

💻 AI-Revenue Doubts Hit Tech as the Yield Spike Extends

Nasdaq fell 1.25% Thursday after a Financial Times report questioned whether OpenAI's revenue figures were overstated, reviving AI-valuation jitters across the mega-cap complex (NVDA -2.94%, MSFT -1.35%). The Dow (+0.10%) and small caps (Russell 2000 +0.03%) were far steadier, showing the damage was concentrated in AI-adjacent names rather than broad-based. Meanwhile the 10-year Treasury yield pushed to a fresh 24-year intraday high of 5.357% before settling near 5.32% — this is a continuation of Wednesday's spike, not the retreat markets had hoped for after that day's strong auction.

🥤 Correcting Yesterday: PepsiCo Beat, But Cut Guidance

Yesterday's brief characterized PepsiCo's Thursday print as "in-line." Fuller results show a modest beat — adjusted EPS $2.34 versus $2.29 consensus, revenue $25.27B versus ~$25B expected — but management cut full-year core EPS growth guidance to 2.5-3% from a prior 5-7%, citing persistent North America cost and margin pressure. The stock rose 3.73% to $128.34 as the beat outweighed the guidance cut in Thursday's trade. We're flagging the correction for the record.

✈️ Delta Beats to Open Earnings Season; China Reopens Calmly

Delta reported Q3 EPS of $2.01 this morning, beating a consensus that was unusually scattered across data providers ($1.76-$1.96), with management citing "significant improvement" in the revenue outlook despite a roughly 40% YoY jump in jet fuel costs. Separately, China's mainland markets reopened Thursday after the Golden Week holiday in orderly fashion — Shanghai Composite +0.2% to ~3,848, Shenzhen Component +0.6% to ~12,960 — largely shrugging off the overnight Wall Street selloff. Attention now turns to next week's Chinese trade and inflation data for signs of stimulus traction.

🇮🇷 Hormuz Campaign Widens Geographically — Oil Climbs to ~$91

Iran's tanker-attack campaign is expanding beyond the strait itself: vessels were struck off Qatar (the first incident in that zone in six months) and near Fujairah in the Gulf of Oman within a 24-hour span this week. UK Maritime Trade Operations counted 9 attacks in the strait so far this month — already half of September's full-month total. Traffic through Hormuz has fallen to a two-month low even as broader Gulf oil exports have largely recovered, pushing freight, insurance, and security costs higher and keeping WTI elevated near $91.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs — Macro Research

AI Power Costs and Chip Constraints Are Structural, Not Transitory

Goldman's latest macro view flags AI-driven electricity demand and semiconductor supply constraints as structural inflation drivers rather than temporary pass-through effects, projecting developed-market inflation staying above 3% through at least 2028. The note also cites the 2026-2027 global election calendar — covering over 40% of world population — as a source of elevated bond-yield risk premium, a relevant lens given this week's 24-year-high Treasury yields.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

$365.5B Cash Pile Stays Ready for Opportunistic Deals

With Howard Buffett now non-executive chairman and Greg Abel continuing as CEO (Warren Buffett now chairman emeritus), Berkshire's governance transition is largely complete. The firm's enormous cash position — ~$365.5B — continues to be read by analysts as dry powder for buying opportunistically if current elevated valuations correct.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Debt-Cycle Limits Could Squeeze Lower-Income Borrowers First

Building on his Oct 6 warning of a possible US debt crisis within roughly three years, Dalio added this week that rising borrowing costs — a direct consequence of yields like this week's 24-year highs — are likely to squeeze lower-income borrowers before they show up in broader credit-market stress. He maintains his recommendation to stay short-duration and hold gold as a hedge.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital
