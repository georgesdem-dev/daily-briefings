🌟 Top Story: Three Tankers Struck Mines in Hormuz as Iran Discusses a Seven-Day Plan to Reopen the Strait 3 TANKERS AFIRE · TRUMP DOWNPLAYS VANCE LETTER

Iran's military said three oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines over the past 24 hours while attempting to transit an unauthorized route through the Strait of Hormuz, part of a restricted corridor east of the strait. The Iranian army said the vessels had switched off their radar and navigation systems and disregarded IRGC navy warnings before the strikes. No side has claimed responsibility for laying the mines. On the diplomatic track, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran remains in close and ongoing contact with mediators — including Oman, Pakistan and Qatar — over a seven-day confidence-building plan, first floated to Washington via Qatar in September, that would reopen Hormuz within a week if conditions are met: a ceasefire across the Middle East, the unfreezing of Iran's foreign assets, relief from oil-sector sanctions, and an end to the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. Araghchi said the timeline could potentially be compressed to less than a week if mediators find a workable sequencing. Separately, Iranian media confirmed Tehran received a letter from US Vice President JD Vance but has not yet formally responded; Trump downplayed the letter's significance to reporters and denied that active peace talks are underway, even as his administration continues signaling that any major military or diplomatic move likely waits until after the November 3 midterms. Elsewhere in the region, Saudi Arabia intercepted a Houthi missile, and Iran again warned Israel against resuming strikes.

🌟 Top Story: Volt Typhoon-Linked Group Exploits Maximum-Severity Cisco SD-WAN Flaw to Steal Encryption Keys CVSS 10.0 · UAT-11473 · "KEYDRIFT" MALWARE

Cisco Talos disclosed that a state-linked advanced persistent threat group it tracks as UAT-11473 exploited CVE-2026-20127, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) authentication-bypass flaw in Catalyst SD-WAN Controller, Manager and Validator, to plant a custom malware framework called KeyDrift. The vulnerability sits in the peering authentication of SD-WAN controllers, letting a remote attacker send crafted requests to log in as a high-privileged internal user with no password — opening access to NETCONF and full control over network configuration. Once inside, KeyDrift sits quietly in the control plane and harvests AES-256 encryption keys protecting inter-site traffic, decrypts network messages, and doubles as a backdoor; a companion component called LittleFilch targets the vdaemon process and exfiltrates collected data encrypted with RSA. Talos found strong infrastructure overlaps between this campaign and Volt Typhoon's covert relay network, and named space and satellite services, defense and aerospace firms, and policy research organizations among confirmed targets. Separately, Cisco also patched a actively exploited ASA/FTD SSL VPN zero-day (CVE-2026-76418) used for denial-of-service attacks.

A CVSS 10.0 authentication bypass in core network infrastructure, exploited by a group with Volt Typhoon ties, targeting defense/aerospace and critical infrastructure sectors: patch Catalyst SD-WAN controllers immediately and treat any unexplained NETCONF configuration changes as a potential compromise indicator, not routine drift.

🌟 Top Story: Anthropic Says an Unreleased Claude Model Built Five Working Exploit Chains, Including a Zero-Click Linux Root RESPONSIBLY DISCLOSED · ZERO-CLICK LINUX ROOT, ONE-CLICK CHROME

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team disclosed that an unreleased Claude research model independently chained known and newly identified vulnerabilities into five working, cross-platform exploit chains against Chrome, Safari, Android, Windows and Linux. The most serious is a zero-click remote-root exploit against Linux servers running a multipath TCP listener: it requires no login and no user action, first leaking a kernel pointer to defeat KASLR, then reusing a race-condition bug to overwrite a callback pointer and execute a shell command. A separate one-click Chrome chain strung together four bugs — memory corruption in the V8 JavaScript engine, a sandbox escape that defeated ASLR and hijacked the renderer process, a CacheStorage race condition that leaked the browser process's memory layout, and a use-after-free in the FedCM sign-in feature to complete the escape. The model also chained exploits against Safari, Android and Windows. Anthropic says every vulnerability was reported to the relevant vendor first and fixes have been released; these were controlled lab tests, not real-world incidents, but the model was never specifically trained for offensive security research. Separately, Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Carbon, a cyber-defense-specialized model restricted to vetted security defenders rather than general release, which narrowly edged out Anthropic and OpenAI models by a single point on a newly overhauled frontier benchmark. OpenAI and Anthropic also publicly diverged this week over who should control the "kill switch" for risky AI systems — OpenAI argues a single kill switch doesn't fit how differently AI systems are deployed — while OpenAI separately faces a wrongful-death lawsuit alleging ChatGPT's medical advice contributed to a user's death.
