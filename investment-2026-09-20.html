🪖 Iran War — Regional Conflict Grinds On, Oil Stays Volatile

The ceasefire cycle that briefly calmed markets in April and May has broken down repeatedly since. As of early September, Trump declared an earlier truce over and revoked Iran's license to sell oil globally; Tehran's Foreign Minister Araghchi has publicly derided the new sanctions. Brent has swung between roughly $70 and $118 over the year, and WTI touched a September high near $109 before easing back toward $95-100 this week as fresh Saudi-Houthi fighting complicated the picture rather than resolving it. The Strait of Hormuz remains a persistent chokepoint risk.

Markets have grown somewhat desensitized to the headline risk — VIX sits at 14.8, near its calmest level of the year — but diesel prices hit a record $6.29/gallon this week (+68% YoY), a reminder the energy shock is still working through the real economy even as equities shrug it off.

📉 Macro Backdrop: Goldman Raises Targets, Flags September Seasonality

Goldman Sachs Research lifted its S&P 500 year-end target to 8,000 (from 7,600), anchored on a raised 2026 EPS forecast of $340 (+24% YoY) with AI infrastructure investment accounting for roughly half of that earnings growth. Goldman Sachs Asset Management remains overweight equities, expecting 2026 EPS growth of 31% in the US. The bank simultaneously flagged that September is historically the S&P's worst month (CFRA: -0.6% average), amplified this year by midterm-election-cycle volatility patterns and a VIX that has drifted above 16 at times this month.

The Fed's first rate hike in three years landed this week, yet stocks rallied Friday as softer oil and strong jobs data eased broader rate-hike anxiety — an unusual "hike and rally" dynamic. The 10-year Treasury yield is hovering just under 5%, a level that continues to pressure rate-sensitive valuations even as growth data holds up.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cash Pile Pushes Past $400B — "We Aren't in It to Make 5-6%"

Berkshire's cash and T-bill hoard, already a record $397.4B at Q1, is now believed to have crossed $400B under CEO Greg Abel, who has extended Buffett's multi-year selling pattern (Apple trimmed from ~50% to ~22% of the equity book; Bank of America cut by more than half). Buffett has explained the reserve directly: minor market drops of 5-6% aren't enough to deploy it. Buybacks resumed in March after a 21-month pause, but only at a token $234M — signaling Berkshire still sees the broad market as fully valued, even as UBS flags BRK as a defensive outperformer amid elevated geopolitical risk.

Goldman Sachs Research — September 2026 Outlook

S&P 500 Year-End Target Raised to 8,000 — AI Earnings Power Underestimated

Goldman lifted its year-end S&P target from 7,600 to 8,000, anchored on a raised 2026 EPS forecast of $340 (+24% YoY), with AI infrastructure investment driving roughly half that growth. Goldman Sachs Asset Management remains overweight equities, expecting 2026 EPS growth of 31% in the US, 17% in Europe, and over 70% in EM. The caution: September's well-documented seasonal weakness is being amplified this year by midterm-election-cycle volatility patterns, and the VIX has drifted above 16 at points this month even as it settled near 14.8 on Friday.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Debt "Capital War" Warning — Yet Still Buying US Tech

Dalio continues to warn that US government borrowing needs (a ~$2T 2026 deficit, ~$1T in annual interest costs, ~$10T in maturing debt to refinance) are outpacing investor appetite, and that a dovish-leaning new Fed chair pushing rates down risks inflating further bubbles even as it supports near-term prices. Notably, despite the gloom, Bridgewater has been adding aggressively to US AI names — recent 13F filings show large additions to Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon. The practical takeaway: Dalio is hedging the macro tail risk (dollar, gold) while staying invested in quality AI compounders — a barbell this brief's picks largely mirror.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

AI Is Structural, Not a Fad — But Selectivity Still Matters

In his latest memo, Marks argues AI has moved from abstraction to economic force "at a speed that approaches instantaneous," making it a structural development investors must grapple with rather than dismiss as hype. He pairs this with continued caution on the US fiscal position, describing America's "golden credit card" — cheap borrowing on global trust — as increasingly strained by debt issuance colliding with buyer capacity limits. His prescription remains unchanged: favor income-generating, reasonably-valued quality over high-beta names trading at extreme multiples — the framework behind this brief's JNJ/XOM/NEE/JPM income sleeve.
