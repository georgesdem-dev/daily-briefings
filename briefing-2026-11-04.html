🌟 Top Story: ARTEX AI Agents Now Automate Full Active Directory Attacks, Including Kerberoasting 200+ ORGS, 14+ COUNTRIES SINCE AUGUST

New reporting details how attackers are using ARTEX — the Go-based, multi-agent penetration-testing and command-and-control framework behind last week's WorkOS breach — to fully automate Windows domain intrusions. The framework splits an attack across six autonomous sub-agents that independently enumerate usernames, list managed service accounts, review Active Directory Certificate Services templates, and execute Kerberoasting to harvest service-account credentials, all without a human operator directing each step. In one documented case, ARTEX pulled more than 110,000 Active Directory objects and read the contents of the Domain Admins group unattended. Researchers say ARTEX activity has been observed since late August 2026, with one controller alone linked to targeting of more than 200 organizations across at least 14 countries, and confirmed post-exploitation at nine of them. The technique mirrors known Kerberoasting methods (including the "Ghost SPN" variant), but the shift is architectural, not technical: a six-agent pipeline now performs reconnaissance-through-credential-theft end to end, collapsing work that used to require a skilled human operator into an unattended pipeline — the same commoditization pattern already seen in ARTEX's use by Chinese and North Korean state-linked groups and in the WorkOS breach.

ARTEX has now been tied to cloud-sales-tool breaches (WorkOS), nation-state espionage, and now full on-premises Active Directory compromise in the span of under two weeks. Any organization still relying on detecting a human operator's pacing or mistakes to catch an intrusion should assume that signal is gone against this tooling.
