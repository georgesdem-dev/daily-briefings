🔥 Huang Reinstated — The AI Trade's Biggest Single-Day Reversal in Years

Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as President and CEO effective immediately on Monday, less than two weeks after his resignation had helped trigger the prior week's AI-capex-fear selloff. The stock closed +31.48% at $165.05, adding roughly $950 billion in market value in a single session — among the largest one-day market-cap gains ever recorded for any company. The move instantly reversed the market's core anxiety: that AI infrastructure spending had lost its steadiest hand. Chip and AI-adjacent names followed Nvidia higher across the board — AMD closed +6.85% at $453.46, and semiconductor peers broadly outperformed.

The index-level reaction was the mirror image of the prior week's rout: S&P 500 +4.73% (best session since April 2025), Nasdaq +6.51%, Dow +3.01%. VIX collapsed -25.83% to 26.62 as hedges unwound at speed — still elevated versus the ~17-20 "calm market" range, signaling traders aren't fully convinced the volatility regime has ended even as they bought the rally.

📉 What Didn't Reverse: Gold and Oil

Gold rose a further +3.4% to roughly $3,634 even as equities staged their sharpest rally in over a year — an unusual combination that suggests some institutional positioning (inflation hedges, dollar diversification) didn't unwind alongside the equity-specific AI-fear trade. Oil continued lower to $80.17/bbl, consistent with still-soft global demand signals that the one-day equity rally didn't change. The 10-year Treasury yield held flat at 4.92%, suggesting bond markets are taking a wait-and-see approach rather than fully re-pricing for a return to risk-on conditions.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs Macro Desk

Semiconductors "Clearly Oversold" — Called It Before Monday

Goldman analyst James Schneider had flagged NVDA, AVGO, and AMD as being "clearly in oversold territory" with tech-heavy indices sitting at valuations not seen in over a decade — a call that looks prescient after Monday's reversal. Goldman's broader point stands even after the rally: positioning had become so one-sided bearish on AI-capex fears that a relief rally was a when, not an if, question. The reinstatement of Huang was the trigger, but the oversold setup was already in place.

JPMorgan Equity Strategy

Positioning Unwind Drove Monday's Scale of Move

JPMorgan's strategy desk noted that weak investor sentiment and oversold technical indicators created an attractive risk-reward setup heading into Monday — and that the speed and size of hedge unwinds (reflected in VIX's -25.8% collapse) amplified what was already a fundamentally positive catalyst. JPMorgan's own CFRA-rated Buy thesis on JPM itself ($375 target) is framed around steady US economic expansion continuing regardless of the AI-news cycle.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

One Day Doesn't Resolve the Underlying Debate

Marks' standing view from last week — that current conditions reflect a valuation reset rather than a classic bubble bursting — gets a partial vindication from Monday's rally, but his caution hasn't changed: a single catalyst-driven +31% move in one stock doesn't retroactively validate the AI-capex spending levels that triggered the selloff in the first place. His prescription remains quality, income, and reasonable valuations over chasing momentum — which is why this brief still anchors on JPM/JNJ/HII alongside the AI-chip re-entry.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Record Cash Still Sitting on the Sidelines

No new public commentary from Buffett since the correction began, but Berkshire's large cash position remains unchanged and uncommitted as of last report — consistent with the thesis that Buffett sees single-stock-driven rallies like Monday's as noise relative to broader market valuation, not a signal to change course.
