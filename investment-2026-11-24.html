🔥 The Story of the Day: Jensen Huang Returns to NVIDIA — $1 Trillion Added in One Session

NVIDIA's board voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, reversing a contentious board ouster that had rattled investor confidence in the company's AI leadership. The market's reaction was immediate and seismic: NVIDIA stock surged +31.48% in a single session, adding approximately $900 billion to $1 trillion in market capitalization. This triggered a cascade across the AI supply chain — semiconductors, data centers, power utilities, and AI-adjacent software all followed sharply higher.

Wall Street scrambled to issue upgraded price targets. NVIDIA had been down more than 30% from its $243 peak in mid-November, closing at around $125–130 before the news. The stock closed Nov 23 at $165.05, with analyst 1-year consensus now revised to $244.55 — implying a further +48% from here. Huang's return effectively signals that the AI infrastructure buildout, Blackwell GPU roadmap, and CUDA ecosystem are all intact and accelerating under his leadership.

Important context: The past week (Nov 17–21) had been a bruising correction driven by the Fed's hawkish pivot, the Anthropic IPO disappointment, an AMD short-seller report on China exposure, and post-midterm election uncertainty. The S&P 500 had touched 6,910 on Nov 20 — down ~8% from peaks. Yesterday's explosive recovery suggests the correction was a capitulation event, not the start of a bear market.

🏛️ Macro Backdrop: Post-Midterm Uncertainty + Fed December 9 Risk

Democrats won the November 2026 midterm elections in what analysts are calling a "blue wave" result — adding policy uncertainty around AI regulation, drug pricing, defense spending, and corporate taxes. Historical patterns from similar midterm cycles show that markets typically sell off into the election, then stage strong recoveries in the subsequent 3–6 months as the presidential cycle third year (2027) tends to be the strongest for equities.

The Federal Reserve had been cutting rates since September 2024 — 1.45 percentage points of cuts (from 5.33% to 3.88%). But paradoxically, long-term rates rose during this cycle. NY Fed President John Williams recently signaled that rates may need to go higher "over time" to address rebounding inflation. The next Fed meeting is December 9, 2026 — just two weeks away. Bill Ackman is publicly warning the Fed not to hike, citing collapsing credit conditions (SpaceX just borrowed at 8.26% secured yield). A December hike would be a major negative catalyst for equities.

Thanksgiving week (US holiday Thursday Nov 26) means thin trading volumes Wednesday through Friday. Markets tend to drift higher in holiday-shortened weeks, but any macro shock (surprise rate signal, geopolitical event) would be amplified by low liquidity.

Bill Ackman — Pershing Square Capital Management

"The Fed is About to Make Another Mistake" — Don't Hike on December 9

In a widely-circulated post on November 22, Ackman issued an urgent warning to the Federal Reserve: "Financial conditions have tightened massively." His evidence: SpaceX just raised $25.2 billion in secured debt at 8.259% yield — over 2 percentage points above the 6% average for investment-grade unsecured bonds. This signals that real credit markets are already breaking under the strain of elevated rates, even as official Fed funds sit at 3.88%. Ackman also challenged the 2% PCE inflation target as "misguided" given deglobalization, tariffs, defense spending, and AI energy infrastructure costs. He advocates for a 3% inflation target, warning that forcing inflation to 2% risks triggering a U.S. and global recession. For investors: a December 9 rate hike would be a severe shock to high-multiple equities, REITs, and telcos. A pause or hold would be powerfully bullish.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

$325B+ Cash Pile — the Question Is When, Not Whether, Buffett Deploys

No new direct Buffett statement this week, but Berkshire's stock is an interesting case study: BRK-B had been viewed as the premier "anti-AI trade" — a safe harbor as tech corrected last week. The stock held near its 52-week high of $596.92 even as the market sold off -8%. Now, with valuations having come down sharply and Jensen Huang's return resetting the AI cycle, at least one sell-side analyst cautioned that the "anti-AI trade is now crowded" in Berkshire. Buffett's $325B+ cash pile (the largest in history — roughly one-third of market cap) remains the central question. This level of cash signals Buffett sees equities as still richly valued even after last week's dip, OR he is staging the largest acquisition in history. Either way, BRK-B at $570–$575 is a stable value anchor for a moderate portfolio.

Ruchir Sharma — Breakout Capital / CNBC

"As Long as Interest Rates Go Up, the AI Trade is Still at Risk"

Sharma, the globally followed macro investor and former Morgan Stanley EM head, issued a succinct but important warning on CNBC this week: the structural bull case for AI stocks is fundamentally premised on a benign (or declining) rate environment. When the 10-year Treasury yields 4.94% and the 30-year yields 5.29%, the discount rate for long-duration growth assets rises — compressing valuations on 100x+ earnings multiples. The AI trade (NVDA, cloud, semiconductor ecosystem) has demonstrated extraordinary volatility: NVDA fell from $243 to $125 and rebounded to $165 in just 10 days. Sharma's warning is a reminder that even yesterday's euphoric recovery has not resolved the underlying tension: the bond market is not celebrating the AI recovery.
