📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🚀 Monday: The Best Day Since April — And a Governance Shock at Nvidia

Markets staged a powerful relief rally Monday Nov 9: the S&P 500 jumped +2.35% to 7,549.94 (its best day since April 8), the Nasdaq surged +2.74% to 25,253.14, and the Dow rose +2.15% to 52,389.75. Every S&P sector finished higher, led by Industrials and Technology, as beaten-down chip and AI names found aggressive buyers following the prior week's correction. The VIX fell -22.3% to 19.92 — its first close under 20 since the selloff began — and crude oil slid -4.4% to $86.85 (Brent -5.9% to $92.57) after President Trump downplayed the odds of renewed strikes on Iran, draining the war-risk premium from oil.

The rally had an asterisk: Nvidia fell -4.27% to $178.67 after its board announced an independent investigation into reports that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence operation. Huang reportedly told employees he had been unfaithful to his wife; US intelligence assessed that China's Ministry of State Security had recruited a Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang, to gather information on him. Nvidia's board said it has no reason to believe trade secrets or national security were compromised and expressed confidence in Huang's leadership, but three US House committee chairs have requested Huang attend a closed-door briefing on November 18.

🌡️ Tuesday: Hot CPI Hits the Brakes

The rally stalled Tuesday Nov 10 as the October CPI report came in hotter than expected — headline inflation rose +0.4% month-over-month versus the +0.3% consensus, and core CPI rose +0.3% versus +0.2% expected. The S&P 500 slipped -0.38% to 7,520.93, the Nasdaq fell -0.74% to 25,065.87, and the Dow eased -0.30% to 52,231.89. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped roughly 10 basis points to near 4.99% as CME FedWatch odds moved to ~88% for a 25bp December hike (and 11% for a 50bp move); Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he'd support a 25bp hike in December even with some inflation improvement, and would consider 50bp if core CPI ran at 0.3%+. Nvidia extended its slide, falling a further ~5.5% as the governance story continued to weigh on sentiment.

📈 Broad Monday Rally, Narrower Tuesday Pullback

Monday's advance was unusually broad — every one of the 11 S&P sectors closed higher, with Industrials and Technology leading as the chip-stock rebound powered a market-wide bid. Tuesday's hot-CPI pullback was comparatively contained: the Nasdaq (-0.74%) underperformed the Dow (-0.30%) as rate-sensitive growth names bore the brunt of the higher-yield reaction, while Nvidia's governance overhang kept Technology among the weaker sectors both days relative to its Monday gain.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Concentration Risk Thesis Gets a Real-World Stress Test

Dalio's recent warning — that roughly 26 cents of every dollar in a broad S&P 500 fund now rides on six AI-tied mega-caps — reads differently after a week where a single-company governance scandal (Nvidia) moved the tape. His point isn't that AI is necessarily a bubble, but that whether or not it is, the market's concentration in a handful of names amplifies any idiosyncratic shock into a systemic one.

Fed Commentary — Christopher Waller

Hawkish Signal Even Before the Hot CPI Print

Fed Governor Waller said this week he'd support a 25bp December hike even if October inflation data improved — and would consider 50bp if core CPI ran at 0.3% or higher. Core CPI then came in at exactly that threshold, pushing CME-implied odds of a hike to ~88% for 25bp and 11% for 50bp. This is a more hawkish-leaning Fed commentary cycle than markets were pricing just two weeks ago.
