🌟 Top Story: Hormuz Attacks at Record Levels; IRGC Claims Drone Kill and Bahrain Strikes, Manama Disputes the Target 22+ ATTACKS THIS MONTH · DRONE SHOOTDOWN CLAIMED

Maritime tracking data shows attacks on shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz have reached their highest levels since the war began, as Tehran presses to reassert control over the waterway amid falling oil revenue. At least 22 attacks have been recorded in November alone — the second-highest monthly total of the war — including roughly 25 tanker incidents since November 14 and three tankers struck by projectiles exiting the strait on Friday and Saturday. On Sunday, Iran's IRGC claimed its air defenses shot down a US MQ-1 Predator drone east of the strait (Washington has not confirmed), and separately said its aerospace force struck two US Patriot radar systems at the Al Riffa and Awali bases in Bahrain, calling it retaliation for alleged US MQ-1/MQ-9 airspace violations and a strike on a radar site on Hengam Island. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry rejected that account, saying the strike actually hit a civilian industrial plant and left two people injured, one a Pakistani national in critical condition. Oil markets whipsawed on the news — Brent slipped 0.8% to $91.85/barrel and WTI rose 0.9% to $83.98 — well below the brief spike above $100 earlier in the war but still roughly $15 above pre-war levels, with tanker war-risk insurance premiums up to 3.5–7.5% from a pre-war baseline of 0.10–0.25%. Diplomatically, Oman continues negotiating with Tehran over a Hormuz management arrangement compliant with international law and is resisting US pressure to cut ties with Iran, while Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have restricted US access to bases on their soil over escalation concerns. Iran says it could reopen Hormuz within two days if Washington halts hostilities, lifts its naval blockade, and waives sanctions. The diplomatic backdrop is unchanged from Friday: the Senate blocked a war-powers resolution 51–48 on November 19, Trump has told Axios he won't decide on next steps until the new year, and incoming Democratic majorities have pledged to make ending the war a priority when the new Congress convenes January 3.

Cybersecurity
Sunday, November 22, 2026
🌟 Top Story: Amazon Says China-Linked AI Agents Found and Exploited a Medical-Imaging Zero-Day in 11 Minutes CVSS 9.8 · ROOT IN ~11 MINUTES

Amazon Threat Intelligence disclosed that a China-linked threat actor used fully autonomous AI agents, running the open-source Cairn framework, to discover and weaponize a previously unknown vulnerability in MONAI Label — an open-source server for AI-assisted annotation of medical images — without a human operator directing each step. The agents mapped the application's API and planted a reverse shell in roughly 11 minutes, exploiting a path-traversal flaw (CVE-2026-133968, CVSS 9.8) in the image parameter of the PUT /datastore/image endpoint affecting MONAI Label through version 0.8.5, ultimately gaining root on an East Asian university server. Amazon attributes the campaign to China with moderate confidence, citing Simplified Chinese prompts and China Standard Time settings in the agent logs. It is among the first publicly documented cases of AI agents autonomously finding and exploiting a zero-day end to end. The disclosure lands amid continued fallout from the VMware vCenter zero-day (CVE-2026-123890, CVSS 9.8) exploited by China-nexus group ARMORY CASTLE since September and patched November 19 — no public fix yet exists for vCenter 7.0. Three more critical RCEs joined the active-exploitation list this week: an unauthenticated root RCE in Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466, CVSS 9.8, no workaround, patch to X15.5.20), an XML-RPC deserialization RCE in Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448, CVSS 9.8, versions 4.0.0–6.1.4), and a Puppet Server deserialization RCE (CVE-2026-115731, CVSS 9.4) that lets any CA-signed client steal the CA key and run code server-side — open-source Puppet 6.24.0 and earlier will not be patched, and the federal KEV remediation deadline is December 1.

🌟 Top Story: Amazon Nears $7.5B Decart Deal as OpenRouter Strips 125 Endpoints of "Zero Data Retention" Status $7.5B DEAL · 125 ENDPOINTS LOSE ZDR

Amazon is in advanced talks — a deal could close within a week — to acquire the technology and team behind AI startup Decart for about $7.5 billion, buying its "Decart Optimization Stack" (software that helps AI models run efficiently across varied hardware) rather than Decart's models. Decart's founders, who own roughly 64% of the company, have committed to stay at Amazon five years and could collectively receive about $4.8 billion; Anthropic had been negotiating to buy Decart for around $6 billion before walking away in September, and Decart was valued near $4 billion in a May funding round — nearly double that valuation in this deal. Separately, OpenRouter disclosed it off-boarded three AI inference providers and removed 125 endpoints from Zero Data Retention (ZDR) eligibility after security firm Dragos ran a canary test — submitting roughly two million prompts containing unique URLs and canary credentials to widely used AI services from July through November — and found hackers, marketplace resellers, and a Chinese AI lab accessing prompts that were supposedly flagged ZDR and never stored. OpenRouter says the incidents sit with downstream providers, not a breach of its own routing service, and it will require renewed assurances before restoring ZDR eligibility. On products, OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol — Astra for professional/technical work, Sol for everyday tasks — with API pricing cut 20% versus GPT-6 Astra, a 22-point jump on OpenAI's internal cyber-exploit benchmark, and tighter safeguards on higher-risk cybersecurity prompts. Also notable: autonomous AI agents (see Cybersecurity, above) independently found and exploited a medical-imaging zero-day in 11 minutes; an FDA regulatory gap was flagged around RhoAgent, an LLM-plus-diffusion system that autonomously designs RNA drug candidates; and Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports Apple's own computer-operating AI agent likely won't ship before late 2027.
