🌟 Top Story: Maximum-Severity SonicWall SMA1000 Flaw Exploited Days After Patch Release CVE-2026-102255 · CVSS 10.0

SonicWall's SMA1000 secure-access appliances (the 6210, 7210 and 8200v models) are now under active attack via CVE-2026-102255, a pre-authentication SSRF flaw in the Appliance WorkPlace interface that SonicWall patched only on October 6 — exploitation attempts were detected within three days. Researcher Ryan Dewhurst told Bleeping Computer his honeypot network observed crafted OPTIONS requests reaching the appliance's internal CouchDB service on 127.0.0.1:5984, attempting to traverse into a CouchDB design document and invoke its _rewrite function while supplying default admin:admin credentials — a textbook SSRF-to-internal-service pivot. This is SonicWall's third pre-auth SSRF disclosed in 2026 alone, and CISA has now added 19 SonicWall vulnerabilities to its KEV catalog over four years, 13 of them confirmed used by ransomware operators — prompting one analysis to call the pattern "not another one-off bug" but a structural weakness in the product line. Meanwhile last week's NetScaler SAML flaw (CVE-2026-88779) and the still-unpatched FortiMail file-write bug (CVE-2026-104286, CVSS 9.8) remain active risks, keeping edge-appliance exploitation the dominant cybersecurity theme of the month.

SonicWall SMA1000 admins: patch CVE-2026-102255 immediately and treat any internet-facing WorkPlace interface as potentially already probed — the exploitation window opened within 72 hours of the fix. If you run SMA1000 with default or weak internal service credentials, rotate them now.
