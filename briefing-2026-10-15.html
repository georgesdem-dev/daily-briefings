🌟 Top Story: Record Week of Hormuz Attacks as Iran Ramps Up Pressure Ahead of US Midterms 10+ SHIPS STRUCK SINCE OCT 11

The Strait of Hormuz saw its highest concentration of ship attacks of the war this week. The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride caught fire and sank Tuesday after being struck while transiting the strait; all crew were evacuated safely. CENTCOM said the vessel was hit by an Iranian projectile, directly contradicting the IRGC's own claim that the ship struck a naval mine on an "unauthorized route." A second vessel, the Antigua and Barbuda-flagged Cape Dao, also sank this week, weeks after being struck on September 23 — underscoring how damaged ships are increasingly going down well after the initial strike. At least 10 ships have now been struck in and around the strait since October 11 alone, the highest total recorded since the war began in late February. A UK maritime monitor warned that vessels transiting the strait continue to face "indiscriminate attacks" regardless of flag or cargo, and analysts increasingly frame Iran's intensified campaign as timed to keep oil prices elevated through the US midterm elections. Houthi forces separately warned that "Saudi Arabia's airspace and airports are unsafe," keeping the Yemen front active in parallel with the Hormuz escalation.

🌟 Top Story: Chinese Espionage Group Exploited BeyondTrust Zero-Day for Seven Weeks Before Disclosure CVE-2026-102764 · CVSS 9.3

BeyondTrust disclosed CVE-2026-102764, a critical flaw in its Remote Support product that allows an unauthenticated remote attacker to perform arbitrary file operations on a vulnerable appliance. Google's Threat Intelligence group, publishing jointly with the disclosure, says the flaw was exploited as a zero-day starting August 29 — nearly seven weeks before today's patch — by a suspected PRC-nexus espionage actor it tracks as "Armory Castle." Google attributes subsequent deployment of two previously undocumented malware families, dubbed SAWFLY and BARECLAW, to the same actor. The long undetected dwell time is the headline risk here: organizations running BeyondTrust Remote Support should assume compromise is possible for any instance exposed since late August, not just from today's patch date forward. Separately, a public proof-of-concept was released for a critical Fortinet FortiClient EMS authentication and authorization bypass (CVE-2026-35616), raising the urgency for FortiClient EMS admins to patch before mass exploitation attempts begin. Both stories land in the same week as CISA's October 16 remediation deadline for the actively exploited Microsoft Windows Shell zero-day (CVE-2026-96951) and continuing fallout from Ivanti Connect Secure's unpatched privilege-escalation component (CVE-2026-104935).

If you run BeyondTrust Remote Support, treat any instance internet-reachable since late August as potentially compromised — hunt for SAWFLY/BARECLAW indicators rather than relying on today's patch alone to close the exposure window.
