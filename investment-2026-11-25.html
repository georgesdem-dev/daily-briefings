🎢 The AI Trade's Whiplash: Monday's Rally Gives Back Ground

One day after Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO triggered the best market session since April 2025, Tuesday brought a sharp reversal in the same names. NVDA closed -5.86% at $155.38, giving back roughly $240 billion of Monday's ~$950 billion gain — despite fresh bullish analyst action (StoneX's Cody Acree upgraded to Buy with a $270 target; Oppenheimer's Rick Schafer upgraded to Buy at $250, both signaling ~29%+ upside from current levels). AMD fell further, -8.86% to $413.27, compounded by a 337-million-share resale filing that added fresh supply overhang on top of profit-taking.

The net read: the re-rating thesis from Monday hasn't been invalidated — if anything, sell-side upgrades are still arriving — but the stocks are now trading with significant two-way volatility as the market digests both the leadership news and near-term technical/supply pressure. VIX holding roughly flat at 26.36 (rather than falling further) suggests traders are keeping hedges on through this chop rather than declaring the episode over.

🏛️ Dow 50,000 — A Milestone Driven by Rotation, Not Broad Strength

The Dow's first-ever close above 50,000 is notable, but it reflects a value/defensive rotation rather than broad market strength — the S&P and Nasdaq both fell the same day. Dollar Tree's Q3 beat (EPS $1.16 vs. estimates, comp sales +4.6% on a 3.8% traffic increase) reinforced the value-and-staples leg of the rotation; the healthcare M&A deal (Sanofi/Insmed) did the same for defensives. Dell and Best Buy earnings were also due Tuesday, with Dell carrying a record $95B AI server backlog into the print — a read-through worth checking against actual results before trading on it.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Sell-Side Analyst Action (StoneX, Oppenheimer)

Fresh Upgrades Arrive Even as NVDA Pulls Back

Notably, two fresh analyst upgrades on NVDA landed Tuesday — StoneX's Cody Acree to Buy with a $270 target, and Oppenheimer's Rick Schafer to Buy at $250 — even as the stock fell 5.86% intraday to end-of-day. That combination (price down, targets up) is a useful signal: the pullback looks more like profit-taking and positioning unwind after Monday's 31% spike than a reassessment of the underlying Huang-reinstatement thesis.

William Blair

AMD Upgraded Despite the Resale-Filing Overhang

William Blair upgraded AMD to Outperform the same day a 337-million-share resale filing hit the stock for an 8.86% decline — again suggesting the sell-side view on fundamentals is diverging from the day's technical/supply-driven price action. Worth distinguishing: a resale filing is existing shareholders selling, not new dilution from the company, so the long-term share count impact is different from a capital raise.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Two-Way Volatility Is the Market Working, Not Breaking

Marks' standing framework — that this is a valuation reset rather than a bubble bursting — gets further support from Tuesday's pattern: a sharp move up, a sharp move back, analyst targets holding or rising through both. That's consistent with a market still searching for a fair price on AI infrastructure spend, not one in freefall. His prescription is unchanged: quality, income, and reasonable valuations as the stable core while the AI-chip names find their level.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Still No New Commentary — Cash Position Unchanged

No fresh public statement since the correction began. Berkshire's large cash position remains the backdrop: a single volatile week in AI-chip names is the kind of noise Buffett has historically treated as irrelevant to a multi-year capital allocation decision.
