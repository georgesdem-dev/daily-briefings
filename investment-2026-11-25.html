💥 Nvidia Crisis — The Governance Storm That Won't Quit

Nvidia's tumultuous month continues. The stock has lost approximately $2.75 trillion in market cap over the past 30 days, falling ~43% from an October peak of ~$222 to a November 20 low of ~$125. Yesterday (Monday), shares surged +31% when the board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, president, and board director after his brief removal. Today, the market recalibrated, taking the stock -5.97% to around $155.

The governance crisis involves multiple overlapping issues: (1) SEC investigation into the company's accounting practices; (2) a California subpoena related to alleged chip sales to China-linked entities; (3) board concerns over Huang's undisclosed relationship with a Singapore-based executive classified by US authorities as a Chinese intelligence asset. While the board reinstated Huang — implying confidence in his ability to manage through — the legal overhangs remain live. Goldman Sachs AI spending thesis depends heavily on Nvidia maintaining its dominant GPU market position; any forced restructuring or regulatory action would shatter that framework.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (-7.88%) reflects the contagion: Micron (-11.28%), Analog Devices (-17.70%), HPE (-17.16%) all hit hard. Morgan Stanley's sector strategist warned that semiconductor stocks could decline another 10-15% from prior levels before finding support.

📉 Macro Backdrop: Tariffs Return, Fed Hike Back on Table, 10Y at 4.9%

Three macro headwinds materialized simultaneously today, explaining much of the tech and growth stock weakness. First: reports that President Trump will announce fresh import tariffs targeting China and dozens of other countries, echoing "Liberation Day" but with sharper focus on semiconductor supply chains and AI hardware — directly threatening chip stocks. Second: Fed rate cut expectations have fully reversed. Markets are now pricing a 95% probability of a December rate hike, with the 10-year Treasury yield climbing to 4.908% — its highest level since October. At this yield level, tech stocks with high forward P/E ratios (Nvidia, Palantir) face significant multiple compression. Third: Goldman Sachs warned that AI-related investment now accounts for nearly half of S&P 500 EPS growth in 2026, creating dangerous concentration — a slowdown in hyperscaler capex spending would disproportionately hurt earnings.

The divergence is stark: Berkshire Hathaway's Greg Abel is sitting on ~$300B+ in cash/T-bills, quietly building dry powder as the Shiller CAPE ratio exceeds 40 — a level last seen at the dot-com peak. Buffett's most recent public statements issued his "starkest warning about the stock market in years," while maintaining his core philosophy: stay invested in productive businesses, but do not engage in speculation at any price.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"That's Not Investing, That's Gambling" — Buffett's Starkest Warning in Years

Warren Buffett has issued what analysts describe as his most direct market warning in years, stating that some investors are "not speculating — they're gambling" at current US stock valuations. The Shiller CAPE ratio has exceeded 40, approaching levels last seen at the dot-com bubble peak of 2000. Berkshire is now holding approximately $300B+ in cash and T-bills — nearly one-third of its market cap. Meanwhile, Greg Abel (CEO) reportedly evaluated but passed on multiple major acquisition targets this year, suggesting nothing is sufficiently cheap. Buffett's core message remains unchanged: stay invested in productive businesses, never market-time, but do not pay any price for any company. His most recent counsel: "If a stock market crash is coming, the best move investors can make is to do nothing" — meaning those with quality holdings should sit tight and those without cash should not panic-sell. Read: at Dow 50,000, Buffett is cautious but not bearish.

Goldman Sachs — November 2026 Market Pulse

S&P 500 Target: 8,000 — But AI Becoming a Risk, Not Just a Tailwind

Goldman Sachs maintains its 8,000 year-end S&P target — about 11% above current levels — but its tone has shifted materially. The core concern: AI-related investment now accounts for nearly half of the S&P 500's projected EPS growth in 2026. Goldman warns that "AI investment could shift from supporting earnings growth in 2026 to becoming a drag by 2028" if hyperscalers' capex cannot translate into measurable productivity or revenue gains fast enough. The firm still likes technology and AI infrastructure as overweights, but is increasingly advocating for global diversification and active stock selection rather than passive AI exposure. Key call: broadening earnings across sectors is essential — a market rising only on AI concentration is fragile. Goldman's preferred sectors for Q4 2026 → Q1 2027: Healthcare (echoing RBC's upgrade), Energy, and international equities.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"AI Is the Most Consequential and Least Predictable Development for Investors"

Howard Marks cited Warren Buffett's 1979 warning — when investors were fleeing stocks despite attractive valuations — as the perfect mirror lesson for today's AI boom. His conclusion: today's investors are doing the opposite, rushing into AI at valuations that assume everything goes right forever. Marks described AI as "the most consequential and least predictable development for investors in at least a generation" and recommended "calibrated humility regarding strategy selection, position sizing, and risk-taking." His practical advice: approach AI exposure with smaller position sizes than your conviction suggests, maintain larger cash or income allocations than you think necessary, and do not confuse the importance of a technology with the certainty of its near-term earnings trajectory. This brief's moderate-risk selections are designed to reflect Marks' framework — quality businesses, income, and defensible valuations.

JPMorgan Markets Research

"Quality Over AI Hype" — Diversify Away from Semiconductor Concentration

JPMorgan forecasts 30% global earnings growth in 2026 and 20% in 2027, underpinning its 8,000 S&P target. But its tactical November message is unambiguous: quality businesses over indiscriminate AI exposure. JPMorgan is growing concerned that hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) are accessing debt markets to fund AI capex as spending exceeds cash flow — a sign that the cycle is maturing. The firm favors defensives, dividend payers, and international equities over US mega-cap tech in the near term. Specifically citing today's action, JPMorgan's sector desk upgraded Healthcare to reflect the defensive rotation they'd been anticipating since the Nvidia crisis escalated in October. Their call: "own the businesses where free cash flow is real and returns to shareholders are contractual."

Morgan Stanley — Most Bearish Near-Term View

7,100–7,200 Is Key Support; Semiconductor Stocks Could Fall Another 10–15%

Morgan Stanley's chief equity strategist is the most cautious of the major banks. The team identifies 7,100–7,200 as the critical S&P 500 support zone — we're at 7,201 today, essentially on the edge. A close below 7,100 would signal broader market deterioration. More alarmingly, 60% of Russell 3000 stocks are already down 20%+ from their June 2026 peaks — a bear market is already happening beneath the headline indices. Semiconductor stocks are warned to potentially decline another 10–15% from prior-week levels before finding fundamental support. Morgan Stanley maintains its 8,000 year-end target, but the trajectory requires a resolution of the Nvidia governance crisis, tariff clarity, and a Fed that does not hike in December. All three are uncertain. For the moderate-risk investor: today is not a day to add tech exposure.
