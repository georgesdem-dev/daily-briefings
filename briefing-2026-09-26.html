Week of September 24–26, 2026
🌟 Top Story: Bitget Loses $351.6M — 2026's Largest Crypto Heist, North Korea Suspected BACKEND COMPROMISE

Crypto exchange Bitget disclosed that attackers stole $351.6 million from its hot and warm wallets on Sept 24, moving funds across Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BNB Chain, and Base. Rather than stealing private keys, the attacker compromised a critical backend system inside Bitget's wallet infrastructure, used it to spoof transaction data, and triggered the authorization process to move funds out. IP-behavior patterns and on-chain analysis point to North Korean state-linked actors — Bitget is working with Mandiant and SlowMist on the investigation. Losses will be covered by Bitget's $464M User Protection Fund, though the theft could drain roughly 76% of it.

🕳️ SalesBleed: Zero-Click Flaws Let Attackers Hijack Salesforce's AI Agents AI AGENT SECURITY

Zenity Labs disclosed three vulnerabilities in Salesforce Agentforce, collectively named SalesBleed. Two enabled zero-click exfiltration of CRM data to attacker-controlled infrastructure via poisoned leads, requiring no employee click or approval. The third let an anonymous insider weaponize the trusted identity of an Agentforce-connected Slack agent to post phishing messages under that agent's name, since the integration didn't reliably attribute which user triggered the action. Salesforce was notified June 1 and confirmed all three issues remediated by Aug 19 — but the case is now a widely cited reference for zero-click risk in enterprise AI-agent deployments.

September 26, 2026 · Industry Self-Regulation Takes Shape
🌟 Top Story: Google, OpenAI, Anthropic Court Sriram Krishnan for a Self-Regulatory "Frontier AI Standards Agency" SELF-REGULATION, NOT GOVERNMENT

Days after their CEOs told the UN Security Council the industry needs global oversight, the three labs are quietly building their own alternative: a voluntary self-regulator — tentatively the Frontier AI Standards Agency, modeled on Wall Street's FINRA — that could launch by year-end or early 2027. They've approached Sriram Krishnan, the former White House AI policy advisor, to be CEO, alongside other candidates including ex-Biden tech official Arati Prabhakar, former diplomat Condoleezza Rice, and VC David Friedberg. The pillars under discussion: shared technical evaluations, pre-release audits, independent testing, and standardized safety protocols — all industry-run, with no government body involved. The irony reporters have noted: Krishnan spent his time in the White House arguing against exactly this kind of centralized oversight, saying there would be "no FDA for AI." OpenAI's Chris Lehane confirmed the three labs have been coordinating on this for several weeks.

Read alongside Wednesday's UN Security Council briefing, this looks like the industry moving to define its own guardrails before governments do — a governance model your organization's AI vendors may soon be judged against instead of, or in addition to, any binding regulation.

🔗 Crossover: SalesBleed Shows Why Agent Governance Matters Now

The SalesBleed flaws in Salesforce Agentforce (see Cybersecurity section) are a live example of the exact risk category the Frontier AI Standards Agency and this week's Dataiku Agent Management launch are both trying to address: autonomous agents with real permissions and trusted identities, operating with weak attribution and audit trails. Expect "who can act as this agent, and can we prove it" to become a standard enterprise AI-governance question over the next few months.
