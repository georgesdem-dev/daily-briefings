📊 Market Snapshot — Friday's Rebound LIVE DATA

💻 It Was a Methodology Mix-Up, Not a Retraction — Tech Rallies Back

Friday's gains fully erased Thursday's AI-revenue-doubt selloff after Bloomberg reported OpenAI expects annualized revenue to reach or exceed $70B by year-end. The real story: Bloomberg clarified that Thursday's scarier Financial Times figure (~$50B run rate) and the newer $70B figure reflect different accounting methodologies between OpenAI and Anthropic for how cloud-provider revenue is treated — not a correction of a false number. Markets took the clarification as reassurance. One wrinkle: NVIDIA (-0.52%) didn't fully participate in the rebound even as the Nasdaq (+0.64%) and MSFT (+2.38%) did — worth watching for whether that's noise or an early signal.

✈️ Correcting Friday: Delta's Earnings Were a Miss, Not a Beat

Our prior report said Delta beat Q3 estimates with EPS of $2.01. Fuller results show the opposite: Delta reported adjusted EPS of $1.72, missing the $1.77 consensus, driven by a 62% surge in fuel costs ($4.14B for the quarter). Management cut full-year 2026 guidance to adjusted EPS of $5.10-5.60, down sharply from a prior $6.50-7.50. The stock fell as much as 4.4% in early trading before recovering to close essentially flat (+0.04%) after the CEO said he sees "no cracks in travel demand." We're flagging the correction for the record — several sell-side desks (Bernstein SocGen, Susquehanna, Wells Fargo, BofA) had already trimmed targets to the $95-106 range ahead of the print while keeping Buy ratings.

🇮🇷 Hormuz Campaign Hits a "Wartime High" — No Weekend De-Escalation

Eleven tankers were struck in the week ended October 4 — the most since the shadow conflict began in late February — with attacks continuing to spread beyond the strait itself (Qatar, Fujairah). Weekend reports (Oct 10) indicate Iran's IRGC claimed a fresh strike on an LPG tanker, suggesting the campaign is intensifying rather than cooling off. Oil-tanker traffic exiting Hormuz is running roughly 30% below normal pre-war levels; Brent fell 1.6% to $102.60 Friday even so, as the broader risk-on tone in equities dominated the session.

💵 Yields Ease Off the Highs — But the Bigger Debate Isn't Settled

The 10-year Treasury eased to roughly 5.23-5.24% Friday, down about 9 basis points from Thursday's 24-year intraday high of 5.357% — consistent with the equity rebound as discount-rate pressure lifted. But Ray Dalio and Howard Marks both pushed back this week on reading too much into any single day's relief: Dalio called it a "bond bear market" with "more to go," while Marks's latest Oaktree memo argues rising long yields are a rational response to sticky inflation, a ~6%-of-GDP peacetime deficit, and AI-driven capital demand — not something Treasury buybacks can fix.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs — Macro Research

Still Constructive: 2026 Global Growth Seen at 2.8%, Above Consensus

Goldman reiterates its above-consensus 2026 global growth call (2.8% vs. 2.5% consensus) and US outperformance (2.6% vs. 2.0%), with Q3 US GDP tracking around 3.4%. The desk flags that "hot valuations" paired with sturdy growth could raise volatility even as the overall backdrop stays constructive for equities — a useful counterweight to Dalio's and Marks's more cautious yield-driven warnings this week.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Quiet Week — Steady Accumulation in Lennar Continues

No major new market commentary from Buffett directly this week. Berkshire continued its measured buying of Lennar shares (+2.4M shares, ~$192.6M), building toward a ~12.2% stake — consistent with the firm's pattern of opportunistic, patient accumulation rather than any market-timing signal.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

"Bond Bear Market" With More to Go — Stocks Losing Their Buffer

In an Oct 6 Bloomberg interview, Dalio reiterated his debt-crisis warning (within ~3 years) and said rates will "continue rising until brakes are put on the economy." He argues equities are losing the earnings-growth buffer that's cushioned them against rising yields so far, and continues to recommend a 5-15% gold allocation alongside short-duration positioning.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Rising Yields Are a "Rational" Response — Not Something Buybacks Can Fix

Marks's latest memo (third in a series) argues the climb in long-term Treasury yields reflects sticky inflation, a roughly 6%-of-GDP peacetime deficit, and AI-driven capital demand — a rational market adjustment, not a dysfunction that Treasury buybacks could "repeal." A companion memo examines how the current AI investment cycle compares with, and differs from, prior technology cycles.
