🕊️ US-Iran Talks: Phased Hormuz Reopening on the Table

US and Iranian negotiators meeting in New York reportedly explored a phased arrangement — modeled on an earlier Memorandum of Understanding — in which Iran would agree not to strike vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz while the US eases its naval blockade of Iranian ports. Markets took this as constructive, sending oil down toward $93 and helping fuel Friday's rally. Caveat: similar talks have collapsed before at the finish line, so treat this as a positive signal, not a resolved risk.

💵 Yields "Little Changed" — But Still Historically High

The 10Y Treasury yield closed the week at 5.17%, essentially flat on the day but still near its highest level since 2007. CNBC framed it as the market absorbing a genuinely volatile week rather than reversing the trend — the rate backdrop remains the single biggest headwind for high-multiple growth and rate-sensitive sectors (utilities, real estate) heading into next week.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
JPMorgan — Reaffirms NVDA, Trims Target Slightly

New $265 Target Still Implies Meaningful Upside

JPMorgan raised its 12-month NVIDIA target to $265 from $250 while keeping an Overweight rating — a more measured number than some Street peers' $300+ targets, reflecting the recent AI-capex-financing scrutiny (Oracle) without abandoning the bull case. Read as a sign analysts are recalibrating growth assumptions, not backing away from AI infrastructure demand.

Goldman Sachs Asset Management — Market Pulse

Sturdy Growth, But "Hot Valuations" Keep Volatility Elevated

Goldman's house view remains that solid global growth plus non-recessionary Fed policy should support equities into 2026, but stretched valuations mean volatility spikes — like this week's — should be expected along the way rather than treated as trend breaks.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Still Not Deploying the Cash Pile

Buffett has indicated the market's recent dip isn't deep enough to put Berkshire's roughly $373-400B cash pile to work — a signal he still sees valuations as stretched even after this week's pullback and Friday's bounce. Patience over conviction remains the posture to emulate.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Watching Fed Leadership and the Debt Cycle

Dalio continues to flag that incoming Fed leadership will likely be biased toward pushing rates down over time — supportive for asset prices near-term but a long-run risk if it inflates a bubble on top of already-elevated debt levels. A reminder that this week's yield spike is a symptom of a bigger structural debate, not a one-off event.
