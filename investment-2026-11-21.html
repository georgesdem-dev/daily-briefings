🔥 The Week's Story: AI-Capex Fear Triggers a Correction

What started as profit-taking in megacap AI names snowballed into the worst week for US equities since 2020. The S&P 500 fell for seven straight sessions, closing -8.6% on the week and in correction territory alongside the Dow. The Nasdaq's -5.28% Friday rout erased all of its 2026 gains. The trigger: mounting investor anxiety that hyperscaler AI capital spending (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Google, Oracle) is outrunning near-term revenue proof points, compressing free-cash-flow and raising questions about depreciation schedules on GPU fleets.

Nvidia's Q3 report — the first under interim leadership structure following reported changes at the top — beat on revenue and guidance but failed to stop the bleeding, a classic "sell the news" signal that the market had already priced in perfection. VIX nearly doubled off its recent lows to 35.89, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.92% as money rotated into bonds — reversing an earlier hawkish-Fed-driven climb toward 5.1%.

🏥 UnitedHealth / Humana Plunge on New CMS Audit Proposal

UnitedHealth fell -10.21% Friday (extending a -5.4% drop the prior session) after CMS floated a stricter Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment audit framework, threatening a meaningful chunk of MA plan revenue. Humana was hit even harder, -13.16%, with the selloff spreading across the managed-care group (CVS, Elevance, Cigna all down in sympathy). This is a distinct, idiosyncratic risk on top of the broader AI-capex correction and is the reason UNH has been pulled from this edition's top picks into the watchlist below.

📉 Investor Reaction: Bubble Talk vs. Skepticism

Warren Buffett and Ray Dalio both flagged bubble-like conditions in parts of the market this week — Buffett pointing to Berkshire's continued cash build, Dalio drawing parallels to late-cycle debt and leverage dynamics. Howard Marks pushed back, arguing current conditions don't meet his checklist for a true bubble (no retail euphoria, no "story over numbers" investing at scale) and that this looks more like a valuation reset than a bursting bubble. Goldman Sachs' macro desk reiterated a defensive "hide out" basket first flagged Nov 7 — HII, Expedia, Hyatt, Chime, Tyson, CVS — as oversold names worth adding into the weakness.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Record Cash Pile Looks Prescient Amid the Selloff

Berkshire's continued refusal to deploy its large cash position into the AI-capex rally now looks well-timed. Buffett's long-standing caution about paying up for "story" growth without matching earnings power echoes through this week's repricing of megacap tech. No change to the thesis: Berkshire remains a patient, all-weather vehicle, and a market correction of this size is historically closer to when Buffett becomes a buyer than a seller.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Late-Cycle Debt Dynamics Warning

Dalio reiterated concerns this week about leverage and valuation excess concentrated in AI-related capital spending, framing the pattern in terms of his long-running "big debt cycle" work. His practical takeaway for investors: diversify across asset classes and geographies, don't be fully exposed to any single narrative (including AI), and expect volatility to persist until the capex-versus-returns question is resolved by actual earnings data over the next few quarters.

Goldman Sachs Macro Desk

Defensive "Hide Out" Basket Reiterated as Oversold

Goldman's basket first flagged Nov 7 — Huntington Ingalls (HII), Expedia, Hyatt, Chime, Tyson Foods, and CVS Health — was reiterated this week as offering an oversold entry point, selected for low correlation to the AI-capex narrative and idiosyncratic, company-specific catalysts. This basket underpins the inclusion of HII in this edition's top picks.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Pushing Back on "Bubble" Framing

Contrary to Buffett and Dalio's caution, Marks argued this week that current conditions don't meet his own checklist for a classic bubble — there's no widespread retail euphoria, and valuations, while stretched in pockets, are still underpinned by real earnings and cash flow at the largest AI spenders. His read: this is a valuation reset and sentiment shakeout, not the start of a 2000-style collapse. His prescription is unchanged from prior weeks — favor quality, income, and reasonable valuations over momentum.
