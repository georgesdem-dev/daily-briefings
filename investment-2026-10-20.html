📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🔄 Chips Snap Longest Losing Streak in 18 Years

Monday's session delivered the relief bounce markets were looking for after last week's worst drawdown since June. The Nasdaq Composite led with a 1.11% gain to 26,430.89, driven by an AI/chip-stock rebound — the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.6%, snapping an eight-session losing streak, its longest in 18 years. The S&P 500 added 0.41% to 7,690.73, while the Dow slipped a modest 0.07% to 51,966.03 as some rotation out of industrials funded the tech bounce. The VIX fell sharply to 18.44 (-7.75%), a clear signal that Friday's fear spike was overdone, at least for now.

Amazon was the standout mover, surging 5% to $262.45 after Morgan Stanley named it a top pick and detailed five catalysts supporting a higher price target — adding to Goldman's existing $375 Conviction List call. With chip stocks stabilizing and Netflix, GM, GE Aerospace, RTX and 3M all reporting today, the market gets its first real test of whether this bounce has legs or was just short-covering ahead of the heaviest stretch of Q3 earnings season.

🏭 Sector Performance — YTD Leaders & Laggards

Energy remains the runaway YTD leader at +51.3%, powered by the sustained Hormuz war premium on crude, with Technology a distant second at +32.3% — both still comfortably ahead of the S&P 500's +13.3% YTD total return. Healthcare (+12.4%), consumer staples (+10.8%) and materials (+9.5%) round out the positive column, while industrials (+7.7%) and real estate (+2.8%) are modestly positive. Four sectors remain in the red for the year: financials (-0.5%), utilities (-2.3%), communication services (-5.9%) and consumer discretionary (-6.6%) — though Monday's chip rebound gave technology and communication services a one-day reprieve from the prior week's AI-trade sell-off.

🪖 Hormuz Risk Persists Even as Markets Calm

The geopolitical backdrop hasn't actually improved — it's just being priced more calmly. Iran's IRGC confirmed on October 17 that a supertanker carrying over 2 million barrels of crude exploded and caught fire after striking a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, part of a surge that saw at least 11 tankers attacked in the week ending October 4, the most since the war began in February. Oil wavered Monday as traders weighed the escalation risk against still-robust Gulf crude flows, with Brent settling near $109.55 (just off its five-month high) and WTI around $98. This remains the single biggest swing factor for energy, inflation expectations and the Fed's rate path into year-end.

🖥️ Big Tech Earnings Week Is Underway

Netflix opens the week's marquee earnings today, with analysts expecting EPS of roughly $0.82 versus $0.59 a year ago, and the market focused on advertising-tier growth and Q4 guidance. GM, GE Aerospace, RTX and 3M also report Tuesday. Wednesday brings the week's biggest single session: Meta and Tesla report after the close, both treated as proxies for AI-infrastructure spending and automation bets. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Intel don't report until October 28-29 — meaning this week's prints are an early tell, not the full verdict, on whether AI capex is still being rewarded by the market after last week's wobble.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cautious on Valuations — "Tough to Find Values" as Buffett Indicator Flashes 227%

Buffett has reiterated that the current pullback doesn't meet his threshold for action, with Berkshire's cash pile around $373B — the message is to wait for real distress, not just slightly cheaper prices. The "Buffett Indicator" (total market cap to GDP) sits near 227%, well above the 200% level Buffett once called "playing with fire." Despite the caution, Berkshire's quiet ~$37.4B build in Alphabet shows selective conviction persists even in an expensive market — the lesson for this portfolio is to stay selective (AMZN, UNH, JNJ) rather than broadly aggressive.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Bubble Warnings Persist Alongside Tariff and Geopolitical Risk

Dalio remains among the hedge-fund titans flagging bubble-like conditions in the AI trade, while separately warning on tariffs and geopolitical tension (including the ongoing Hormuz conflict) as compounding risks to the rate and inflation outlook. His view: equities' cushion against rising yields narrows as AI capex continues to outpace free cash flow generation — a dynamic investors should watch closely heading into this week's earnings from Meta and Tesla, two of the most AI-capex-exposed reporters.

Goldman Sachs — October Conviction List (unchanged this week)

Amazon, Burlington, HII, JCI, OXY Remain the Desk's Top Buy-Rated Names

Goldman's refreshed October Conviction List — Amazon ($375 target), Burlington Stores, Huntington Ingalls (top pick, $439 target), Johnson Controls and Occidental Petroleum — remains the anchor for this week's picks, with Monday's Morgan Stanley upgrade of Amazon independently validating the thesis. Goldman has separately flagged UPS, Omnicom, Nu Holdings and Baker Hughes as other buy-rated names, spanning defense, cloud/retail, energy and industrials — a useful cross-check against any single-sector AI jitters.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Investors "Overly Optimistic" — Unconvinced This Is a Full-Blown Bubble

While Dalio and other billionaires warn of bubble conditions, Howard Marks remains unconvinced the market has crossed that line, instead describing investor behavior as overly optimistic: fear receding, skepticism evaporating, and FOMO replacing disciplined screening. His prescription echoes Buffett's playbook — stay invested in quality rather than retreating to cash, but trim the most narrative-driven, stretched positions. That supports this week's tilt toward Goldman's conviction names and healthcare/financial ballast (UNH, JNJ, JPM) over chasing pure AI momentum into binary earnings catalysts.
