🔥 The Week That Was — Historic Volatility

Tuesday November 25 — Black Tuesday: The S&P 500 plunged 3.2% intraday (Dow -1,100+ pts) as three forces collided simultaneously: (1) October PCE inflation printed at 3.8% YoY — a 5-month high — crushing rate-cut hopes; (2) Trump proposed sweeping new tariffs on 14 countries including China (+20% excess-capacity levies), Japan, the EU and India; (3) Alphabet and Meta led an AI selloff as Howard Marks' memo on AI unpredictability spread across trading desks. The Nasdaq briefly hit bear market territory (-20.4% from October's all-time high) before a late-session recovery.

Wednesday–Thursday: Bargain hunters stepped in as Thanksgiving holiday reduced liquidity. The Dow reclaimed 50,000 — a psychological anchor — while the S&P held the 7,100–7,200 support zone. VIX pulled back from an intraday spike of 40 to the current 26.36.

Black Friday (today): A shortened session. Volume is expected to be thin. Any directional move is amplified. Goldman Sachs maintains their year-end S&P 500 target of 8,000 — implying +11.4% upside from current levels over the next 5 weeks, but that thesis requires a macro catalyst.

📉 Macro Backdrop: Inflation Resurgence + Tariff Overhang

The October PCE reading of 3.8% YoY is the central concern. This is the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, and a 5-month high removes any near-term rate-cut conversation. JPMorgan now forecasts two more rate cuts "followed by an extended pause" — but the market was pricing in more. The 10-year Treasury at 4.91–4.93% is at its highest level in five months, compressing equity valuations across the board, particularly in rate-sensitive sectors (REITs, utilities, growth tech). Ray Dalio warns this bond bear market "has a long way to run" — $2.1 trillion in net new Treasury issuance expected in 2026 alone.

On tariffs: the proposed levies are inherently inflationary. Tech supply chains (semiconductors, consumer electronics) are most exposed. China faces an additional 20%+ tariff ON TOP of existing ~25% duties. This creates a double hit to tech earnings and consumer spending simultaneously.

OPEC+ meeting (November 29): A critical near-term catalyst. The cartel will decide whether to extend production cuts into Q1 2027. Any surprise cut extension could push WTI back above $90, reigniting energy inflation fears.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Greg Abel — Berkshire Hathaway

The Abel Era Begins — Trimming Tech, Buying Quality Value

Warren Buffett officially passed the CEO baton to Greg Abel in early 2026. In his final moves, Buffett trimmed Apple (still Berkshire's largest holding at ~$61.96B), sold ~50.8M shares of Bank of America, and added Domino's Pizza, Chevron, and Chubb. The message is clear: at current market valuations, with AI speculation running hot and rates elevated, Berkshire is favoring proven earnings, pricing power, and income. The massive cash pile (reported near record levels) signals either deep caution about stretched valuations — or Abel is building dry powder for a transformational acquisition. BRK-B is the market's most credible "sleep well at night" holding right now.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater Associates

"A Triple Storm" — 2026–2028 Is a Particularly Dangerous Period

Dalio is sounding his loudest alarm yet. He identifies a converging "triple storm": (1) AI disruption creating unprecedented labor and capital market uncertainty; (2) US Treasury supply/demand imbalance — $32T in marketable debt, $2.1T in net new 2026 issuance — that the bond bear market "has a long way to run"; (3) Geopolitical fragmentation and dollar destabilization risk as political forces reshape monetary policy heading into the 2028 election cycle. His prescription: diversify aggressively across asset classes and geographies; hold hard assets; reduce US-only equity concentration. The 10-year at 4.92% is consistent with his thesis — higher rates, longer.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital — "A Layman Thinks About AI" (Nov 11, 2026)

AI Predictability: 2 Out of 10 — Investor Caution Warranted

In his landmark November memo, Marks called AI "the most consequential and least predictable development for investors in at least a generation." He rated frontier AI lab competitive predictability at 2/10 — extremely low. His key data point: global AI-related debt issuances in 2026 have approached $600 billion, with ~$400B flooding the US investment-grade bond market (20%+ of all new IG supply). The question he forces investors to confront: can this $600B AI CapEx supercycle generate returns sufficient to justify the investment? His verdict: unknown, and the competitive moats are narrower than assumed. His prescription echoes our picks — increase income-generating assets, reduce 100x+ P/E growth names, hold cash for optionality.

Goldman Sachs — Q4 2026 Market Know-How

S&P 500 Year-End Target: 8,000 — "Earnings Power Underestimated"

Goldman Sachs lifted its S&P 500 year-end target to 8,000 (from 7,600) on stronger earnings. Their EPS forecasts: $340 for full-year 2026 (+24% growth) and $385 for 2027. Goldman's Q4 2026 theme is "Yield Ahead" — the case for income-generating assets strengthens as rates stay elevated. They favor AI-linked sectors, quality growth, and productivity-driven names. The November volatility — Nasdaq -20% intraday — has not shaken their conviction. If correct, the current 7,182 level represents a ~11.4% return opportunity to year-end. Their risk scenario: if PCE stays above 3.5% through December, the Santa Claus rally is delayed into January earnings season.

JPMorgan Markets Research — November 2026 Outlook

Overweight TMT & Utilities · 35% Recession Probability · S&P 7,500 Target

JPMorgan's equity strategy desk has a more conservative S&P 500 year-end target of 7,500 (bull case 8,000 if the Fed signals earlier cuts). They assign a 35% recession probability — not the base case, but meaningfully elevated. Their sector overweights are TMT (Technology, Media, Telecom), Utilities, and Defense. For AI specifically, they highlight Amazon, Nvidia, and Alphabet as the core datacenter basket. On rates: JPMorgan sees two more Fed cuts "followed by an extended pause" — meaning 2027 could see rates stabilizing, which would be a meaningful re-rating catalyst for growth stocks. The October PCE print of 3.8% has pushed their first cut expectation to late Q1 2027 at the earliest.
