🌊 Tariffs + Hot PCE Trigger a Steep Selloff — Then a Dramatic Reversal

Wednesday opened badly: the U.S. Trade Representative proposed new country-specific tariffs on 14+ trading partners — up to 20% additional on Chinese goods, 15% on eligible EU imports, up to 25% on Indian goods, and further proposals touching Japan, South Korea, Indonesia, Switzerland, Taiwan and others. That landed alongside hotter-than-expected October PCE inflation (headline 3.8% vs. 3.6% prior; core 3.3% vs. 3.2% expected) — a combination that raises the odds of a more hawkish Fed, per EY chief economist Greg Daco. The Nasdaq fell as much as 4.7% intraday and the S&P over 3% before buyers stepped back in.

By the close, the Dow had erased an 1,100-point intraday loss to finish up 0.33% at a fresh record, while the S&P and Nasdaq pared their losses to -0.25% and -0.62% respectively. NVDA was down as much as 8.6% at the lows before closing -3.91% at $149.30 (now down roughly 27% for the month of November); AMD closed a more modest -1.75% at $406.03. The scale of the intraday reversal — and VIX still rising +7.32% to 28.29 despite the bounce — suggests markets absorbed the news rather than dismissed it.

💰 JPMorgan Lifts Amazon Target to $365 on AWS Acceleration

Away from the tariff/inflation story, JPMorgan's Doug Anmuth raised his Amazon price target to $365 from $330 (keeping it on his Best Idea list), citing AWS revenue growth of 39% year-over-year in Q3 — the fastest in nearly five years — and forecasting AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028. Amazon now carries 59 Buy ratings and not a single Sell among covering analysts.
