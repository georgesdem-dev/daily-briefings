🌟 Top Story: Hackers Breach Japan's Onamae Desktop Cloud, Hitting Up to 26,739 Servers and Disrupting Forex Trading at 70+ Brokerages EXTORTION DEMAND REJECTED · PLATFORM SHUT DOWN

GMO Internet Group confirmed on October 20 that an unauthorized third party breached its Onamae Desktop Cloud VPS platform — widely marketed in Japan for running continuous MetaTrader 4 forex automation — and installed malware across as many as 26,739 customer server environments. GMO began shutting down affected servers at 4am local time on October 21 to contain the intrusion, rejected an extortion demand from the attackers, and has not set a restart date; instead it will offer customers a free replacement service on separate infrastructure for six months, plus 5,000 yen goodwill payments and loss compensation. The breach triggered fraudulent trades across more than 70 forex brokerages reliant on the platform, and OANDA Securities Japan delayed customer withdrawals on October 22 over concerns that MetaTrader credentials stored on compromised servers had been exposed. GMO says payment card data was unaffected because it sits on separate infrastructure, but it is urging all affected customers to rotate any passwords or secrets stored on their servers and to watch for phishing attempts exploiting the incident. One security researcher has tentatively linked the intrusion to abuse of AI-assisted penetration-testing tools, part of a broader warning from Japanese officials about "AI cyber saturation attacks" that overwhelm conventional defenses.

If you or your clients run trading infrastructure, CI/CD runners, or any automation on third-party VPS platforms, this is a reminder that credential exposure from a hosting-layer breach can cascade into fraudulent transactions within hours — rotate any secrets stored on hosted infrastructure proactively rather than waiting for a breach notice.

Anthropic publicly filed its S-1 registration statement with the SEC on Monday, confirming plans to list Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker ANT, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a 22-bank underwriting syndicate. The filing discloses $16.286 billion in revenue for the six months ended June 30, 2026 — up roughly 1,300% from $1.163 billion a year earlier — with second-quarter revenue alone hitting $11.56 billion and turning operating-income positive at $974 million, following a $1.667 billion operating loss in Q1. The headline net loss of $80.748 billion is dominated by $72.388 billion of non-cash fair-value accounting changes on convertible notes and derivative liabilities tied to the company's soaring valuation; the real operating loss for the half was $693 million, down sharply from $2.135 billion a year earlier. The filing also details roughly $390 billion in combined committed cloud and compute spending — $110 billion with Amazon through April 2036, $120 billion with SpaceX through 2029, $115 billion with Google and $45 billion with Microsoft, both through 2033 — alongside a $125.2 billion five-year equipment lease. The price range, share count and valuation target remain blank, though reporting has floated figures as high as $2 trillion, with some investors reportedly pushing back toward $1.5 trillion given the headline loss.
