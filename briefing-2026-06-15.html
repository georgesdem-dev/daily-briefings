Breaking US & Iran Announce Ceasefire Extension Deal — Hormuz to Reopen at Geneva Signing

The United States and Iran reached an initial agreement today to extend their fragile ceasefire and reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping. The deal, brokered by Pakistan, is scheduled to be formally signed Friday, June 19 in Geneva — with Iran stating it will not implement any terms until the signing ceremony takes place.

Key terms of the deal include a 60-day ceasefire extension, a memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran, and the immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz upon signing. A 60-day window is also established to negotiate the fate of Iran's highly enriched uranium stockpile and nuclear program.

Trump authorized ending the US naval blockade of Iranian ports as part of the accord. France and other Western partners signaled they are "ready to take action very quickly" to help restore normal shipping traffic through the strait. However, energy experts caution it could take months for global fuel prices to stabilize even once the strait reopens.

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz declared that Israel will remain "indefinitely" in occupied territories in Lebanon, Syria, and Gaza — a direct contradiction of Iran's insistence that any agreement must include an end to Israel's fighting in Lebanon against Iranian-backed Hezbollah.

Netanyahu's spokesman reiterated that Israel "will continue to defend itself against any threat," signaling Jerusalem is not bound by the US-Iran accord. This creates a potential decoupling between the wider US-Iran ceasefire and the Israel-Hezbollah front — a gap that could unravel the deal before it is signed.

Context Nuclear Talks Deferred 60 Days; Global Geopolitical Stakes

The agreement deliberately defers the hardest question — Iran's nuclear program — to a 60-day negotiating window. The US and Israel remain deeply concerned that Iran's highly enriched uranium stockpile could support weapons development, while Tehran continues to insist its program is peaceful.

The conflict began on February 28, 2026, when Israel and the United States launched airstrikes against Iran, killing its Supreme Leader. Iran responded with a broad missile and drone campaign against Israel, US bases, and Gulf Arab states, and by closing the Strait of Hormuz. After over five weeks of fighting, a first ceasefire was reached on April 7–8. Since then, multiple extensions and violations have kept the region on edge.

Humanitarian toll to date: thousands dead in Iran and Lebanon, dozens in Israel and Gulf states, and more than one-sixth of Lebanon's population displaced.

Zero-Day Oracle PeopleSoft CVE-2026-35273 (CVSS 9.8) — ShinyHunters Breaches 100+ Orgs

The ShinyHunters extortion group (tracked as UNC6240 by Mandiant/Google Threat Intelligence) has exploited a critical unauthenticated RCE vulnerability in Oracle PeopleSoft Enterprise PeopleTools (versions 8.61 and 8.62) to breach over 100 organizations, including the University of Nottingham, across 300 vulnerable instances.

The flaw — CVE-2026-35273 (CVSS 9.8) — requires only network HTTP access and no login or user interaction to achieve full server takeover. It was exploited as a zero-day from May 27 through June 9, nearly two weeks before Oracle pushed an out-of-band security advisory. Exploitation targeted the PeopleSoft Environment Management Hub (PSEMHUB) endpoints; stolen data was published on the ShinyHunters Data Leak Site on June 9.

Universities have been disproportionately affected, but the sector spread is wide. Mandiant classifies the underlying vulnerability as SSRF-derived.

Sources: The Hacker News · Rapid7 · SecurityWeek · The Register
Law Enforcement Europol Dismantles AudiA6 Crypto-Laundering Service; OnyxC2 Malware Targets 200+ Apps

European authorities, coordinated by Europol, disrupted the AudiA6 cryptocurrency laundering service, a key financial pipeline used by ransomware gangs and cybercriminal networks to wash hundreds of millions in illicit profits. Ongoing Claude Fable 5 Pulled by US Export Directive — Security Community Protests Anthropic Model Ban

The reverberations from last week's Claude Fable 5 launch and removal continue to dominate AI policy conversations. Anthropic launched Claude Fable 5 on June 9, describing it as the most capable model it had ever publicly deployed — scoring more than 10% higher than Claude Opus 4.8 on several benchmarks and excelling in software engineering, vision, scientific research, and autonomous task execution.

By June 12 at 5:21 PM ET, the model was pulled — the first time a US government export control directive has ever been used to take down a live, publicly deployed AI model. The restriction extends to both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 (the restricted government-adjacent variant), citing concerns about the models' offensive cyber capabilities.

Cybersecurity experts are now protesting the ban, warning that restricting access to Anthropic's most powerful defensive AI tools could limit blue-team and threat-hunting capabilities while doing little to prevent adversarial use of equivalent foreign models. The Trump administration's AI security framework executive order — signed this week — "stops short of regulating industry" while acknowledging these risks.
