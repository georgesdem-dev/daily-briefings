📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🗳️ Democrats Sweep Congress — Markets Cheer the Certainty, Not the Policy

Wednesday delivered the strongest single session for US equities in 18 months after Democrats won control of both the House and Senate, ending unified Republican government. The Dow rocketed +1,569.40 points (+3.03%) to 53,356.12 — above 53,000 for the first time since Sept. 8 — while the S&P 500 gained +119.70 (+1.58%) to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq added +364.50 (+1.44%) to 25,736.51. Small-caps outperformed sharply, with the Russell 2000 up roughly +5.5% and regional banks surging as much as +13% intraday.

The rally wasn't a vote of confidence in Democratic policy — it was relief that the binary election-night risk is resolved. Traders had priced in weeks of uncertainty, and futures had swung from down -1.4% to flat overnight as results came in. Pepperstone's Chris Weston had flagged beforehand that "a narrow blue wave may be largely priced in" — but the decisive margin removed the ambiguity entirely, triggering a classic uncertainty-unwind rally.

🛢️ Oil Crashes Nearly -10% — Dollar Weakens, Gold Still Rallies

WTI crude tumbled close to -10% to roughly $93.71/bbl as the political resolution reduced near-term geopolitical-escalation odds and the dollar weakened on expectations of divided-government fiscal restraint. That combination — equities up sharply, oil down sharply, and gold up — is unusual: gold jumped +3.23% to $4,066/oz even as risk assets rallied, as falling Treasury yields (10Y eased to 4.889%) made the non-yielding metal more attractive and some investors treat it as a hedge against the AI-regulation and policy-uncertainty questions a Democratic Congress now raises.

VIX collapsed -20.26% to 16.61, its lowest reading in weeks, as the single largest source of near-term event risk — the election itself — is now behind the market.

🤖 The New Overhang: AI Regulation Under a Democratic Congress

The one note of caution in an otherwise euphoric session: strategists flagged that a decisive Democratic win raises the odds of tighter AI oversight. BMO's Ian Lyngen noted "the better the Democrats perform, the more nervous the market is likely to become regarding AI regulations," while adding the equity market is "holding up relatively well, all things considered." This showed up specifically in defense: Huntington Ingalls (HII) fell -4.08% to $303.99 on expectations that a Democratic Congress could scrutinize defense appropriations more closely, even as the broader industrial complex rose.

History is on the bulls' side regardless: in all 19 midterm election cycles going back decades, the S&P 500 rose over the following 14 months, averaging +20.3% with a weakest case of +1.3% (Carson Research / Yahoo Finance data). The November–April window is historically the strongest six-month stretch of the four-year presidential cycle — a tailwind for the quarter ahead, even with the fresh AI-policy question mark.

Energy was the session's worst performer, pressured directly by oil's ~-10% plunge, and Materials fell -1.9% as the broader commodity complex softened alongside crude.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Sitting on $397B Cash — "We've Never Had People in a More Gambling Mood"

Buffett continues to hold a record cash pile (~$397B) and has publicly described the market as being in a "gambling mood," calling today's valuations "just a slightly cheaper version of an expensive market." His well-known warning that a market-cap-to-GDP ratio above 200% is "playing with fire" remains relevant — that ratio is still well above that threshold even after recent pullbacks. His stance: no deployment until a genuinely large decline arrives, not a one-day election-relief rally.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

AI Valuations Still "Early Stages of a Bubble" — Diversify Into Gold

In a recent CNBC interview, Dalio reiterated that soaring tech valuations could face a correction before companies fully monetize AI investment, and pointed to gold — 2025's best-performing major market — as a key diversifier for 2026. Today's unusual pattern (equities up, oil down, and gold up +3.2%) is arguably a live illustration of Dalio's thesis: the market is celebrating resolved political risk while simultaneously hedging against the AI-regulation and valuation risks that remain unresolved.

Pepperstone — Chris Weston

"A Narrow Blue Wave May Be Largely Priced In" — But the Margin Mattered

Weston's pre-election framing proved prescient: the market had already discounted a close Democratic win, which is exactly why the decisive nature of the sweep (both chambers, comfortably) is what triggered Wednesday's outsized rally — it removed the tail risk of a contested or ambiguous outcome entirely, rather than confirming a result traders were braced for.

BMO Capital Markets — Ian Lyngen

AI Regulation Is the New Swing Factor — But the Market Is "Holding Up Relatively Well"

Lyngen's framing is the key nuance behind today's otherwise euphoric tape: "the better the Democrats perform, the more nervous the market is likely to become regarding AI regulations." That dynamic showed up immediately in defense (HII -4.08%) and is worth monitoring across the AI-infrastructure complex over the coming weeks as committee assignments and early legislative priorities take shape.

Carson Research / Historical Seasonality

Post-Midterm Seasonality Has a Perfect Track Record: +20.3% Average Over 14 Months

Across all 19 midterm election cycles on record, the S&P 500 has risen in the 14 months following the vote, averaging +20.3% with a median of +22.3% — the weakest cycle still returned +1.3%. Separately, Carson Research data shows the six months from November through April is historically the strongest stretch of the four-year presidential cycle. The market entered this election with momentum already somewhat damaged by October's AI-capex selloff, so this tailwind is a genuine offset, not a guarantee.
