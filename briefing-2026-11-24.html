🌟 Top Story: Four Oil Tankers Hit Naval Mines and Catch Fire in Hormuz While Under US Navy Escort US ESCORT FAILED TO PREVENT IT · MINE WARFARE

Iran's IRGC Navy said four oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire early Tuesday in restricted waters east of the Strait of Hormuz, with the first reports describing two tankers hit, then a third, then a fourth suffering a "severe explosion and fire." The IRGC pointedly noted the vessels were transiting under US military escort at the time — escort that "failed to prevent" the mine strikes — a claim that, if accurate, is a serious credibility blow to the US Navy's weeks-old policy of escorting tankers through the waterway. It is not yet confirmed who laid the mines; Tehran has previously warned ships against using what it calls unauthorized routes through the strait. The incident lands the same day Qatar intensified its mediation push: Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Washington to launch the eighth US-Qatar Strategic Dialogue, while Vice President JD Vance separately hosted Qatari envoy Ali al-Thawadi at the White House. Al Thani said the region "cannot afford a new escalation" and called for restoring freedom of navigation through Hormuz alongside a return to US-Iran talks. Iran, however, continues to rule out negotiations until seven conditions are met — reportedly including lifting the US naval blockade on Iranian ports, easing sanctions, releasing frozen funds, ending hostilities on all fronts, and a formal declaration that the war is over. Trump has accused Tehran of not being serious about a deal. Bloomberg estimates Iranian attacks on shipping have averaged roughly one per day through November, with strait traffic still about 65% below pre-war levels and the Pentagon pegging total US war spending at $48.3 billion as of this month.

🌟 Top Story: CISA's Patch Deadline Lands Today for Exploited Outlook NTLM Zero-Day — While AI Agents Are Caught Finding Their Own KEV DEADLINE TODAY · CVE-2026-124194

The federal remediation deadline falls today for CVE-2026-124194, a Microsoft Outlook spoofing flaw (CVSS 6.5) under active exploitation: a specially crafted message file makes classic Outlook silently connect to an attacker-controlled server and leak the user's NTLMv2 hash, with no security warning shown. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 10, the same day Microsoft shipped the fix as part of November Patch Tuesday (1,094 CVEs total, this the only confirmed actively-exploited one). Separately, Amazon disclosed a more unusual story: autonomous AI agents built on the open-source "Cairn" framework independently discovered and exploited a critical path-traversal zero-day (CVE-2026-133968, CVSS 9.8) in MONAI Label, an open-source medical-imaging annotation tool, without human direction. The agents fingerprinted the app, mapped its API, found an unauthenticated upload endpoint vulnerable to path traversal, and achieved arbitrary file writes and a root reverse shell in about 11 minutes — compromising a university server in East Asia in an operation Amazon links to a China-nexus actor and spotted via an exposed Cairn instance on September 10. MONAI Label 0.8.6 fixes it; the CISA KEV deadline is November 26. Rounding out the day: BerriAI's LiteLLM gateway sandbox-escape flaw (CVE-2026-40217), already being used for "LLMjacking" of stolen model access, joined the KEV catalog with a December 2 deadline; and CISA continues to warn of active exploitation of VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890), Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466), and Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448, unauthenticated RCE).

Anthropic launched Claude Ship in public beta for Claude Team and Enterprise GitHub users, pairing its generally-available Code Review agent with two new agents — PR Steward, which automatically fixes failing CI checks, merge conflicts, and review feedback, and Claude Approvals, which can approve pull requests under team-defined rules — to carry a PR from open to merge with minimal human intervention.
