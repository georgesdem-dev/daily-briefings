📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

📉 September Payrolls Badly Miss: Just 29,000 Jobs Added

The September jobs report landed well short of expectations — 29,000 nonfarm payrolls added versus a forecast of roughly 90,000, with the unemployment rate rising to 4.2% from 4.1%. Average hourly earnings rose a modest 0.1% month-over-month (3.0% year-over-year). Healthcare led gains (+17,000), with construction (+11,000) and manufacturing (+9,000) also contributing — but the headline miss marks a sharp reversal from August's much stronger reading and raises fresh questions about labor-market momentum heading into Q4.

📈 Stocks Rally on "Bad News is Good News" Fed Read

Rather than spooking markets, the weak jobs data fueled a rally: the Dow rose 0.5%, S&P 500 gained 0.7%, and the Nasdaq surged 1.2% to a fresh record, with the Nasdaq 100 also closing at an all-time high. The logic: a softening labor market all but cements the case for the Fed to stay on hold — or eventually cut — rather than hike again in October, removing the single biggest overhang that's weighed on yields and rate-sensitive sectors all September.

⚡ Chips Lead the Charge — NVDA Hits New All-Time High

NVIDIA closed Friday at $233.95 (+1.34%) after touching an intraday all-time high of $237.88, dragging the broader semiconductor complex — Intel, Broadcom, AMD — higher alongside it. The chip rally, building on Micron's blowout quarter earlier in the week, reinforced that AI infrastructure spending remains the market's dominant and most durable theme even as the macro backdrop gets choppier.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
CNBC / Street Consensus — Jobs Report Reaction

A Weak Print That the Market Read as Good News

September's 29,000-job gain — a third of consensus — would ordinarily raise recession alarm bells. Instead, equities rallied because it all but guarantees the Fed holds rates steady this month, giving the market exactly the "soft landing, no further hikes" combination it's wanted since yields hit 24-year highs in late September.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Still Sitting on Cash — Weak Jobs Data Hasn't Changed the Math

Buffett continues to view Berkshire's roughly $397B cash pile as better deployed only after a genuine valuation reset. A one-day rally driven by rate-cut hopes, built on top of an already-stretched market, does nothing to change that calculus — if anything it reinforces his "not investing, it's gambling" critique of short-term options-driven trading.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Nearly Four Years of Optimism — And Counting

Marks's standing warning — that a multi-year run of investor optimism raises the odds of paying more than businesses are worth — gets harder to dismiss on days like Friday, when genuinely weak economic data triggers a record-setting rally purely on rate-path hopes rather than improving fundamentals.

Goldman Sachs Asset Management — Market Pulse

Financials' Steepening-Curve Tailwind Still Intact

Goldman's house view continues to flag financials as a beneficiary of a steeper yield curve and resilient capital-markets activity (+12.26% YTD for the sector) — a dynamic that should persist even if the Fed holds in October, since a pause (not a dramatic cut) is what best supports bank net interest margins.
