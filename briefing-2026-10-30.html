Iran War — First Limpet-Mine Strike Since February, as Qatar Returns to Tehran
Operation Epic Fury · Feb 28, 2026 – Present · Day 245
🌟 Top Story: Limpet Mines Disable a Hormuz Tanker for the First Time Since the War Began, as Qatari Mediators Return to Tehran TACTICAL SHIFT · 3 MORE TANKERS AFIRE · DRONE DOWNED

A small boat attached two limpet mines to the hull of the Panama-flagged tanker Louise 1 in the Strait of Hormuz late Thursday night, igniting a fire in its engine room and temporarily disabling the vessel — the first confirmed successful use of limpet mines against shipping in the waterway since the war began on February 28, following a failed attempt with the same device type in September. Maritime security sources describe it as a deliberate tactical shift from the drifting naval mines Iran has relied on so far, since limpet mines require a boat to physically approach and attach the device to a target. The UK's maritime trade agency (UKMTO) confirmed the crew was safe, there was no environmental impact, and the vessel resumed its voyage after the fire was extinguished; no one has claimed responsibility. The attack came amid a broader wave of incidents reported by Iran's own naval forces: the regular navy said two more tankers caught fire after striking mines in the eastern strait, the IRGC Navy separately reported a supertanker carrying 2 million barrels catching fire after a mine strike in the southern strait, and Iran's navy said it destroyed an unmanned enemy drone east of the strait — a claim the US has not confirmed. Oman's navy separately rescued 25 crew members from a vessel "targeted" 122 miles off its Musandam coast. Despite the escalation, diplomacy continued: a Qatari delegation landed in Tehran on Friday for a fresh round of talks, days after US Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Iran's Supreme National Security Council proposing direct, Vance-led negotiations with Iran's nuclear program on the table from the outset. CBS News reported signs of progress, with Iran expected to respond to a revised Qatari de-escalation proposal by the end of the week. The core disagreement remains sequencing: Washington wants Iran's nuclear program addressed immediately, while Tehran wants sanctions relief before further concessions — a pattern Iranian officials have described as the US "talking diplomacy while preparing for war."

🌟 Top Story: North Korea's Lazarus Group Steals a Record $6.36 Billion From Binance LARGEST CRYPTO THEFT EVER · ~$3B FROZEN SO FAR

An attacker drained approximately $6.36 billion from Binance's own wallets across ten blockchains — Ethereum, Bitcoin, Tron, XRP Ledger, Solana, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, Base and Polygon — with most of the theft executed in roughly 20 minutes starting at 20:05 UTC on October 29. This is now the largest crypto theft on record, about four times the $1.5 billion Bybit hack of February 2025, and critically it targeted Binance's corporate funds, not individual user accounts. Blockchain intelligence firms TRM Labs, Elliptic and Chainalysis independently attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, the North Korean regime's elite hacking unit also known as part of the Lazarus Group, citing high confidence based on tactical overlaps with earlier DPRK-linked heists including Bybit. Reporting suggests the attacker compromised a Binance employee's laptop as the initial entry point, though exactly how the device was breached remains unclear. TRM Labs traced the operator's wallet receiving small test withdrawals from Tornado Cash — a crypto-mixing service — beginning October 28, followed by deployment and testing of attack code on two wallets before the main theft. Roughly $3 billion of the stolen funds has since been frozen on-chain by exchanges and blockchain security firms working to intercept the funds before they're laundered further.

A single compromised employee laptop reportedly enabled a $6.36 billion theft from a major exchange's own treasury — a stark reminder that endpoint compromise on privileged staff devices remains one of the highest-leverage attack paths against even sophisticated, security-mature organizations; hot-wallet operational security and device isolation for treasury-access staff deserve board-level attention, not just technical review.

🌟 Top Story: Anthropic's IPO Filing Gives the Industry Its First Audited Look at Frontier AI Economics $16.3B H1 REVENUE · $63B CHIP FINANCING DEAL

Anthropic's S-1 filing for its planned Nasdaq listing under ticker "ANT" disclosed $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, up from $1.2 billion a year earlier, with more than 11,900 customers now paying $100,000 or more annually. Notably, nearly half of that revenue flows through cloud partners Amazon, Google and Microsoft rather than direct sales, underscoring how dependent frontier AI economics remain on hyperscaler distribution. The filing is widely described as the industry's first audited look at frontier AI unit economics, offering a real benchmark for how fast — and how expensively — a leading lab scales. Alongside the filing, a new $63 billion financing vehicle was announced that will let Anthropic lease Google-designed, Broadcom-made TPUs, a deal aimed at securing compute independent of Nvidia GPU supply as Anthropic competes with both OpenAI and Google. In a related infrastructure move, Oracle will begin offering Google TPUs on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure starting in 2027 — the first hyperscaler outside Google to do so — and OpenAI is reportedly moving to buy Google TPUs as well, with OpenAI models also headed to Google Cloud. Google's own unreleased Gemini 4 Carbon model topped the Artificial Analysis intelligence index, scoring one point above Claude Opus 5.5, following September's Argon release. Alphabet separately reported Q3 revenue of $127.6 billion, up 25% year-over-year, with Google Cloud revenue up 103% to $30.7 billion against $51.4 billion in quarterly capex — but executives acknowledged AI Mode search results monetize at a lower rate than traditional search, prompting a global ad rollout inside AI Mode, while Reddit reported losing over a million weekly Google referral visits in October, adding to concerns that AI-generated search answers are starving the publisher ecosystem that depends on referral traffic.
