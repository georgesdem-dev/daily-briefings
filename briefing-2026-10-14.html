🌟 Top Story: Worst Week of Shipping Attacks Since the War Began 2 CARGO SHIPS SUNK TODAY

Ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz are facing the worst spate of attacks since the US and Israel's war on Iran began in late February. Maritime tracking firm Kpler counted 10 tankers struck in the strait between September 28 and October 4, with another the day after; UKMTO separately logged 15 more attacks between October 6 and 14, with 11 of them in just the past four days. Wednesday brought two confirmed sinkings: the Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride, carrying 2,824 TEU, sank after being struck by an unidentified projectile, and a separate cargo vessel carrying more than 2,500 containers sank south of the strait after Iran's IRGC Navy said it struck a naval mine while sailing an "unauthorized and hazardous route" with navigation dark. Iran's military said Wednesday it has expanded surveillance of the strait by deploying new units specifically to monitor and target shipping, and is now telling vessels to coordinate directly with Iranian armed forces for safe passage — a de facto claim of control over who transits the waterway. A senior Iranian official told Reuters Tehran shifted to more aggressive tactics roughly ten days ago against vessels it deems in violation of its restrictions, which tracks with the sharp rise in attacks since early October. CENTCOM pushed back hard on Tehran's narrative, stating flatly: "The only country attacking civilian mariners transiting regional waters is Iran." Separately, Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz threatened to seize more territory in southern Lebanon if Hezbollah joins Iran's fight, and Saudi Arabia launched new strikes on Houthi forces as the Yemen front stayed active in parallel.

🌟 Top Story: Ivanti Connect Secure Zero-Day Gives Root-Level Access to Fully Patched VPN Appliances CVE-2026-104935 · EXPLOITED 10 DAYS PRE-PATCH

CISA issued an urgent warning after researchers confirmed a sophisticated exploit chain against Ivanti Connect Secure, tracked as CVE-2026-104935, that combines an authentication bypass, remote code execution and a privilege-escalation flaw to achieve unauthenticated root-level code execution — on appliances that were already fully patched against prior issues. Security firm Volexity, which first identified the chain, found post-exploitation activity specifically targeting Active Directory credentials and secrets, including LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys — exactly the material an attacker needs to pivot deep into a victim's domain. Threat intelligence firm Defused corroborated the timeline and attributed exploitation to a threat actor dubbed UTA0576, which has been actively using the flaw since October 3 — a full ten days before Ivanti shipped any fix. Ivanti patched two of the three chained vulnerabilities on October 13, but the third component, the privilege-escalation bug that converts file access into root, remains unpatched as of today. CISA added CVE-2026-104935 to its KEV catalog on October 13 with an October 16 remediation deadline for federal agencies. This lands in the same week as Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday, which fixed a record 1,413 CVEs — 1,102 of them in Windows alone — including an actively exploited Windows Shell zero-day (CVE-2026-96951, CVSS 8.8) that bypasses Mark of the Web protections, and a .NET Framework security flaw (CVE-2026-108398).

If you run Ivanti Connect Secure, assume compromise is possible even on fully patched instances until the third (privilege-escalation) component is fixed — hunt for LAPS/BitLocker credential access and anomalous AD activity now, not after a patch exists.
