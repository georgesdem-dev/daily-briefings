🛢️ Tech Steadies, but Dow Sheds 652 Points as Oil Tops $100 on Iran Fears

Friday capped a rocky week with a mixed finish: the Nasdaq Composite essentially stabilized, slipping just 0.21% to 25,955.34 after Thursday's 2.60% rout, while the Dow dropped a sharp 652.82 points (-1.23%) to 52,215.16 as Thursday's tech-led selloff broadened into cyclicals and financials. The S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,623.68. The bigger story was crude: WTI topped $100 a barrel and Brent surged to roughly $116.91 — its highest level since late April* — after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia revived fears that the U.S.-Iran conflict could flare up again.

Markets are closed through the weekend; Monday's open will be the first real test of whether Thursday's AI-capex jitters and Friday's broadening selloff were a one-week scare or the start of something more durable. Alphabet reports Oct 27-28 and Amazon on Oct 29 — both now even more consequential after this week's volatility.

🔄 Rotation Reverses: Energy and Tech Lead, Staples and Industrials Lag

Friday's sector leadership flipped from Thursday's defensive rotation. Energy jumped 2.49% — hitting a record high intraday — as the Brent spike to a 6-month high lifted producers broadly. Technology bounced 0.97% as chip names found some footing after Thursday's rout, and Real Estate added 0.31%. On the other side, Consumer Staples gave back 1.64% of Thursday's 3.31% surge, Industrials fell 1.26%, and Financials slipped 1.06% as the 10Y yield held near 5.00%.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Bank of America Securities — Jill Carey Hall

Record $14.8B Single-Week Stock Sale Signals a Broadening "AI Stampede"

BofA's private-client data shows individual investors sold a record $14.8B of single stocks in one week, while buying just $2.8B of equity ETFs — described as a "multibillion-dollar stampede" out of AI-exposed names that is spreading into broader sectors beyond chips and neoclouds. The framing worth noting: the near-term risk may be less about the AI bubble "bursting" and more about the rush to escape it — a self-reinforcing selling pressure that can outpace any actual deterioration in AI fundamentals. Separately, short sellers have booked roughly $6B in paper profits this month betting against neocloud/data-center names (CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN, Applied Digital, Core Scientific), with short interest running 20-26% of free float across the group — a reminder those gains are unrealized and can reverse quickly.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"Inflection Bubble," Not a Confirmed One — Moderation Over Conviction

Marks continues to resist calling the AI boom a classic bubble outright, instead describing an "inflection bubble" shaped by over $1B in seed funding flowing to unproven startups and heavy corporate debt tied to infrastructure buildout. His standing advice remains relevant after a week like this one: no one should go all-in without acknowledging the risk of ruin, but no one should go all-out and miss a genuine technological step forward either. "A moderate position, applied with selectivity and prudence, seems like the best approach" — exactly the stance this portfolio's defensive/growth split is built around.

Goldman Sachs — Still Bullish on 2026, Even After a Volatile Week

8,000 S&P Target Unchanged; October Conviction List (AMZN, OXY, HII, JCI, Burlington) Holds

Goldman's year-end S&P 500 target of 8,000 (raised from 7,600 earlier this year, on the back of a $340 2026 EPS forecast and 24% earnings growth) hasn't been walked back despite this week's tech-driven pullback and Friday's rotation reversal. The desk's view that earnings growth — not multiple expansion — drives the next leg higher faces its next real test with Alphabet (Oct 27-28) and Amazon (Oct 29) reporting. The October Conviction List, including this week's energy standout OXY and defense pick HII, remains the anchor for this portfolio's picks.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

"Classic Bubble" Warning Still the Backdrop

Dalio's early-October remarks that AI is a "classic bubble" nearing its bursting point — driven by rising rates colliding with roughly $570B of AI-linked debt issuance in 2026, more than double 2025's total — remain the backdrop against which this week's volatility and BofA's "stampede" data should be read. He flagged two triggers: continued rate pressure, and forced liquidation of unrealized gains. The 10Y holding near 5.00% rather than falling further suggests the rate-pressure trigger hasn't materially eased even as equities stabilized Friday.
