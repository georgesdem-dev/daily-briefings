📊 Market Snapshot — Week in Review LIVE DATA

📉 Second Straight Down Week as AI-Capex Fears and Iran Risk Collide

Stocks closed out a second consecutive losing week: the S&P 500 fell 0.43% and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.76% week-over-week, driven largely by Thursday's 2.60% Nasdaq plunge — its steepest since June 5 — on renewed AI-spending concerns. Smaller caps diverged sharply, with the Russell 2000 rallying nearly 2% on the week as money rotated away from the most AI-exposed megacaps. Crude was the week's other big mover: Brent closed Friday at $116.72, its highest close since April, and WTI topped $103, after reports that the U.S. and Israel are planning new strikes on Iran following the midterms.

The AI-capex jitters are no longer a U.S.-only story. Goldman Sachs cut its 12-month Kospi target by 25% (to 9,000 from 12,000) on mounting AI-investment concerns after the Korean benchmark fell more than 35% from its June peak, and CoreWeave sank to its lowest level since shortly after its IPO as its bond yields jumped to as high as 15% — a sign credit markets are starting to price AI-infrastructure risk more aggressively than equity markets had been.

🔄 Sector Leadership for the Week: Energy and Healthcare Out Front

Energy was the week's standout, gaining roughly 1.6% and extending its leadership for 2026 as the Iran-risk premium built through the week. Health Care followed, aided by UnitedHealth and Johnson & Johnson's clean earnings beats. Real Estate, Utilities, Materials, Consumer Staples, Financials and Consumer Discretionary rounded out the week's sector order — a markedly different ranking than the single-day swings seen Thursday (defensive rotation) and Friday (energy/tech bounce), underscoring how much day-to-day noise obscured the week's real signal: a steady bid for energy and quality healthcare names.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio & Hedge Fund Titans

Growing Consensus That the AI Trade Is "Flirting With Bubble Conditions"

Dalio and a widening circle of billionaire hedge-fund managers are increasingly convinced the stock market is flirting with bubble conditions tied to the AI trade, with more professional investors coming around to that view each week. This week's evidence stacked up: Goldman's 25% Kospi target cut, CoreWeave's slide to post-IPO lows with bond yields at 15%, and Meta's 7% post-earnings drop on its first-ever negative free-cash-flow quarter all point the same direction — credit and global markets are starting to price AI-infrastructure risk more aggressively than headline equity indices have.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Unconvinced on "Bubble," But Newly Candid About Rising Risk

Marks remains the holdout among major voices, still unconvinced the AI trade meets the bar of a classic bubble. But his tone has shifted more cautious this week: he notes the U.S. market has been in a phase of optimism for nearly four years, and that in such stretches "fear recedes, skepticism evaporates, and borrower screening standards are lowered... replaced by a willingness to act, excitement, and the fear of missing out." That's a notably more pointed warning than his earlier "inflection bubble, moderate position" framing, even as he stops short of calling a top.

Goldman Sachs — Domestic Conviction Holds Even as Asia Target Gets Cut

8,000 S&P Target Unchanged at Home; Kospi Target Slashed 25% Abroad

Goldman's U.S. year-end S&P 500 target of 8,000 hasn't been walked back, but the desk's 25% cut to its Kospi target (to 9,000 from 12,000, after a 35%+ decline from June's peak) shows AI-capex anxiety is being priced very differently across regions. The October Conviction List, including this week's standouts OXY and HII, remains the anchor for this portfolio's picks — but the Kospi move is a reminder that not every market is treating this week's volatility as a buying opportunity.

The Week Ahead — Fed Decision Takes Center Stage

FOMC Decision Wednesday Oct 28; Markets Price ~80% Odds of a Hold

The Fed's rate decision lands Wednesday, October 28 at 2pm ET, landing in the same 48 hours as Alphabet's earnings. Odds of an October hike have dropped below 22% after weak September payrolls data, with fixed-income markets now pricing roughly an 80% chance the Fed holds steady and pushes any hike to the December 9 meeting instead. A hold would remove one source of near-term rate uncertainty just as markets digest GOOGL and AMZN results — though it leaves the question of a December hike, and its interaction with the AI-capex debate, unresolved.
