🛢️ Hormuz Standoff Hardens — Iran Rejects Quick Reopening

Iran's parliament speaker Qalibaf said Sunday the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until seven conditions tied to a June "Islamabad" US-Iran framework are met, and Trump is reported to have rejected Tehran's proposed 7-day reopening roadmap floated at the UN General Assembly. Transit through the strait remains near-collapsed (around 1% of normal traffic), keeping WTI near $90 and Brent above $102. Backchannel diplomacy continues via Qatar, but a swift resolution looks unlikely this week.

📋 ISM Services Holds Expansion Streak, Cools Slightly

September's ISM Services PMI came in at 54.9%, extending the sector's expansion to 27 straight months but down from August's 55.4%. The read was the first look at the services economy since Friday's weak jobs report, and the modest cooling — not a contraction — kept the "soft landing" narrative intact without materially shifting Fed-pause expectations.

📈 Friday's Jobs-Driven Rally Still Setting the Tone

September nonfarm payrolls rose just 29,000 versus ~90,000 expected, with unemployment ticking up to 4.2% and July/August both revised down. Odds of an October Fed hike collapsed to 21% from 64% the prior week. Nvidia hit a fresh intraday record ($237.88, market cap past $5.7T) on the rate-relief trade. That optimism is carrying into today's session for the S&P and Nasdaq, though the Dow is lagging as cyclicals digest the Hormuz overhang.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs — Macro & Research

Bullish Into Earnings Season, With a Short List of Buys

Goldman's house view remains that earnings growth should keep lifting US equities toward a year-end S&P 500 target of 7,600–8,000, on ~24% projected 2026 EPS growth. Ahead of Q3 reporting, the desk flagged UPS, Omnicom, Nu Holdings, Baker Hughes and Disney as buying opportunities, and reinstated a Buy on Baker Hughes following its Chart Industries deal close.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

A New Chairman, a Busier Checkbook

Buffett formally stepped down as chairman in September, handing the role to his son Howard Buffett (Greg Abel has run the CEO seat since January). Berkshire has kept adding to its Lennar stake — now 11.2% — and resumed buybacks this year, on pace for more than $10B in repurchases after sitting out 2025. Berkshire's own stock has lagged the market YTD, keeping pressure on Abel to put the roughly $360B cash pile to work.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Mispricing, Not Prediction, Is the Edge

Fresh from Oaktree's 2026 client conference, Marks reiterated his core thesis: "What we do is take advantage of the mistakes of others." On sentiment, he noted markets tend to run on one-sided narratives — "good news is incorporated and causes price to go higher, bad news is ignored" — a dynamic worth watching given how quickly Friday's weak jobs print was reframed as good news for stocks.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Don't Sell the Bubble, But Expect a Low-Return Decade

Dalio's standing caution on AI-related valuations — "don't sell just because there's a bubble," but brace for muted returns over the next decade — continues to frame his broader warning about a potential "capital war," where geopolitical tension weaponizes capital flows and foreign Treasury demand. No fresh remarks surfaced this week, but the thesis remains relevant with the 10Y still elevated near 5.25%.
