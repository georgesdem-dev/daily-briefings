Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi described the days-long, Qatari-mediated indirect talks between Tehran and Washington as "very intense," but by Monday the negotiations had ended without any significant movement toward a deal to end the war. Iranian negotiators had been in Doha over the weekend pressing a proposal that would have the US lift its blockade on Iranian ports and waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales in exchange for Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz and halting attacks on shipping — with Araghchi having offered Saturday to compress the implementation window from seven days to just two. But a senior Iranian official told Reuters that Qatar had brought only "unacceptable" ideas, and government spokesman Baghaei reiterated that Tehran's underlying position — that nuclear-program talks can only resume once Iran verifies all its own conditions have been met — remains unchanged regardless of how quickly any interim deal might be compressed. No new US or Israeli strikes on Iranian territory were reported over the weekend, but Iranian-linked projectiles kept striking shipping near Hormuz, including the Qatari LNG carrier hit Friday. The clock now shifts to Washington: President Trump's pledge not to order new strikes before Tuesday's US midterm elections expires tomorrow, November 3, making the Strait the main kinetic flashpoint to watch regardless of whether the diplomatic track revives.

🌟 Top Story: Microsoft Confirms North Korean Hackers Have "Operationalized" AI Across Their Attack Workflow 3 GROUPS AT TOP TIER · BINANCE-LINKED ACTOR AMONG THEM

Microsoft Threat Intelligence released a report on October 30 assessing the AI adoption maturity of eight North Korea-linked hacking groups across a four-stage scale, finding that three — Jade Sleet, Coral Sleet and Jasper Sleet — have reached the top "Operationalized" tier, meaning AI is now integrated into their daily attack workflow rather than used occasionally. A further four groups sit in the "Accelerating" stage and one in "Expanding." The groups use AI to generate phishing PDFs — one group alone sent more than 100 AI-generated phishing documents to South Korean targets this year — develop malware, and spoof identities, and Microsoft's analysis suggests they may be leaning on mainstream AI coding tools such as Cursor to help generate, debug and evade detection for malicious code. Notably, two Reconnaissance General Bureau-affiliated groups, Onyx Sleet (known in South Korea as Andariel) and Pearl Sleet, have actively used the open-source ARTEX AI penetration-testing framework in real-world operations — the same tool CrowdStrike attributed to the China-linked "Vapor Panda" campaign against 200+ organizations reported yesterday, underscoring how quickly agentic offensive tooling is spreading across unrelated state-linked actor groups. Microsoft's assessment is that while AI has meaningfully sped up these groups' operations, none has yet reached full attack autonomy. Separately, cloud application-hosting platform Northflank confirmed a breach beginning October 30 that let an unauthorized party access and abuse customers' AI API keys — including keys for OpenAI and Anthropic services — alongside database passwords, cloud credentials and authentication tokens; Northflank is telling customers to assume all stored secrets were decrypted and to rotate everything immediately.

ARTEX now appears in both a Chinese state-linked campaign (reported yesterday) and North Korean operations (per Microsoft) — the same AI-powered penetration-testing framework being adopted across unrelated state actors in the span of two weeks is a strong signal that agentic offensive tooling has become commoditized infrastructure, not a bespoke capability limited to one nation-state's toolkit.

🌟 Top Story: Publishers Report Click-Through Collapses of Up to 89% as Google's AI Overviews Expand 25.2% → 2.8% DESKTOP CTR · DMG MEDIA

DMG Media, the publisher behind the Daily Mail and Metro, told the UK's Competition and Markets Authority that its desktop click-through rate collapsed from 25.2% to just 2.8% — an 89% drop — on searches where users were shown a Google AI Overview instead of a standard results page. The figure is one of the starkest data points yet in a pattern publishers have been flagging for over a year: Google search traffic to publishers fell 33% globally in the twelve months to November 2025, and the "zero-click rate" for news-related searches — where users get their answer without visiting any site — rose to 69% in the year after AI Overviews launched broadly. Reddit's CEO has separately said Google's AI Mode and AI Overviews are now the primary driver of declining search-referral traffic to that platform. The commercial logic is straightforward and, for publishers, alarming: AI Overviews answer the query using content scraped from the same sites losing the clicks, generating no corresponding referral revenue or ad impressions in return. Separately, SemiAnalysis launched GDPx on November 2 — a new frontier AI benchmark spanning 11 high-value domains including chip design, cybersecurity, biology, legal and quantitative trading, aimed at giving enterprises a more commercially relevant yardstick than general-knowledge benchmarks. Apple also confirmed its first Apple Intelligence features for the Chinese market, with developer testing beginning November 4 ahead of a December rollout alongside iOS 27.3.
