🌟 Top Story: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Zero-Day — Fifth of the Year CVE-2026-76504 · CVSS 9.8

Cisco disclosed active exploitation of CVE-2026-76504, an authentication-bypass zero-day in Catalyst SD-WAN Manager — the fifth Cisco zero-day exploited in the wild this year. The flaw stems from improper URI-encoding handling in HTTP requests, letting unauthenticated attackers reach a restricted admin API endpoint; Cisco's published indicators of compromise show attackers using %6a-encoded characters in malicious requests to bypass authentication entirely. All Catalyst SD-WAN Manager deployments are affected regardless of configuration, and there are no workarounds — only patching (versions 26.2.1, 26.1.2.1, 20.18.4.1, 20.15.6.1, 20.12.8.2, and 20.9.10.1) closes the hole. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a federal remediation deadline of October 3. Separately, government and finance organizations remain under sustained attack via the Citrix NetScaler zero-days (CVE-2026-88771/88772) disclosed last week, with dozens of organizations hit including suspected state-sponsored actors, and Apple's CoreGraphics zero-day (CVE-2026-86950) continues to be tied to a highly targeted espionage-style campaign against specific individuals rather than broad exploitation.

Admin-level compromise with zero workarounds and a 48-hour federal deadline makes this a same-week patch, not a next-sprint item — especially for networks managing SD-WAN fleets centrally, where one compromised manager instance cascades across every connected site.

🌟 Top Story: Tech Giants Sign "Morally Binding" AI Safety Pact at the White House VOLUNTARY · NO ENFORCEMENT

OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, Anthropic, and xAI signed a voluntary AI safety agreement with the Trump administration on September 29, with Trump calling it "almost like a constitution" for the industry. Signatories — including OpenAI President Greg Brockman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, Google CEO Sundar Pichai, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang — committed to strengthening internal safeguards, using independent external auditors, and taking steps to prevent AI systems from accessing computer systems in unintended ways. Trump said he's considering a 10-person federal board to police AI tool safety, though he named no potential members. Crucially, the pact has no enforcement mechanism and doesn't require companies to publish or even name their auditors — a stark contrast with the FTC's newly opened formal probe into OpenAI, Anthropic, and METR over rogue AI agents (disclosed Sept 30), which does carry the threat of compelled testimony and information demands. The juxtaposition is the story: industry self-policing announced one day, binding federal investigation confirmed the next.

A voluntary, unenforceable pact signed the same week the FTC opened a formal information-demand probe tells you where real leverage sits — treat the pact as a PR and goodwill signal, not a compliance framework, when assessing vendor AI risk.
