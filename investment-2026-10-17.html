📊 Market Snapshot — The Week That Was (Oct 12-16) LIVE DATA

🏦 Bank Earnings Kickoff: Mixed Results, Mixed Reactions

JPMorgan missed on EPS ($5.57 on $50.2B revenue/$15.3B net income) — a rare stumble — but raised 2026 net-interest-income guidance to $106.2B, and the stock still rose ~1%. Goldman Sachs was the standout: EPS of $17.01 crushed the ~$13.09 consensus on a big equities-trading beat, sending shares up ~4%. Wells Fargo beat on both lines (EPS $1.87, revenue $21.9B) but fell ~3% anyway — a reaction decoupled from the headline numbers that suggests the market is pricing in something beyond the print (likely credit-quality or expense-guidance concerns). Citigroup delivered the cleanest quarter — revenue +8% to $23.8B, net income +40%, EPS +53% YoY to $2.85 — yet shares were roughly flat. Net takeaway: bank fundamentals held up fine; the stock reactions tell you sentiment, not earnings quality, is driving this tape.

🤖 AI/Semis Selloff Wipes Out the Week's Gains

Wednesday's hot September CPI print kicked off a tech-led unwind that accelerated into Friday: semiconductors (SOXX) fell ~8% on the week (-4% Friday alone), memory names dropped ~5.5%, and CoreWeave fell as much as 15% mid-week and another 12% Friday on concerns about how its AI-infrastructure buildout is financed with debt. The "Magnificent Seven" basket lost ~2% on the day, with Meta down ~4% and Nvidia off ~1.5%; Apple was the lone name to finish Friday positive. The underlying worry, echoed by Ray Dalio this week, is that AI capex is increasingly debt-funded rather than cash-funded — a leverage risk that wasn't priced in through the summer rally.

🇮🇷 Hormuz Escalates Sharply — Brent Spikes Toward $110

The shadow conflict in the Strait of Hormuz intensified materially this week: at least 9 commercial ships were attacked since last Sunday, 3 seafarers injured and 2 missing, and reports emerged of a strike on Saudi Aramco facilities. Hormuz crude flows have fallen to roughly 9.4M barrels/day — about 70% of the pre-war average. Oil spiked in response: WTI +~10% on the week to ~$101.41, Brent briefly touching $110. This is a clear escalation from the "wartime high" flagged in last week's brief — there has been no de-escalation since.

💵 Yields Ease as Money Rotates to Safety — Not a Trend Reversal

The 10Y Treasury fell to roughly 5.09-5.11% (sources vary slightly) from last week's ~5.23-5.24%, reflecting safe-haven demand during the equity selloff rather than a change in the underlying rate trajectory. Fed-funds futures now price ~87-89% odds of a hold at the Oct 27-28 FOMC meeting. Ray Dalio published a pointed note this week arguing the US shows the two classic signs of a country "going broke" — excess debt/equity supply versus demand, and debt-service costs outgrowing income — and called the broader bond selloff a genuine "bond bear market," not a blip, with a potential debt crisis window of ~3 years absent policy change.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights — This Week MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs — Macro Research

Upgraded Equities to Overweight — Just Before the Selloff

Goldman's strategy desk (Christian Mueller-Glissmann) upgraded global equities to overweight from neutral on Oct 12-13, citing strong Q3 earnings and improved post-pullback valuations, with a "barbell" of global tech plus rate-sensitive/bond-proxy names. US and Asia overweight, Europe underweight. The call landed just before the Wed-Fri tech rout — a tension worth watching into next week's hyperscaler earnings. GDP tracking remains strong (US 3.4%, China 4%).

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Most Gambling Mood" He's Seen — Yet Still Buying Selectively

Buffett reiterated his valuation-caution theme, saying markets are in "the most gambling mood" he's observed, and that Berkshire's ~$365.5B cash pile wasn't moved meaningfully by the recent pullback since it "wasn't big enough." Yet Berkshire disclosed a fresh ~$37.4B Alphabet stake this week — now its third-largest holding — showing selective buying continues even amid broad caution. His September farewell letter handed confidence to incoming CEO Greg Abel and Chairman Howard Buffett.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

Two Classic Signs of a Country "Going Broke" — And an AI-Debt Bubble Warning

In a note published mid-week, Dalio argued the US exhibits the two classic signs of a country going broke: excess debt/equity supply versus demand, and debt-service costs outgrowing income. He calls the bond selloff a genuine "bond bear market," warning of a potential debt crisis within ~3 years absent policy change. Separately, he flagged that AI-fueled corporate borrowing (net US borrowing projected to rise from ~$3.5T in 2025 to ~$4.4T in 2027) could be the mechanism that pops the AI bubble, as debt and new share issuance increasingly fund data-center buildouts.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

Rising Yields Are Rational — But Watch for Complacency

Marks maintains that higher long yields reflect sticky inflation, a ~6%-of-GDP deficit, and AI capex demand — not a dysfunction requiring intervention (his "ice pack on a fever" critique of yield-curve control). He supports Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's push for quieter, less forward-guidance-heavy policy, though notes that could mean more near-term bond/rate-sensitive-equity volatility, not less. Separately flags the market has been "run by optimists since late 2022," with the S&P more than doubling since — a complacency watch-flag, not an explicit sell call.
