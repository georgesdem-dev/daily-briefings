🌟 Top Story: Two Italian Technicians Killed by Falling Drone Debris in Second Iranian Strike on Kuwait This Month NATO-COUNTRY CASUALTIES · KUWAIT STRUCK AGAIN

Two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia were killed Thursday in Kuwait when falling shrapnel from Iranian drones intercepted near an air-defence surveillance station struck their position; Kuwait's Defence Ministry said a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national were also wounded. It is the second reported Iranian attack on the US-allied Gulf state this month and the first episode of the war to kill citizens of a NATO member on Gulf soil. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni confirmed the deaths and offered condolences on X; Iran's armed forces spokesman Abolfazl Shekarchi denied any Iranian operation took place, saying "no military or offensive operations whatsoever have been conducted" in the region. The strike lands one day after Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said two more oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on an "unauthorised route," with army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia warning that vessels transiting without coordinating with Iranian forces face escalating risk — effectively formalizing a live minefield as policy. Tehran has offered, via Qatari mediators, to reopen the strait within 48 hours of Washington lifting its naval blockade on Iranian ports and meeting the rest of its seven conditions, but no direct US-Iran channel exists; Qatar and Pakistan remain limited to carrying messages. Compounding the diplomatic drift, reporting indicates Trump told Vance the administration does not need a deal with Iran right now and redirected him toward domestic priorities after midterm losses — a signal that the White House's appetite for reviving talks, which Qatar's PM pushed for this week with Rubio and Vance, may be cooling even as attacks on shipping and now Gulf allies' territory escalate.

🌟 Top Story: 1Password Discloses Breach via Hijacked Employee GitHub Account — Source Code and Customer Data Stolen PASSWORD MANAGER BREACHED · VAULTS SAFE

1Password disclosed that an attacker gained access to a corporate GitHub account belonging to an employee starting around November 19 and used it to pull source code from private repositories, reach the company's CI/CD system, and download customer account records — names, emails, and account metadata, but not vault contents. 1Password says its source code does not contain user secrets and that passwords and other vault data remain secure; the company disabled the compromised account, revoked the stolen token, hardened GitHub/CI/CD and endpoint controls, and rotated internal credentials. The incident is a reminder that even security-focused vendors remain exposed through ordinary developer-account compromise rather than product flaws. Separately, two maximum-severity infrastructure zero-days are under active exploitation with a Friday, November 27 federal patch deadline: F5's BIG-IP APM flaw (CVE-2026-94127, CVSS 9.8) allows remote code execution against instances configured as an OAuth Authorization Server, and Arista's VeloCloud Orchestrator flaw (CVE-2026-93952, CVSS 10.0) stems from improper input validation in certificate-based Edge-to-Orchestrator authentication; a second Arista VeloCloud SQL-injection privilege-escalation bug (CVE-2026-114625) is also exploited. CISA has added CVE-2026-94127 to its KEV catalog. Rounding out the day: the MONAI Label AI-discovered zero-day (CVE-2026-133968) hits its own KEV remediation deadline today; and Drupal disclosed CVE-2026-136716, a follow-on installer access-bypass flaw that can still allow site takeover on roughly 31,500 internet-facing sites even after last week's SA-CORE-2026-017 patch.

🌟 Top Story: FCC Clears SpaceX's "Starmind" — Up to 1 Million AI Data Center Satellites in Orbit ORBITAL AI COMPUTE · 1M-SATELLITE LICENSE

The FCC's Space Bureau authorized SpaceX to deploy a constellation of up to one million solar-powered "Starmind" satellites designed to function as orbital AI data centers — processing AI workloads in space using solar power and relaying results to Earth via Starlink. The license was granted over petitions to deny from Amazon's Kuiper Systems, Viasat, and the American Astronomical Society (the latter concerned about light pollution and orbital debris); regulators found SpaceX's debris-mitigation plan compliant only for satellites below 600km, a problem for four of the six planned orbital shells, which range from 500 to 1,002 km. SpaceX must launch its first satellite by November 2033 and hit 10%/50%/100% deployment milestones through 2040 or the license lapses — a long runway, but the approval itself reframes the AI infrastructure race as now extending into orbit, competing for capital and attention against terrestrial hyperscale buildouts. Elsewhere in AI: OpenAI began merging ChatGPT's "Chat" and "Work" usage into a single shared meter for a small group of Pro users, with heavy users warned they may hit limits sooner even as Instant-model conversations stay uncapped; broader rollout is planned for December. Anthropic published a detailed account of how it rewrote its Messages API — the front door to every Claude request — from Python to Rust with Claude writing most of the code, cutting CPU per request by roughly 70% and memory by over 80% with zero downtime during the full production cutover (completed by September). Anthropic also shipped installable Mac and Windows desktop apps for Claude artifacts.
