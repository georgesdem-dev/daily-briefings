🌟 Top Story: Iran Asserts De Facto Control Over Who Transits the Strait 11 TANKERS HIT THIS WEEK

Diplomatically, the standoff remains frozen — Iran continues to condition any de-escalation on a return to the collapsed June memorandum of understanding and US endorsement of the already-finalized Iran-Oman arrangement, neither of which Washington has moved on.

🌟 Top Story: GitLab Patches Maximum-Severity Unauthenticated RCE in CI/CD Pipeline Engine CVE-2026-97229 · CVSS 10.0

GitLab shipped an emergency patch on Monday for CVE-2026-97229, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in the regex compiler used by its CI/CD pipeline engine. The bug is an integer overflow that can be triggered through a crafted pipeline configuration, letting an attacker with no credentials at all achieve code execution on affected self-managed instances — one of the few CVSS 10.0 ratings assigned to GitLab in its history. The flaw affects self-managed Community and Enterprise Edition installations from version 18.1 up to 19.4.1; GitLab.com's SaaS offering was already protected and did not require customer action. Fixes landed in 19.4.2, 19.3.4 and 19.2.8, released same-day as the advisory, which credits an external researcher (handle "joaxcar") who reported the flaw through GitLab's HackerOne bug bounty program. Within hours of disclosure, CyberScoop reported internet-wide scanning activity consistent with attackers probing for unpatched instances, though no confirmed successful exploitation has been reported yet. Given GitLab's role as a CI/CD and source-code backbone for a huge share of enterprise software pipelines, an unauthenticated RCE at this severity is a textbook "patch today, not this sprint" situation.

If you run GitLab CE/EE self-managed between 18.1 and 19.4.1, patch to 19.4.2 / 19.3.4 / 19.2.8 immediately — scanning activity has already started, and an unauthenticated RCE on your CI/CD pipeline engine is a direct path to source-code and secrets compromise.

🌟 Top Story: AMD Acquires Fei-Fei Li's World Labs for $8.2B, Installs Her as Chief Scientist ALL-STOCK · CLOSING END OF 2026

AMD announced an all-stock acquisition of World Labs, the "spatial intelligence" AI lab co-founded by Stanford computer scientist and former Google Cloud chief AI scientist Fei-Fei Li, in a deal valuing the startup at $8.2 billion. World Labs has focused on models that reason about 3D space and the physical world — a different bet from the text- and chat-centric frontier labs — generating interactive 3D environments from a single image or prompt. Under the deal, Dr. Li joins AMD as Executive Vice President and Chief Scientist, reporting directly to CEO Lisa Su, and will lead AMD's push into spatial and embodied AI alongside its existing silicon business. The acquisition is expected to close by the end of 2026, pending customary approvals. It is AMD's largest and highest-profile AI-talent acquisition to date, signaling the chipmaker wants to own research and model capability, not just supply the hardware underneath Nvidia's and others' AI stacks — a strategic shift that puts AMD in more direct competition with the frontier labs it currently counts as customers.
