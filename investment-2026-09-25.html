🖥️ Oracle Force Majeure Cracks the AI-Capex-Financing Story

Oracle invoked force majeure on its $165B Project Jupiter data center (New Mexico) after a denied energy permit left the project's debt — held by developer Blue Owl Capital — trading below 90 cents on the dollar. Oracle shares fell 4%. This is a notable crack in the "AI infrastructure spend is unstoppable" narrative: financing and power-permitting bottlenecks are becoming real constraints, not just supply-chain ones. Worth watching for read-through to MSFT/NVDA capex commentary at upcoming earnings.

🪖 Trump Hosts Xi, Iran Signals Conditional Hormuz Reopening

Trump is hosting Xi Jinping this week and pressing China to help pressure Iran into a resolution. Separately, Iranian officials have signaled — per Kyodo News — that Iran would fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the US eases its naval blockade of Iranian ports. That's a real, if fragile, diplomatic opening; oil's continued rise Thursday shows markets aren't yet pricing it as done.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Goldman Sachs & JPMorgan — Rate Path Firming Up

Convergence Building Around a Second Fed Hike

Goldman Sachs now expects a second 25bp hike, a shift from its earlier view that September would be the only move this cycle — aligning it more closely with JPMorgan's higher-for-longer stance. This convergence is a key driver behind this week's yield spike and helps explain the market's defensive rotation.

Ed Yardeni — Independent Strategist

Cuts Year-End S&P Target from 8,400 to 7,900

Yardeni trimmed his year-end S&P 500 target citing more-than-one-hike risk and higher-for-longer oil pushing bond yields up further, with growing risk that inflation becomes entrenched given the economy's resilience. A useful, more cautious counterweight to Goldman's still-standing 8,000 call — the range between the two brackets this week's key uncertainty.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Cash Discipline Looking Increasingly Prescient

Buffett's ongoing caution about speculative AI-capex spending and stretched valuations looks better-timed by the day, with Oracle's force-majeure news the latest evidence that not all AI infrastructure bets are as bulletproof as the market has priced. Berkshire's near-$400B cash pile remains dry powder for exactly this kind of volatility.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"We Should Be Careful" — Now Playing Out in Real Time

Marks' recent warning that risk-taking is elevated and prudence isn't in the ascendancy is being validated this week: rising yields, an AI-capex financing crack (Oracle), and elevated oil are combining into the first genuinely multi-factor risk-off stretch since this brief began. His prescription — quality and income over speculative breadth — is the operative playbook right now.
