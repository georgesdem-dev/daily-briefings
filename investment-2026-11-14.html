📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

👔 Thursday: Nvidia's Board Ousts Jensen Huang

Nvidia's board announced Huang's resignation as President, CEO, and board member effective immediately, following the governance probe opened Nov 8 into his alleged relationship with a Chinese national U.S. intelligence assessed as a Ministry of State Security recruitment target — plus separate allegations involving a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. CFO Colette Kress, a 13-year Nvidia veteran, becomes interim CEO; Harvey Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair. The board says operations and the product roadmap continue uninterrupted and a permanent-successor search (internal and external candidates) is underway — Trump has publicly floated Tim Cook. NVDA plunged as much as -14% intraday Thursday before closing -4.3%, while AMD and Broadcom rallied on hopes of picking up market share. Market breadth was notably weak: only 123 of 500 S&P members rose. The S&P 500 closed -0.4% to 7,592.40, Nasdaq -0.39% to 25,366.09, Russell 2000 -1.66% to 2,799.06 (the week's weakest index).

This followed a strong Wednesday Nov 11 rebound (S&P +1.31% to 7,619.38, Nasdaq +1.60% to 25,466.19) led by Apple hitting a fresh all-time closing high — the session before Huang's ouster became public.

📉 Friday: Yields Spike, Gold Sinks, Tech Drags

Friday Nov 13 closed mixed: the Dow eked out a +0.1% gain to 52,347.55 (the only index up on the day) while the S&P 500 fell -0.4% to 7,561.67 and the Nasdaq dropped -0.91% to 25,136.27 as the Nvidia leadership turmoil kept pressure on chipmakers and data-center names — AMD and Broadcom gave back part of their post-Huang pop. Despite the tech drag, breadth was actually positive (314 S&P names up vs. 185 down) as money rotated into staples, communication services, and energy. The bigger story was the bond market: the 10-year Treasury yield jumped to ~5.14-5.15%, its steepest weekly rise since April 2025, driven by a firmer-than-expected October PPI print and renewed Iran tension after Trump said he's no longer interested in a deal with Tehran. Gold fell a further -2.5% to ~$3,881 as the firmer dollar and higher yields made it costlier to hold. For the week overall, all eleven S&P sectors finished higher — Energy (+5.37%), Communications (+3.72%), Industrials (+3.73%), and Technology (+3.3%) led — as the S&P gained +2.51%, the Nasdaq +2.27%, and the Dow +2.07%, snapping the index's four-week losing streak.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

"Bond Bear Market" Thesis Meets a Real Governance Shock

Dalio's October view — that stocks can keep climbing even as bond yields rise because earnings growth remains strong enough — got tested directly this week as the 10-year's steepest weekly jump since April 2025 coincided with the Nvidia leadership crisis. He has reiterated the market sits roughly "80% of the way" toward classic bubble conditions (echoing 1929 and 2000) but continues to advise against selling purely on bubble recognition, estimating long-run equity returns near 4.7% — modest, but not a call to exit.

Corporate Governance — The Huang Succession

A 33-Year CEO Tenure Ends Overnight

Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney called removing Huang "an Apple firing Steve Jobs level of idiocy," while Nvidia's new board chair Harvey Jones insisted operations, customer commitments, and the product roadmap continue without interruption under interim CEO Colette Kress. The split reaction — a 14% intraday crash followed by a swift halving of losses — suggests the market hasn't yet decided whether this is a buying opportunity or the start of a prolonged overhang. Tuesday Nov 17's Q3 FY27 earnings call is the first real test of investor confidence in Nvidia under new leadership.
