🔴 Breaking: Jensen Huang Resigns from Nvidia — The AI Era's Defining Leadership Shock

The week's dominant story: Jensen Huang, co-founder and CEO of Nvidia for 33 years, submitted his resignation on November 12 after the board launched an investigation into an extramarital affair with a Singapore-based data center executive, raising concerns about potential conflicts of interest related to Megaspeed — a company involved in Nvidia chip routing amid US export controls. CFO Colette Kress has been appointed interim CEO while the board conducts a permanent search.

Market impact today: NVDA -2.0% (market cap slipped below $4 trillion for the first time since 2025), AMD -8.75%, Broadcom (AVGO) -6.2%, Arista Networks (ANET) -4.5%. The selloff reflects deep investor anxiety about whether Nvidia's extraordinary culture, speed-to-market, and ecosystem dominance were inseparably tied to Huang's singular vision. His resignation comes as the company navigates escalating US–China chip export restrictions and the transition from H100 to Blackwell Ultra architecture.

The bigger question: Is this a generational buying opportunity in AI infrastructure, or the beginning of a leadership vacuum that cedes ground to AMD, Intel, and emerging custom-silicon players like Amazon's Trainium and Google's TPU? Analysts are split. Goldman Sachs downgraded NVDA to Neutral today; Wedbush maintained Outperform and called it a "once-in-a-decade entry point." For moderate-risk portfolios, the message is clear: avoid adding to chip exposure today; wait for succession clarity.

📉 10Y Treasury Breaks 5.15% — Gold's Worst Week in 5 Months

The other major story of the week: US 10-year yields surged 29 basis points to 5.15%, their steepest weekly jump since April 2025. The catalyst: stronger-than-expected producer price data (PPI +0.4% MoM vs +0.1% expected) on Thursday, combined with hawkish Fed speak from multiple governors hinting that a December rate hike is back on the table. Markets now price in a 68% probability of a December 2026 hike — a dramatic reversal from last week's 22%.

The collateral damage: Gold crashed -4.6% on the week to $3,881/oz, its worst week since June. A stronger dollar (DXY +1.2% on the week) and higher real yields make the non-yielding metal costlier to hold. COMEX gold fell from $4,075 to $3,886. For portfolio context: this validates the case to reduce gold and shift to income-generating quality names. Bonds remain unattractive below 5.15% until clarity on December's Fed decision.

Rising rates hit rate-sensitive sectors: Real Estate (-1.08%) and Utilities (-1.24%) led declines. High-multiple growth tech is doubly squeezed — both by the discount-rate effect and by Nvidia's leadership disruption. The Buffett Indicator is at 228%, well above historical warning levels. Capital allocation discipline has never been more critical.

🛢️ Oil Reclaims $88.74 — OPEC Discipline & Middle East Tensions Sustain Premium

WTI crude gained +1.98% to $88.74/bbl, Brent to $95.82/bbl, as markets reassessed the Middle East risk premium. Despite earlier ceasefire optimism in the Iran conflict (from May's brief selloff), new signals this week suggest a stalled diplomatic track. OPEC+ reiterated supply discipline at its November meeting, keeping production curtailments in place through Q1 2027. The IEA raised its 2026 demand forecast by 220,000 bpd, citing stronger-than-expected Asian industrial activity.

Energy stocks were the only sector to close positive today (+1.14%). XOM at ~$170 continues to generate extraordinary free cash flow — breakeven around $65/bbl means every dollar above that is pure margin. The stock is up ~12% YTD. At $88–90 oil, integrated majors like XOM and CVX remain compelling holds for a moderate-risk, income-oriented portfolio.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"If There Is a Big Decline, We Will Deploy" — $373B Waiting

Buffett has a blunt message for 2026: the market at S&P 7,560 and Buffett Indicator at 228% (well above the 200% "playing with fire" threshold) does not qualify as a "big decline." He said in October that the recent dip "wasn't big enough to deploy" Berkshire's historic $373 billion cash pile. The strategic implication: Buffett is essentially holding a 5%+ yield on T-bills while waiting for a genuine dislocation — think S&P 6,000 or below. Meanwhile, the cash pile itself generates roughly $18B per year in interest income. His comparison of financial markets to "a church with a casino attached" captures the moment — speculative AI trades coexist with long-term compounding. BRK-B is both a hedge and a pure expression of Buffett's thesis: patience at current valuations.

Ray Dalio / Bridgewater Associates

"The AI Trade Is Flirting With Bubble Conditions" — Cash & Diversification Now

Ray Dalio is in the bearish camp on today's AI-driven market valuations. He and several other prominent billionaire investors have warned that the artificial intelligence trade has bid up stocks to levels that require near-perfect execution to justify. The Nvidia CEO resignation is exhibit A of the execution risk embedded in high-multiple AI names. Dalio's prescription: build true diversification — international equities (particularly in markets less correlated to US tech), commodities, and inflation-linked assets. Bridgewater has reportedly reduced US large-cap tech exposure and increased allocations to emerging market debt and commodity-linked assets. For moderate-risk portfolios, this supports the XOM (commodities), AGX (real infrastructure), and BRK-B (true diversifier) thesis.

Goldman Sachs Macro Desk — November 2026

"Forget Tech — Hide in Defensives" — Q4 Sector Rotation Underway

Goldman Sachs issued a stark sector rotation note this week: in an environment of 5%+ yields, decelerating tech earnings growth, and leadership risk in the AI complex (crystallized by Huang's departure), their overweights shift decisively to Healthcare, Energy, and select Financials. They specifically add Huntington Ingalls Industries (defense), Hyatt Hotels (travel recovery), and Chime (fintech). They maintain their S&P 500 year-end target at 7,800 — implying ~3% upside from current levels — but acknowledge that the composition of that return shifts dramatically: from AI-driven Nasdaq momentum to Dow-style defensive quality. Their single clearest message: at 5.15% on the 10Y, any stock yielding less than 2% with a P/E above 40x needs exceptional fundamental support to hold its valuation.

JPMorgan Equity Strategy — November 14, 2026

AGX and ASML Are the Two Highest-Conviction Picks for Q4 2026 and Beyond

JPMorgan's equity research team has flagged Argan (AGX) as "trading at very attractive levels" with a $785 price target representing the most compelling risk/reward in their entire coverage universe. The power infrastructure thesis is structural: US electricity demand is growing at the fastest pace since the 1950s, driven by AI data center buildout, EV adoption, and manufacturing reshoring. Meanwhile, ASML remains on JPMorgan's Active Focus List with a $2,350 target — the analyst argues that regardless of which company leads AI chip development, all roads lead back to ASML's EUV lithography monopoly. Taken together, these two picks represent the "infrastructure layer" of the AI trade — lower beta, more defensible moats, and less exposure to individual CEO risk than the hyperscalers.

Howard Marks / Oaktree Capital

"We Are Not in a Safe Market" — Marks Unconvinced by Bubble Warnings, But Urges Selectivity

Howard Marks' latest memo draws a nuanced line: while he disagrees with Dalio's binary "bubble" framing, he is explicit that "we are not in a safe market." His reading of Buffett's 1979 warnings — that uncertainty in markets is the permanent condition, not an aberration — leads him to advocate for selectivity over breadth. His practical advice: favor income-generating assets (dividend stocks, corporate bonds) over zero-yield growth names; keep 15–20% in cash or alternatives for optionality; and avoid the temptation to chase AI momentum names that have already priced in perfection. The Jensen Huang situation is exactly the kind of company-specific shock that Marks' framework accounts for — an idiosyncratic risk that cannot be modeled but can be hedged through diversification and valuation discipline. This brief's picks are built to reflect the Marks philosophy.
