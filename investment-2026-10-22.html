📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🖥️ Meta Beats Big, Tesla Disappoints on Capex — AI Trade's Rebound Faces Its First Real Test

Wednesday's regular session closed strongly for a third straight day (S&P +0.58% to 7,757.17, Nasdaq +1.05% to 26,702.86) on continued SpaceX enthusiasm, but the after-hours reaction to Meta and Tesla earnings told a more complicated story. Meta posted record revenue of $64.6B (+26% YoY) and EPS of $3.83 (vs. $3.57 expected), announced a stock split, and rose as much as 4.25% after hours to $723.93 — even after absorbing a $9.9B legal-settlement charge that sharply dented reported operating income.

Tesla's reaction was the opposite: EPS beat consensus ($0.48 vs. $0.45 est.) but revenue missed by about $200M, and the company cut its 2026 capital-expenditure forecast to roughly $20B from more than $25B. The stock initially popped 2% before reversing to close down about 3.3% after hours at $373.62, as investors weighed what the lower capex means for Tesla's AI and robotics ambitions. Futures whipsawed overnight — Nasdaq-100 contracts fell as much as 1.3% on doubts about the AI-trade rebound before paring losses as SpaceX, Super Micro and semiconductor strength reasserted itself into the open.

🇨🇳 China Stocks Nearing a Bear Market Adds to Global Risk-Off Crosscurrents

China's CSI 300 Index fell as much as 1.6% Thursday, bringing its total decline from its June high to around 20% — the threshold for a technical bear market — as waning hopes for meaningful stimulus, lackluster earnings and lingering AI-trade jitters weigh on sentiment. Combined with the mixed Meta/Tesla reaction and escalating Middle East tensions pushing oil higher, this is shaping up as a day where several separate worries are compounding rather than one dominant narrative — worth watching for contagion into U.S. risk appetite.

🏭 Sector Performance — Energy and Tech Still Leading, Healthcare Cooling Slightly

Energy remains the runaway YTD leader, extending gains as Brent pushes above $111 on Middle East risk. Technology continues to lead the "growth" cohort, rising another 1.68% on the sector level Wednesday as investors rotated back in post-earnings. Healthcare ticked down modestly (XLV -0.49% on the day) even as UnitedHealth — this portfolio's healthcare pick — held steady on its earnings beat, a reminder that sector-wide moves don't always track single-name conviction stories. Financials, utilities, communication services and consumer discretionary remain the YTD laggards.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Paul Tudor Jones / Tudor Investment Corp

Bearish 2027 Call Looks More Relevant After Tesla's Capex Cut

Jones's warning this month that "2027 is going to be really, really challenging" — driven by negative equity supply-demand flows as AI-sector share offerings pile up — gains a fresh data point with Tesla's decision to cut 2026 capex guidance by roughly $5B. If even AI/robotics bellwethers are pulling back on spending, Jones's thesis that buybacks and M&A can no longer offset new share supply looks harder to dismiss. Still a standing risk for 2027 planning rather than a reason to change today's tactical picks.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

Selective Conviction Persists Even as China Wobbles and AI Reactions Diverge

Buffett's playbook remains unchanged: Berkshire's ~$373B cash pile signals he's waiting for real distress, not just volatility. Days like today — a mixed Meta/Tesla reaction, China nearing a bear market — are exactly the kind of noise his approach is built to ignore. Berkshire's ~$37.4B Alphabet stake, now sitting on a deeper pullback after two down sessions, is arguably a better value today than it was Monday. The lesson for this portfolio: stay selective (UNH, AMZN, JNJ) rather than broadly aggressive or reactive to single-session swings.
