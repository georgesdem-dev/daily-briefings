📊 Market Snapshot LIVE DATA

🗳️ Midterm Election Day — Markets Cautious Into the Results

Americans vote today in the midterm elections, a binary event risk that's been looming over markets all week. Futures traded mixed into the open, and VIX jumped to 21.15 (+8.91%) overnight as investors priced in both the election outcome and the knock-on effect it could have on the timeline for any further US-Iran military decision, which the administration has reportedly deferred until after today's results.

Monday's session set the tone for the divergence now running through the market: the Dow rose 213.67 points (+0.41%) to 51,983.98 on broad-based strength outside tech, while the S&P 500 fell 38.67 points (-0.51%) to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq dropped 272.68 points (-1.07%) to 25,164.32. AI-related names fell roughly 3% as a group, while the rest of the S&P 500 (ex-AI) rose approximately 1% — a clean rotation signal, not a broad risk-off move.

🛢️ Oil's Slide Deepens — Three Straight Down Days as Iran-Qatar Talks Progress

WTI crude has now fallen for three consecutive sessions: from Friday's five-month high near $104.12, to roughly $100 on Monday, to approximately $96.46 today — a cumulative slide of 7-8%. Iranian President Pezeshkian reportedly secured Supreme Leader Khamenei's approval to pursue peace talks, and a third-plus consecutive day of Iran-Qatar negotiations in Doha is steadily deflating the Hormuz-attack risk premium that built up all last week. This is good news for the broad market and for the US consumer, but it's a direct headwind for the portfolio's energy-hedge position — see the OXY note below.

Apple's post-earnings reaction continues to weigh on tech sentiment: the stock closed Monday at $338.32, down roughly 1.8-4.6% depending on the measurement window (regular session vs. combined with after-hours), as a weak margin forecast tied to rising memory-chip costs overshadowed the firm's revenue and EPS beat. It remains the market's clearest read on whether the tech selloff that started with Intel's Oct 30 crash is stabilizing or still working through the system.

🧠 Trusted Investor Insights MACRO VIEWS
Ray Dalio / Bridgewater

The AI-vs-Non-AI Split Is the Bubble Story Playing Out in Real Time

Monday's clean divergence — AI-linked names down ~3% as a group while the rest of the S&P 500 rose ~1% — is close to a textbook illustration of Dalio's thesis: capital that chased AI-capex names is now rotating out, not because the broader economy is weakening, but because that specific trade got crowded and expensive. His standing call that AI capex looks like a bubble approaching its bursting point looks more, not less, relevant after a week that started with Intel's crash and continues with Apple's margin-driven selloff.

Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway

"Cold, Hard Reality Check" — Now Playing Out Sector by Sector

Buffett's warning about crowded, overvalued positioning is showing up exactly where he'd expect: Technology and Communication Services remain the two weakest groups over the trailing month, while Financials, Industrials, Materials and Consumer Discretionary — the less-crowded, more reasonably valued corners of the market — are leading. Diversification away from the most crowded AI names remains the better-tested approach into election results and Q4 earnings season.

Goldman Sachs & JPMorgan — Rotation Playbook

Energy's Reversal Shows the Rotation Is About Valuation, Not Oil Prices

Energy led the market just days ago on the Hormuz risk premium; today it's the weakest sector as that same premium unwinds. That's a useful reminder that a single-stock or single-sector hedge tied to one geopolitical scenario — like OXY's current setup — needs active management, not a "set and forget" allocation. GS and JPM's broader call for rotation into value/defensives away from crowded AI names still holds; the OXY-specific hedge is the piece that needs resizing.
